Division I considered light as a primordial principle of disclosure. Division II places that principle within the particular metaphysical structure of The Architecture of Hidden Forces. Here light is not an isolated substance, nor is it a universal explanation imposed upon every phenomenon. It is one indispensable activity within a larger architecture composed of source, force, ether, mediation, quality, form, reception, memory, and return.

The central proposition of this division:

> Light is the activity through which force becomes distinguishable, form becomes perceptible, and hidden relation becomes capable of meaning.

Light reveals, but it does not act alone. It requires a source from which it proceeds, a medium through which it becomes qualified, a vessel through which it is embodied, and a receiver through which its presence becomes experience.
