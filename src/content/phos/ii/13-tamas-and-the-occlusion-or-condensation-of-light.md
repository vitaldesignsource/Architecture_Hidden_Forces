Tamas is heaviness, inertia, obscuration, fixation, and resistance. It can block light, absorb motion, and preserve patterns after their living purpose has ended.

Yet tamas is also necessary for embodiment. Without stability and resistance, no current could become a durable form. Boundary, sleep, rest, mineral density, memory, and material persistence all participate in the tamasic function.

## Vessel, not prison

Tamas becomes pathological when condensation ceases to serve life. Form hardens against renewal, memory becomes imprisonment, and protection becomes occlusion. Light remains present as possibility but cannot pass through the accumulated density.

The task is not to destroy tamas but to restore its rightful function. Tamas must become vessel rather than prison, foundation rather than stagnation, and fertile darkness rather than unconscious obstruction.

Condensed light becomes form. Excessively condensed light becomes opacity.
