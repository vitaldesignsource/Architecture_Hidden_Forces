Aristotle rejects the view that vision requires fire or rays to travel outward from the eye. If the eye emitted sufficient light to see, he argues, it should be able to see in darkness. He instead places the transparent medium — *to diaphanes* — at the centre of vision.

The transparent is not limited to objects we ordinarily call transparent, such as clean water or glass. It is a capacity present in air, water, and certain bodies. The transparent can exist potentially in darkness or actually in light.

## Light as actuality

Light is the actuality of the transparent as transparent. It is not, for Aristotle, a body, a stream of particles, or a substance travelling from the luminous source to the eye. When fire, the sun, or another luminous body is present, the transparent medium becomes actualised. Darkness is [[vi-3|the privation]] of that actual condition.

This is an important conceptual transformation. Light becomes a state or activity of a [[iii-8|medium]] rather than an object moving through empty space. The medium is not a passive gap separating observer and object. It is an active condition required for visibility.

Colour possesses the power to move or alter the actually transparent medium. The medium then affects the sense organ, allowing the soul to receive the perceptible form of the object without receiving its matter. [The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's treatment of Aristotle's psychology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aristotle-psychology/) summarises the sequence as colour acting upon the transparent medium, which then acts upon the sense organ.

This account explains why colour cannot be seen without light. Colour exists at the boundary or surface of the visible body, but it requires the actualised transparent to exercise its perceptible power. Light does not manufacture colour; it allows colour to become effective within a medium.

Aristotle also argues that the transparent must intervene continuously between object and eye. Perception cannot occur through a void. If the distance were completely empty, the object might not be seen at all, even if it were immediately before the eye. Mediation is not an imperfection added to vision — it is one of vision's necessary conditions.

Within the Architecture of Hidden Forces, Aristotle's transparent is especially important. It offers a philosophical predecessor to [[ii-5|Morphaithēr]] as a field of mediation, although the two concepts should not be treated as identical. A power can be present potentially within a medium and become actual when an appropriate source enters relation with it — [[i-7|light as actualisation]]. Light is then not simply placed within the field; it is the field's awakened condition.
