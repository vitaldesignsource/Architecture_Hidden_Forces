Medieval *perspectiva* was much broader than the modern artistic technique of linear perspective. It was the science of light, vision, reflection, refraction, colour, mirrors, lenses, visual geometry, and the psychological interpretation of sensory appearances. Its development depended heavily upon the translation of Arabic optical works - above all [[xv-78|Ibn al-Haytham's Book of Optics]] - into Latin.

Thinkers such as Robert Grosseteste, Roger Bacon, Witelo, and John Pecham studied how light and visible information proceed through media toward the eye. They investigated the geometry of rays, the angles of reflection, the bending of light through transparent bodies, and the errors produced by distance, atmospheric conditions, mirrors, and the limitations of the visual organs.

Roger Bacon gave perspectiva an especially elevated position. Optics demonstrated that natural effects occur through determinate lines, angles, figures, and relations. Light became the ideal example of how a power could proceed from an agent, traverse a medium, act upon a recipient, and generate an effect without the source bodily relocating itself.

## Species

Medieval authors frequently described such transmission through *species*. A species was not necessarily a biological species or merely a mental image. It could mean a likeness, form, intention, or propagated influence emitted by an object and received by another. Visible species permitted the object to become perceptually present without transferring its entire substance into the eye.

The metaphysical potential of this theory is considerable. A thing can act at a distance by communicating a formal likeness. The recipient does not contain the original object, yet it enters into a real relation with it. Light supplies the physical paradigm for influence without material displacement.

## A science of error

Perspectiva also cultivated a science of error. An appearance may be genuinely produced by an external object while still being misjudged. Refraction can displace an image. A mirror can reverse it. Distance can alter its apparent magnitude. A turbid medium can transform colour. The senses therefore provide genuine contact with reality, but their reports require disciplined interpretation.

[Roger Bacon's *Perspectiva*](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/roger-bacon/) became part of a new optical curriculum in the universities of the Latin West, while Pecham's work served as a widely used teaching text.

Within the present system, medieval species provide a useful vocabulary for [[iii-30|luminous imprinting]]. A spiritual, psychic, planetary, or elemental force may communicate a formative signature without transferring the totality of its source. The receiving soul acquires a real likeness of the encountered power, but this likeness may be refracted by the receiver's tattvic composition, psychic history, expectations, and degree of transparency.

This establishes a law of mediated realism: an experience can be real and interpreted imperfectly at the same time. Error does not prove that nothing was encountered. It may reveal that a real transmission passed through an unsuitable lens.
