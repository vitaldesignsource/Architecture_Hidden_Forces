Empedocles of Acragas, active in the fifth century BCE, explained nature through four enduring roots — earth, water, air, and fire — whose combinations are governed by Love and Strife. His theory of vision belonged to this elemental cosmology. The eye was not an entirely passive opening through which the world simply entered. It contained elemental structures capable of meeting and recognising what proceeded from external objects.

Surviving fragments and later reports compare the eye to a lantern. As a lantern protects its flame behind a translucent screen while allowing its light to pass outward, the eye contains an interior fire surrounded by membranes and other elemental structures. [The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's discussion of Empedocles](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/empedocles/) notes both sides of the account: effluences travel from objects toward the perceiver, while a second activity proceeds from the organ itself.

## Like meets like

Empedocles therefore should not be reduced to the crude claim that sight occurs only because beams shoot from the eyes. His account appears to involve an encounter between outward-moving ocular fire and effluences proceeding from visible things. Vision occurs through a correspondence between the perceiving organ and what it receives.

The pores of the sensory organs admit only effluences proportioned to them. Different senses are capable of receiving different objects because their passages possess different structures. The eye does not hear sound because its organisation is not fitted to receive the relevant effluence. Perception depends upon compatibility.

Fire and water within or around the eye were also associated with the perception of light and dark. The precise reconstruction remains disputed because Empedocles' works survive only in fragments and later testimony. Nevertheless, the governing principle is clear: like becomes perceptible through a properly proportioned relation with like.

This places vision within the same architecture as the rest of Empedoclean nature. The eye is a microcosm composed of the same roots as the world it sees. It can perceive the cosmos because [[viii-21|the elements within the perceiver]] correspond to the elements beyond it.

Within the Architecture of Hidden Forces, Empedocles offers an early theory of active reception. The eye does not manufacture the visible world, but neither does it [[iii-17|receive]] without participation. An interior light goes forth, an exterior influence approaches, and sight arises where the two enter a relation of sufficient correspondence.
