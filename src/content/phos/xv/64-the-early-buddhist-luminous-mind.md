[A famous passage in the Aṅguttara Nikāya](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an01/an01.049.than.html) declares that the mind is luminous but defiled by incoming or adventitious defilements. It then states that the mind can be freed from these incoming defilements and that the instructed disciple understands the possibility of its development.

The Pāli word *pabhassara* can signify luminous, radiant, or brightly shining. Yet the passage is brief, and Buddhist traditions have disagreed about its implications. It should not be treated as an unambiguous statement that early Buddhism taught an eternal metaphysical Self hidden inside the personality.

Some interpreters understand luminosity as the mind's basic capacity to know. Others connect it with meditative concentration or with a mind temporarily freed from the five hindrances. Later traditions use it to support stronger doctrines of natural purity.

## What "incoming" means

The "incoming" character of the defilements remains crucial. Greed, aversion, delusion, agitation, and fixation do not define the complete possibility of mind. They arise dependently, condition perception, and can cease when their supporting conditions are removed.

This does not necessarily mean that an eternally pure substance sits unchanged beneath them. Early Buddhist thought generally analyses mind as a conditioned stream of events rather than an unchanging witness. Luminosity can therefore belong to clarity and knowing without becoming a permanent entity.

[A detailed comparative study by Bhikkhu Anālayo](https://buddhanature.tsadra.org/index.php/Articles/The_Luminous_Mind_in_Therav%C4%81da_and_Dharmaguptaka_Discourses) examines luminous-mind passages in Theravāda and Dharmaguptaka discourse traditions and cautions against reading later doctrinal conclusions uncritically into the earliest material.

The Buddhist emphasis is practical. If defilements were the immutable essence of mind, development would be impossible. Because they are conditioned and removable, attention, ethics, meditation, and insight can transform the field.

Luminosity is thus associated with workability. A clear mind can observe arising and passing, distinguish wholesome from unwholesome states, recognise craving without obeying it, and perceive experience without immediately constructing an enduring self around it.

## Obscuration without essential corruption

Within the Architecture of Hidden Forces, the early Buddhist teaching describes obscuration without essential corruption. The field becomes clouded, but clouding is not necessarily the final truth of the field.

This resembles the distinction between light and the psychic structures that interrupt it - [[vi-14|obscuration by form]]. A pathology can genuinely disturb consciousness, spiritual life, and the subtle organism without proving that the deepest possibility of the person has been destroyed.

The Buddhist caution must remain, however: luminosity should not be converted too quickly into a permanent soul merely because another tradition desires such a conclusion. Its value lies partly in showing that awareness can be luminous without being owned by an unchanging ego.
