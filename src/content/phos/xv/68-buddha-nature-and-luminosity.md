Buddha-nature teachings use several related terms, especially *tathāgatagarbha*, which can suggest the womb, embryo, or interior presence of a Tathāgata, and *buddhadhātu*, the Buddha-element or Buddha-domain.

These teachings affirm that awakening is possible for sentient beings because defilement does not constitute their final nature. The possibility of Buddhahood is not inserted into the person from an entirely foreign source. It is uncovered, matured, recognised, or actualised.

Tathāgatagarbha texts employ striking images: a Buddha hidden inside a decaying lotus, honey surrounded by bees, a kernel within its husk, gold buried in filth, treasure beneath a poor person's house, a great tree contained in a seed, or a golden image wrapped in rags.

The coverings are real as coverings. They obstruct access and produce suffering. Yet they do not transform the hidden treasure into their own nature. This provides the basis for the language of adventitious defilement: obscurations are present and effective without being inseparable from awakening.

## Interpretations differ

The Ratnagotravibhāga and related traditions join Buddha-nature with natural purity and luminosity. The mind's luminous nature is obscured by temporarily associated afflictions, just as the sun is covered by clouds without being destroyed.

Interpretations differ sharply. Some traditions use strongly affirmative language, treating Buddha-nature as an actual presence of awakened qualities. Others understand it as emptiness, the absence of fixed defilement, or the universal potential for transformation. Still others warn that positive language is medicinal and must not be converted into a permanent metaphysical soul.

That warning is particularly important when comparing Buddha-nature with the Upanishadic Ātman. Similar language of inward light does not erase the Buddhist rejection of an independent, unchanging self. Many Buddhist interpreters insist that Buddha-nature is empty of self-existence even while it is luminous and endowed with awakened qualities.

A translated Mahāyāna scripture expresses the teaching directly: "Mind is by nature luminosity", untouched in its nature by mental disturbance. So [84000's translation of The Teaching by the Child Inconceivable Radiance](https://84000.co/translation/toh103) renders it.

The union of emptiness and luminosity is central. Emptiness means that mind cannot be found as a permanent, isolated thing. Luminosity means that experience nevertheless appears, knows, and manifests. Emptiness prevents reification; luminosity prevents emptiness from being mistaken for blank nonexistence.

## Incorruptible capacity

Within the Architecture of Hidden Forces, Buddha-nature can be compared to an incorruptible capacity for right relation. It does not mean the empirical personality is already perfect or that illness, cruelty, and delusion are unreal. It means these formations do not possess final sovereignty.

The doctrine offers a sophisticated account of hidden light, close in structure to [[xv-49|Or ha-Ganuz]]. Light can be fully obscured at the level of lived experience without being annihilated at the level of possibility and nature. Liberation is neither manufacturing light from nothing nor declaring the obscuration irrelevant. It is removing, transforming, and seeing through the architecture that prevents luminosity from manifesting freely.
