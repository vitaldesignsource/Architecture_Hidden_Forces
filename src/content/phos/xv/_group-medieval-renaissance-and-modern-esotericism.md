From the thirteenth through the twentieth century, European theories of light developed along several intersecting paths. Natural philosophers investigated physical propagation and visual perception; theologians treated light as the first corporeal form or image of divine creation; Renaissance Platonists understood the sun as a mediator of intelligible power; alchemists sought the light concealed within nature; occultists described an astral light bearing images, forces, and memories; and later esoteric movements constructed elaborate systems of planes, rays, subtle bodies, and formative ethers.

This history does not describe a simple movement from superstition to science. Physical optics and metaphysical speculation frequently developed together. Even when they later separated, both continued to address different dimensions of illumination: what light physically does, how it appears to consciousness, what it means symbolically, and whether sensible light manifests a more primordial formative power.
