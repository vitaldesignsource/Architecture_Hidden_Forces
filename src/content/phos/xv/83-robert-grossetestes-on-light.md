Robert Grosseteste's short thirteenth-century treatise *De Luce*, or On Light, presents one of the boldest medieval cosmologies of illumination. Its central proposition is that light - *lux* - is the first corporeal form. Prime matter is not initially an extended physical body. It acquires three-dimensional extension because light, by its nature, multiplies itself equally in every direction and carries matter outward with it.

A dimensionless luminous point expands spherically. Through its virtually infinite self-multiplication, lux extends matter into the primordial cosmic body. The universe is therefore not merely illuminated after its formation. Light is implicated in the very production of dimensionality. It is the activity through which matter becomes spatially extended and physically manifest.

## Lux and lumen

Grosseteste then describes a second movement. After the outermost sphere has been established, it generates *lumen*, a derived radiance that proceeds inward toward the centre. As this returning luminosity descends, it gathers and differentiates matter into further celestial spheres. Cosmic order emerges through the interaction of an outward primordial expansion and an inward formative return.

The distinction between lux and lumen is philosophically important. Lux is light considered at its source, as the first formative presence inherent in matter. Lumen is the radiance proceeding from an already constituted luminous body. One is fontal and generative; the other transmitted and organisational. Grosseteste thus distinguishes source-light from the work performed by its [[iii-2|procession]].

The higher celestial bodies remain comparatively simple, rarefied, and luminous. The lower elemental world is more compounded, dense, and opaque. Yet all bodies retain some participation in the first light because light supplied the form through which matter initially became corporeal. The difference between heaven and earth is consequently a difference in luminosity, simplicity, and condensation rather than the absolute presence or absence of formative light. [Clare Riedl's translation](https://www.themathesontrust.org/library/on-light-de-luce) makes the treatise available in English.

## Field before form

Within the Architecture of Hidden Forces, Grosseteste offers an extraordinary precedent for [[ii-2|Field Before Form]]. Light does not travel through a preexisting neutral container; its propagation produces the field of extension in which bodies can appear. Space, matter, and form are consequences of luminous activity. The outward diffusion of lux resembles procession, while the returning lumen resembles the formative recollection by which dispersed possibility is organised into differentiated worlds.

Morphaithēr can similarly be understood as more than a passive medium. It is a living atmosphere in which radiance acquires spatial, qualitative, and morphogenic determination. Light reveals form because, at a deeper level, formative light participates in producing the conditions under which form exists.
