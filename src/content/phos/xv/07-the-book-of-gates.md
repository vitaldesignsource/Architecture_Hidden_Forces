The Book of Gates reorganises the nocturnal journey around a succession of guarded thresholds. It first appears at the end of the Eighteenth Dynasty and becomes especially important in Ramesside royal tombs. The night is divided into regions separated by gates, each possessing guardian beings whose identities and names must be known.

The gate is not merely an obstacle. It distinguishes one ontological condition from another. Passage requires recognition, appropriate identity, knowledge, authorisation, and the ability to withstand the quality of the region beyond it. The traveller cannot move through the entire Duat as an unchanged observer. Every threshold tests and transforms the being who crosses it.

## Revelation and judgement

The solar barque carries Re through these successive fields. Gods, the blessed dead, guardians, enemies, and forces of punishment occupy their appropriate places. The sun illuminates the structure of the Netherworld, separating beings according to their relationships with order and regeneration. Light here acts as revelation and judgement.

One of the best-known scenes associated with the Book of Gates depicts groups of humanity within the created order. Its importance lies in showing the solar cosmos as extending beyond Egypt alone, although the image remains expressed through Egyptian categories and royal theology. Re's journey concerns the maintenance of a world larger than a single tomb.

The guarded gates exemplify the Law of Right Relation. A threshold opens when the traveller, guardian, name, time, and region correspond. The power of a name is not a password in the modern mechanical sense. Knowledge of the name establishes recognition of the being's nature and the relationship required for passage.

In the system, the Book of Gates becomes an anatomy of luminous thresholds. Higher light cannot simply be seized by desire. Every passage demands a change in capacity. The gate protects both the sacred region from unprepared intrusion and the traveller from a force they are not yet able to receive.
