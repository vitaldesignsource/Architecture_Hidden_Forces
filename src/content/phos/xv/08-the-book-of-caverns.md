The Book of Caverns belongs to the later New Kingdom tradition of Netherworld cosmography. Important versions occur in the Osireion at Abydos and in Ramesside royal tombs, especially the tomb of Ramesses VI. Unlike the Amduat and Book of Gates, it is organised into six major divisions rather than a straightforward sequence of twelve hourly panels.

Its world is vertical, enclosed, subterranean, and intensely Osirian. The caverns contain divine bodies, forms of Osiris, the blessed dead, enemies of the solar order, guardian powers, and hidden sites of punishment and regeneration. Re passes above or enters into relation with these depths, addressing their inhabitants and illuminating the concealed structure of the underworld.

## Concentrated interiority

The cavern is an image of concentrated interiority. Where a gate emphasises passage between regions, a cavern emphasises what is contained within depth. It can function as tomb, womb, reservoir, prison, sanctuary, or chamber of transformation. What has disappeared from the surface remains present in another mode within the cavern.

The Book of Caverns also gives severe attention to beings excluded from luminous regeneration. Solar light does not render every distinction meaningless. It reveals whether a being participates in cosmic order or has become fixed in opposition to it. The same light that vivifies the justified exposes and judges the enemies of Re and Osiris.

Yet its deepest movement remains regenerative. Re approaches the concealed body of Osiris, the fragmented and mummified powers of the depths are gathered into a larger solar-Osirian process, and the sun is prepared for renewed emergence. Modern academic work continues to examine the surviving copies and their transmission, including the extensive version in Theban Tomb 33 — see [Daniel Werning's study in the *Bulletin de l'Institut français d'archéologie orientale*](https://journals.openedition.org/bifao/5059).

Within this encyclopaedia, the Book of Caverns belongs beside [[ii-27|the Crypt of Primordial Memory]] and [[ii-28|the Ossuary of Living Forms]]. The cavern preserves what has withdrawn from visible life. It is the chamber in which residual forces are judged, retained, recombined, or prevented from returning in a disordered form.
