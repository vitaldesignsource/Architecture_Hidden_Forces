The Amduat — "What Is in the Duat" — is also called the Book of the Hidden Chamber. Partial versions appear near the beginning of the New Kingdom, while the tomb of Thutmose III preserves the first complete surviving version. It describes the sun's nocturnal journey through twelve hours or regions of the Netherworld using an inseparable combination of text and image; [the Museo Egizio's study of its Amduat papyri](https://rivista.museoegizio.it/article/the-amduat-papyri-in-the-museo-egizio-tradition-and-innovation-between-the-twenty-first-and-twenty-second-dynasties/) traces the composition's later transmission.

The twelve hours should not be understood merely as equal units of clock time. They are stages within a transformational passage. The sun enters the western horizon, proceeds through increasingly hidden regions, distributes light and power to the inhabitants of the Duat, confronts obstruction, unites with the deepest sources of renewal, defeats the force that would arrest its motion, and emerges reborn.

## Graded participation

The inhabitants of the Duat awaken when Re reaches them. They receive speech, breath, nourishment, recognition, or temporary luminosity from his passage. When the solar barque moves onward, they return to their appointed condition. This is a remarkable conception of graded participation: the same solar presence affects different beings according to their nature and location.

The middle of the night contains the central mystery. Re in his ram-headed nocturnal form approaches the hidden body of Osiris. Solar *ba* and Osirian corpse enter union. Light descends into the extremity of death, while the power concealed within death becomes the womb of solar renewal. Dawn is possible because light has entered darkness deeply enough to recover the life hidden there.

During the final hours, the forces of regeneration gather around the sun. Apophis, the serpent of arrested movement and disorder, is restrained. In the twelfth hour, the solar company passes through the body of a great serpent and emerges transformed. The aged sun of the preceding evening becomes the newborn sun of dawn.

## A cosmography of transformation

The Amduat is therefore not simply a map of where the dead go. It is a cosmography of transformation. [A later Amduat papyrus at the Metropolitan Museum](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/547790) explicitly places the deceased in the sun's nocturnal journey and depicts the descent to Osiris.

Within the Architecture of Hidden Forces, the Amduat is among the clearest ancient precedents for the principle that light regenerates through descent. Darkness is not defeated from a safe distance. The solar power enters the hidden chamber, unites with what appears dead, and releases a new dawn from within it.
