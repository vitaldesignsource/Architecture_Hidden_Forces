The Mesopotamian relationship between light and justice arises from a concrete observation: wrongdoing depends upon limited vision. Human beings conceal actions, suppress testimony, manipulate appearances, and exploit those who lack powerful witnesses. The sun, however, enters courtyards, streets, fields, and distant roads. Its universal presence becomes the natural body of a divine witness who cannot easily be deceived.

Justice is therefore a luminous operation. It [[xiii-15|exposes]] what has been concealed, distinguishes truth from false testimony, brings the hidden act into public visibility, and restores a damaged relationship. Judgment resembles sunrise because both replace an uncertain field with one in which distinctions can be seen.

## The stele of Hammurabi

This relationship appears prominently on the stele of Hammurabi. At its summit, Hammurabi stands before the enthroned Shamash, from whose shoulders rise solar rays. The king receives insignia of authority — commonly interpreted as a rod and ring — from the solar god. The image does not simply say that Shamash dictated every legal ruling. It places the king's juridical authority beneath the divine source of justice and presents royal law as an earthly participation in solar discernment.

The text below the image presents Hammurabi as a king called to prevent the strong from oppressing the weak and to cause justice to appear in the land. Yet this should not be romanticised as modern equality. The rulings preserve social hierarchy and impose punishments according to status, gender, occupation, and circumstance. The luminous ideal of justice operated through the political and social architecture of its historical world.

Shamash was also connected with oaths, contracts, ordeals, divination, and the investigation of uncertain matters. A judge, diviner, or king sought not only information but a divinely warranted disclosure of what was hidden. Justice required that the invisible relation among action, responsibility, and consequence be made visible.

Within the system, this is light acting as exposure, [[xiii-16|judgment]], and restoration. Divine vision reveals the actual pattern created by an action. Justice then seeks to return the affected powers and persons to [[ii-25|right relation]]. Light is judicial because it prevents appearances from permanently concealing the architecture beneath them.
