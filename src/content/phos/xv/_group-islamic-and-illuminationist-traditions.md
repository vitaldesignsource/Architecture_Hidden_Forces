Islamic civilisation developed several powerful but distinguishable sciences of light. The Qur'an established light as a primary language of divine guidance and presence. Ibn al-Haytham investigated the physical propagation of light and the mechanisms of vision. Al-Ghazālī transformed the Qur'anic Light Verse into a spiritual psychology of progressively interior illumination. Suhrawardī went further, constructing an ontology in which immaterial reality consists of living orders of light. Islamic mystics explored illumination through prayer, recollection, unveiling, visionary colour, divine self-disclosure, and the purification of the heart.

These traditions cannot be reduced either to physical optics or to poetic symbolism. They encompass optical light, intellectual light, prophetic light, imaginal light, angelic light, and the uncreated source from which every derivative illumination receives its capacity to reveal.
