The Bundahišn, meaning "Primal Creation", is a major Middle Persian compilation of Zoroastrian cosmogony and cosmography. Its surviving forms were compiled in the Islamic period, principally around the ninth century CE, but they preserve, interpret, and systematise much older Avestan traditions. It should therefore not be treated as a text written by Zarathustra or as a direct record of the earliest Iranian religion; [Encyclopaedia Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bundahisn-primal-creation/) surveys its recensions and transmission.

The Bundahišn presents an immense architecture of light. Ohrmazd exists above in Endless Light, while Ahriman exists below in Endless Darkness. A void initially separates them. Ahriman perceives the light, attacks the good creation, and introduces mixture — *gumēzišn* — into a world originally formed in ordered distinction.

This [[vi-13|mixture]] does not mean that material creation is intrinsically evil. The material world becomes the field in which destructive power can be contained, confronted, exhausted, and ultimately overcome. Embodiment is not simply a fall away from light. It is part of the strategy through which the good creation enters conflict and moves toward final restoration.

## The brilliant enclosure

The heavens are described as a brilliant enclosure, variously compared in Middle Persian literature to stone, crystal, glass, shining metal, or an eggshell surrounding creation. These images combine firmness with luminosity. The sky is a protective body capable of admitting ordered light while resisting the invasion of destructive force.

Within this enclosure are the stations of the fixed stars, moon, sun, and Endless Light. The constellations and fixed stars are arranged as a defensive celestial army. Zodiacal constellations, numerous smaller stars, and the four directional guardians maintain the boundaries of the ordered heavens. Tishtrya guards the east; other stellar chiefs govern the west, south, and north.

The moon and sun occupy increasingly elevated regions above the stellar sphere. Beyond them are the highest spiritual stations and the Endless Light of Ohrmazd. [Encyclopaedia Iranica's account of Zoroastrian cosmology](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cosmogony-i/) describes this sequence as the spheres of stars, moon, sun, and boundless light, with divine luminosity descending through the ordered levels of the cosmos.

## Fixed stars and wandering planets

The Bundahišn also distinguishes beneficent fixed stars from the destructive planetary powers of its later cosmological system. This does not mean that all Iranian traditions or all historical Persian astrologers regarded planets in the same manner. It reflects a particular Zoroastrian interpretation in which the regular fixed stellar order defends creation while the wandering planets can represent disruptive counter-movements.

This is especially important for the history of astrology. Celestial influence was not merely symbolic decoration. The positions and motions of heavenly bodies were understood as expressions of real powers operating within cosmic history. Observation, calendrical calculation, myth, ritual, and moral cosmology were woven together. Symbolic perception and empirical observation belonged to one participatory study of the heavens.

The Bundahišn also describes several forms of fire distributed throughout creation: fire in the highest realm, fire within human beings and animals, fire in plants, fire in clouds, and ordinary terrestrial fire. Luminous power therefore descends from the celestial orders and becomes internal to living and material forms.

The history of the cosmos culminates not in the permanent victory of darkness but in *frašgird*, the final renovation. Mixture is resolved, destructive power is rendered incapable of further corruption, the dead are restored, and creation reaches a perfected condition. The purpose of cosmic conflict is not endless dualism but the final removal of distortion from existence.

Within the Architecture of Hidden Forces, the Bundahišn supplies one of history's most explicit models of a vertically ordered luminous cosmos. Light proceeds from an inexhaustible source, passes through graded celestial spheres, establishes stellar and [[vii-16|planetary]] relations, enters fire and living bodies, confronts distortion within matter, and moves the whole creation toward [[xiii-20|transfiguration]].
