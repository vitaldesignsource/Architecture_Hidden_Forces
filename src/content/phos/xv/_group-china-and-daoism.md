Chinese traditions approach light through several distinct but interrelated languages. The Mohists investigated its physical behaviour through observation and geometry. The Dao De Jing and Zhuangzi used brightness, obscurity, reflection, and heavenly illumination to describe perception and alignment with the Dao. Early cultivation texts treated clarity as a condition produced by refining the heart-mind and vital breath. Alchemical traditions subsequently placed the sun, moon, fire, water, and numinous radiance within an operative cosmology of transformation.

These traditions should not be collapsed into a single doctrine. Mohist optics, early Daoist philosophy, correlative cosmology, medieval alchemy, and Qing-period inner cultivation belong to different historical settings. Together, however, they provide an unusually complete spectrum: light as measurable activity, cosmic polarity, interior clarity, subtle vitality, alchemical power, and realised spiritual presence.
