The Transfiguration appears in the Synoptic Gospels as a concentrated revelation of Christic glory. Jesus takes Peter, James, and John upon a mountain. His appearance changes before them; his face shines like the sun in Matthew, while his garments become intensely or dazzlingly white.

The light does not descend upon Jesus as though illuminating an otherwise unrelated object. It radiates through his face and clothing. The body becomes transparent to a glory ordinarily concealed within the conditions of historical existence.

Moses and Elijah appear with him. They evoke the Law and Prophets, Sinai and Horeb, Exodus and eschatological expectation. Both are figures of the mountain, theophany, divine speech, and departure from ordinary human conditions.

## Glory and the coming exodus

In Luke, they speak of Jesus's coming "exodus" at Jerusalem. The Transfiguration therefore cannot be separated from suffering, death, and resurrection. Glory does not prove that vulnerability has been avoided. It reveals the deeper identity and direction of the path through vulnerability.

A bright or overshadowing cloud enters the scene, and the divine voice identifies the beloved Son and commands the disciples to listen. Once again, cloud and light are united. The highest revelation contains concealment because the divine presence exceeds what the witnesses can steadily receive.

The disciples fall, become afraid, or struggle to remain awake according to the different accounts. Their bodily and psychic responses demonstrate the limited capacity of the receiver. Genuine higher illumination can [[v-29|overwhelm]] without being deceptive.

Later Eastern Christian tradition would interpret the Transfiguration as a revelation of divine or uncreated light and a foretaste of *theōsis*, humanity's participation in divine life. The event was not understood only as a temporary external miracle but as the unveiling of Christ's glory and the destiny of human nature united with God.

Yet the brightness alone is not the full revelation. The voice, identity, scriptural witnesses, approaching passion, and consequences must be held together. The Transfiguration is luminous because the complete architecture is truthful, not merely because the spectacle is intense.

## Transfiguration, not replacement

Within the present system, the event demonstrates [[xi-13|transfiguration]] rather than replacement. The human form does not disappear. It becomes capable of manifesting a higher order of its own truth.

The body is therefore neither discarded nor treated as the source of divinity. Spirit inhabits and actualises form so completely that the form becomes radiant. Matter is [[ii-26|spiritualised]] without ceasing to be embodied.
