The *Timaeus* offers a physical and cosmological account of vision different from the metaphysical images of the *Republic*. Plato describes the eye as containing a pure, gentle fire related to the light of day. This interior fire passes outward through the eyes and joins with external daylight, forming a continuous visual body between observer and object.

When this visual current encounters a compatible external body, motions produced at the point of contact are transmitted through the visual stream to the soul. Vision is therefore neither purely external reception nor purely internal projection. It is an active continuity formed among inner fire, outer light, visible object, body, and soul. [The surviving text](https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/timaeus.html) describes the stream of vision as joining with daylight to form a kindred substance along the path between the eye and the object.

This explains why vision ordinarily fails at night. The inner fire remains, but it cannot easily unite with an adequate external light. Without the surrounding daylight, the visual body cannot be formed in the same stable way. When the eyelids close, the interior fire is contained, contributing to [[ix-4|sleep]] and the internal movements associated with dreams.

## The meeting of fires

For Plato, visible bodies emit flames or streams of differently proportioned particles. When these encounter the visual current, their relative size and motion produce different colours. Particles equal to those of the visual stream may pass without producing a strongly distinguishable sensation, while larger or smaller particles contract or dilate the stream and generate perceptions associated with dark and light.

White and black serve as major poles within this account, while bright, red, gleaming, blue, green, purple, and other colours arise through specific combinations and effects. Plato recognises that precise colour mixture is extraordinarily difficult because it involves proportions that human calculation may not fully determine.

[[iv-15|Colour]] is thus an event produced when one kind of fire encounters another through a receptive visual medium. It does not exist solely inside the observer, yet neither is it simply a surface property detached from perception. The visible world appears through a meeting of motions.

## The higher purpose of sight

The *Timaeus* also gives vision a higher purpose. The eyes allow humanity to observe the movements of the sun, moon, planets, and stars. From celestial observation arise number, time, philosophical inquiry, and the desire to bring the disordered revolutions of human thought into harmony with the ordered revolutions of the cosmos.

Vision therefore begins as elemental contact but culminates in cosmic reorientation. The eye is a channel through which celestial order educates the soul. In the system, this provides a direct precedent for understanding astronomical observation and symbolic perception as mutually supportive rather than mutually exclusive: [[vii-6|the inner fire and the outer light]] meet in one act of seeing.
