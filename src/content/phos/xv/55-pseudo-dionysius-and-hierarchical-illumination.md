The writer known as Pseudo-Dionysius composed his works around the late fifth or early sixth century under the identity of Dionysius the Areopagite, Paul's Athenian convert. His synthesis of Christianity, scripture, liturgy, angelology, and late Neoplatonic philosophy became enormously influential in Eastern and Western Christianity.

For Dionysius, God is the superessential source of every good and every illumination. Divine light proceeds generously toward creation without division or depletion. It remains simple in its source while appearing in different forms according to the capacities of its recipients.

A being does not receive less because the divine source is unwilling to give. Reception differs because vessels possess different capacities, functions, degrees of purification, and modes of existence. One ray may therefore produce multiple forms of participation without ceasing to be one - [[v-27|the same light received at different levels]].

## Hierarchy as transmission

Hierarchy is the sacred order through which beings are purified, illuminated, perfected, and drawn toward likeness with God. It is not properly a system of domination in which higher beings exploit lower ones. A higher order proves its elevation by transmitting the good it has received.

Dionysius organises the celestial hierarchy into three triads: Seraphim, Cherubim, and Thrones; Dominions, Powers, and Authorities; Principalities, Archangels, and Angels. Their names disclose modes of divine participation rather than biological species in the modern sense.

The highest orders receive illumination in a more unified way. They communicate it to the orders below in forms those orders can receive. The lower orders are not cut off from God; they encounter divine light through a graded economy of mediation.

Purification removes obstruction and disordered attachment. Illumination communicates knowledge and presence. Perfection completes the recipient's movement toward its proper divine likeness. These three operations recur through angelic ministry, scripture, sacrament, ecclesiastical life, and contemplation.

## Dissimilar symbols

Symbols and material rites are necessary because embodied beings cannot ordinarily receive purely intelligible illumination without mediation. Scripture clothes divine realities in names, images, stories, bodies, animals, fire, cloud, wheels, temples, and ritual forms. These coverings conceal while simultaneously revealing.

Dionysius sometimes prefers "dissimilar symbols" - images that are strange, lowly, or visibly inadequate - because beautiful images can tempt the observer to identify the symbol too completely with its divine source. A strange symbol openly announces its own insufficiency and drives contemplation beyond its surface.

His hierarchy therefore operates like a luminous prism. The one divine ray enters differentiated orders and becomes receivable through many colours. The differentiation belongs to the receivers and their offices, not to fragmentation within God.

[The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pseudo-dionysius-areopagite/) emphasises that Dionysian ascent does not require creatures to abandon their identities or simply move spatially upward. Scripture, ritual, ethical action, and hierarchical mediation return each being to a deeper fulfilment of its divinely intended identity.

Dionysius also leads the soul beyond hierarchy into apophatic darkness. God is praised through every divine name - Good, Being, Life, Wisdom, Light - and then recognised as exceeding every name. At the summit of contemplation, affirmation and negation are both surpassed in the "dazzling darkness" of the unknowable God.

Within the Architecture of Hidden Forces, Dionysius provides a mature doctrine of the Fourfold Veil. Every veil both protects and transmits. It adapts the higher current to the receiver while preserving a path of return through the same symbolic structure.

His teaching also clarifies the law of right relation. Higher beings are not those that accumulate the most power. They are those that most perfectly receive without distortion and transmit without captivity. True hierarchy is measured by transparent mediation.
