Paracelsus distinguished the *lumen naturae*, the Light of Nature, from the light of divine revelation. The Light of Nature is not ordinary daylight. It is the interior intelligibility placed within creation: the power by which nature reveals its hidden operations, signatures, sympathies, remedies, poisons, transformations, and correspondences.

Nature is not mute matter awaiting arbitrary human explanation. It is a living book whose forms contain signs. The physician must learn to read the shapes, colours, growth patterns, habitats, chemical behaviours, celestial correspondences, and inner virtues of things. A plant or mineral communicates something of its power through its signature, although the signature cannot always be read through superficial resemblance.

## The knower and the known

The lumen naturae also operates within the human knower. Because the human being is a microcosm containing correspondences with the greater world, knowledge of nature can awaken from within. Observation and experiment remain necessary, but they are joined by an interior recognition of kinship between knower and known.

Paracelsus thereby rejects a sharp division between objective nature and subjective imagination. [[x-13|Imagination]] can be a genuine formative faculty. Properly disciplined, it perceives and directs subtle relationships. Disordered imagination, however, can deceive, intensify illness, or generate destructive patterns. The same faculty that permits magical or medical insight can become an instrument of projection when separated from reason, experience, and ethical orientation.

His medicine also recognises more than one source of disease. Illness may arise from bodily constitution, environmental toxins, astral influences, psychic disturbances, spiritual causes, or divine providence. This does not mean every illness is punishment or evidence of spiritual failure. It means that physical pathology is not the only possible level of causation.

## Two lights, not one

The distinction between the Light of Nature and the Light of Grace prevents nature from becoming the absolute source of spiritual truth. Natural illumination discloses creation; divine illumination concerns the relation between the creature and God. The two can cooperate, but one should not be mistaken for the other. [Modern scholarship](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/00033790.2024.2334418) accordingly describes Paracelsus's intellectual world through the interaction of the Light of Nature and the Light of the Holy Spirit.

Within this encyclopaedia, the lumen naturae is the intelligible radiance concealed within formed existence. It is what permits mineral, plant, organism, planet, and landscape to disclose their intrinsic patterns. It is closely related to the visibility of force: the hidden power becomes legible through its characteristic form, behaviour, rhythm, and signature.

The physician or alchemist does not impose order from outside. The practitioner collaborates with the formative intelligence already working within the substance. Healing becomes a restoration of [[ix-20|right relation]] among physical, etheric, astral, psychic, environmental, and spiritual causes.
