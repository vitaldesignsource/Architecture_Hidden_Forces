The *Pistis Sophia* is a complex Coptic work compiled from multiple textual layers, probably during the third and fourth centuries. Its cosmology should not be assumed to duplicate that of the *Apocryphon of John*. Its Sophia inhabits a different architecture, and the text's many aeons, rulers, mysteries, light-streams, and treasuries belong to its own religious environment.

Pistis Sophia dwells in the thirteenth aeon. She looks upward and longs for the higher Light, but she is deceived by a false or imitative luminosity produced below by Authades, the Self-Willed power. Mistaking the lower light for the Light of Lights, she descends toward it. Her error is not the desire for transcendence itself; it is a failure to discern the origin and direction of the radiance that attracts her.

## The theft

Once she enters Chaos, the archontic powers surround her and strip away her light-power. A lion-faced power generated by the Self-Willed becomes a principal agent of her oppression. Her luminous strength is divided, appropriated, and used by hostile forces. She remains a being of light, but her power is diminished and her capacity to return is obstructed.

The theft of light-power is more than the loss of brightness. Light-power is agency. It is the capacity to ascend, perceive, act, maintain identity, and resist assimilation. To steal another being's light is to appropriate its operative vitality while leaving it unable to express the fullness of its own nature.

Pistis Sophia responds through a long series of repentances and invocations. Repentance here is not merely guilt or self-condemnation. It is a repeated act of reorientation. She recognises the false direction of her movement, calls upon the true Light, names her condition, and gradually re-establishes conscious relation with the source above her.

Her restoration occurs through divine assistance. Jesus sends a light-stream, and luminous powers including Michael and Gabriel assist in returning her scattered powers. The light-stream does not simply reassure Sophia; it enters the contested field, restrains the hostile powers, and restores what has been taken. One episode in the second book explicitly describes the light-stream returning Sophia's light-powers to her; [Mead's translation](https://sacred-texts.com/chr/ps/ps001.htm) gives the whole sequence.

## Five kinds of shining

This narrative makes an essential distinction between true light, reflected light, borrowed light, stolen light, and [[v-21|counterfeit light]]. Something may shine because it is united with its own source. It may shine because it faithfully reflects a higher power. It may also shine because it has captured, imitated, or temporarily accumulated the power of another.

Brilliance alone therefore cannot establish divine identity. The direction of the light, the source from which it proceeds, the relations it establishes, and the consequences it produces must all be examined. True light restores agency and right relation. Counterfeit light fascinates in order to draw the perceiver into dependency, fragmentation, or extraction.

This principle applies both to visionary encounters and ordinary life. Ideologies, charismatic personalities, institutions, psychic formations, and spiritual entities can acquire luminosity from the attention and life-power invested in them. Their apparent greatness may be partly constituted by captured participation. The restoration of the individual then requires both discernment and the recollection of dispersed power.

Within the present system, Pistis Sophia exemplifies a fracture in the relationship among spirit, soul, astral image, and directed desire. Her aspiration is real, but desire becomes attached to a deceptive image — [[vi-18|astral glamour]] in its classic form. The astral body moves toward the image; the psychic architecture becomes exposed; and hostile or disordered forces seize upon the opened current.

Her restoration is therefore a luminous *solve et coagula*. The false configuration is dissolved, stolen power is released, and the scattered luminous faculties are reassembled around their rightful source — [[xiii-27|captive light liberated]]. The story does not warn against seeking higher light. It teaches that authentic ascent requires the discernment of luminous provenance.
