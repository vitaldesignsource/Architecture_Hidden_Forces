The Psalms employ light with extraordinary variety. The Lord is called light and salvation; divine favour shines like a face; revelation becomes a lamp; justice shines forth; lightning announces divine movement; darkness becomes transparent before God; and creation is sustained beneath a deity clothed in light.

Psalm 104 presents one of the most majestic images: God is clothed with splendour and majesty and [wrapped in light as with a garment](https://www.sefaria.org/Psalms.104.2). The heavens are stretched out like a tent, the clouds become a chariot, and the winds and flames become messengers. Light is royal vesture - the outward radiance of sovereign life.

A garment both reveals and conceals. It makes the wearer visibly present while preventing the observer from claiming direct access to the wearer's essence. When God is clothed in light, luminosity becomes a merciful mediation. The glory is genuinely manifested, yet the divine reality remains greater than its radiance.

## Seeing light by light

Psalm 36 declares that with God is the fountain of life and that "in your light we see light". Divine light is not merely one object among others. It is the condition through which lesser lights become intelligible. The perceiver does not simply look at God's illumination; the perceiver sees by participation in it.

Psalm 27 brings the image into personal existence: "The Lord is my light and my salvation." Here light becomes courage, orientation, deliverance, and inward stability. The divine light does not guarantee that no enemies or dangers will appear. It prevents those dangers from becoming the ultimate definition of reality.

Psalm 119 calls the divine word a lamp for the feet and a light for the path. The emphasis is deliberately measured. A lamp may not illuminate the entire future at once. It discloses the next steps. Luminous guidance is often sufficient without being total, teaching the traveller to remain attentive rather than demanding complete control.

## Kinds of darkness

Other Psalms complicate any simple opposition between divine light and darkness. Psalm 18 presents God as making darkness a hiding place while fire and lightning proceed from the concealed presence. Psalm 139 declares that darkness is not dark to God; night shines like day. Darkness can obstruct human perception without restricting divine awareness.

The Psalms therefore distinguish several forms of darkness. There is the darkness of danger and wickedness, the darkness of ignorance, the [[vi-8|concealing darkness]] surrounding divine mystery, and the ordinary night within which God remains present. Darkness cannot be assigned one meaning in every context.

Within the present system, the Psalms describe divine radiance as a spectrum of relations: garment, face, fountain, lamp, path, lightning, chariot, and field of perception. Light may surround, descend, guide, disclose, vivify, protect, or remain hidden inside darkness.

Their deepest contribution is relational. Light is not presented only as a cosmic substance. It is the experienced quality of divine nearness. The Psalmist does not merely ask, "What is light?" but "Whose light is this, what does it reveal, and how must I live within it?"
