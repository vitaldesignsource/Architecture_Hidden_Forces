Yin and yang are not two substances locked in moral warfare. They are complementary tendencies within transformation. Yin is associated with shade, concealment, inwardness, receptivity, coolness, condensation, rest, and the moon. Yang is associated with brightness, exposure, outward movement, warmth, expansion, activity, and the sun. Yet neither is absolutely pure or self-sufficient. Each reaches a limit at which it begins to generate or disclose the other.

Light must therefore be understood rhythmically. Solar brightness is not permanently victorious over lunar darkness, nor is night an evil interruption of the day. Day becomes night; activity requires restoration; manifestation proceeds from [[vi-5|latency]] and returns to it. [[vii-12|Lunar light]] is received and reflected, but reception is not passive in the sense of impotence. The moon measures, modulates, gathers, and redistributes celestial influence through phases.

## Correlative order

Chinese correlative systems extended these relationships across seasons, directions, organs, emotions, substances, colours, planets, and stages of development. This was not simply decorative symbolism. It expressed a worldview in which Heaven, Earth, the body, and society participate in resonant patterns. [Chinese scientific and medical thinkers](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/chinese-phil-science/) used yin-yang correspondences in models of the body and in accounts of solar, lunar, stellar, and seasonal cycles.

Planetary and lunar influences should consequently not be limited to a crude choice between mechanical causation and imaginary superstition. The gravitational relation between Earth, moon, and tides is physical; solar radiation plainly sustains terrestrial life; calendrical and astrological systems organise human activity and symbolic expectation; and subtler forms of resonance remain subjects of spiritual investigation. These modes of influence can coexist without being identical.

## The primordial alternation

Within the Architecture of Hidden Forces, yin and yang describe the primordial alternation through which light becomes operative. Yang is emissive radiance; yin is receptive depth. Yang reveals; yin retains. Yang differentiates; yin gestates. Their conjunction generates a living field. The sun and moon are therefore not merely external astronomical objects but cosmic governors, symbolic powers, and physical participants in terrestrial life. Their internal counterparts are the projective and receptive poles through which spirit, soul, and body exchange force.
