Modern Theosophy presented the universe as a living hierarchy of visible and invisible planes. Helena Petrovna Blavatsky's writings drew upon Western esotericism, Neoplatonism, Kabbalah, Spiritualism, Hindu and Buddhist terminology, contemporary science, and claimed occult instruction. Later Theosophists - including Annie Besant and C. W. Leadbeater - systematised these teachings more rigidly, while Alice Bailey developed the doctrine of the Seven Rays in another direction.

## Planes as states

Theosophical terminology changed among authors, but a common arrangement distinguishes physical, astral, mental, buddhic, atmic, monadic, and divine planes. These should not be imagined as separate floors stacked inside ordinary physical space. A plane is a state of substance, consciousness, vibration, law, and perceptual capacity. Several planes can interpenetrate while remaining inaccessible to senses adapted only to physical matter.

The physical plane itself includes denser and etheric subdivisions in many later Theosophical systems. The [[ix-18|etheric body]] serves as the immediate formative and vital interface between the dense organism and subtler vehicles. The [[x-18|astral body]] bears desire, affect, sensation, and mobile imagery. The mental vehicle bears thought. Higher vehicles mediate intuition, spiritual will, unity, and monadic identity.

This supplies a genuine ontology of subtle bodies rather than treating them as poetic metaphors. The physical body is one vehicle among several. Sleep, dream, trance, projection, initiation, death, and postmortem experience involve changes in the centre of conscious activity and its relation to these vehicles.

## The rays

The doctrine of rays describes differentiated streams of universal life. Blavatsky introduced important septenary structures, but the highly developed personality typology of Seven Rays belongs especially to later Theosophical and Bailey traditions. The rays function as primordial qualities of divine activity expressed through cosmic beings, planets, civilisations, religions, souls, psychological tendencies, and historical periods.

They are not simply seven colours. Each ray represents an extensive complex of will, love, intelligence, harmony, knowledge, devotion, or ceremonial organisation. Their planetary and human expressions are mixtures rather than perfectly isolated channels. An individual may have one dominant monadic or soul ray, another personality ray, and different ray conditions affecting the mental, emotional, and physical vehicles.

Theosophy also portrays planets as embodiments or vehicles of greater intelligences. Their physical globes are the densest visible portions of multidimensional planetary beings. [[vii-16|Planetary influence]] is therefore not exhausted by gravitational or electromagnetic interaction. It can be transmitted through corresponding planes of consciousness and substance. [Theosophical sources](https://www.theosophy.world/encyclopedia/planets-sacred) explicitly describe the seven sacred planets as the vehicles or centres of differentiated solar forces.

## Web rather than ladder alone

Within the Architecture of Hidden Forces, the Theosophical planes provide a useful vertical taxonomy, but they should not become rigid boxes. [[v-2|The luminous web]] may describe reality better than a ladder alone. Etheric, astral, mental, planetary, archetypal, and spiritual orders interpenetrate and influence one another through correspondences.

The rays closely resemble differentiated morphogenic currents, whereas the [[ii-8|tattvas]] describe biases within the media through which those currents become manifest. A ray is a directing quality or intention; a tattva is a formative coloration of the field; a guṇa describes the condition of clarity, motion, or inertia under which transmission occurs. Keeping these functions distinct makes the architecture more precise.
