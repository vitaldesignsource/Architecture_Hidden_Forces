The Neiye, or Inward Training, is preserved as a chapter of the Guanzi and is generally situated in the late Warring States period. It is among the earliest Chinese texts devoted systematically to interior cultivation. Rather than presenting salvation as adherence to a creed, it describes the refinement of the human being through posture, regulated breathing, tranquillity, careful awareness, and the stabilisation of vital essence. [Harold Roth's influential study](https://cup.columbia.edu/book/original-tao/9780231115650/) dates it approximately to the middle of the fourth century BCE and interprets it as an early expression of contemplative practice associated with the emerging Daoist tradition.

## Jing, qi, and shen

The text's central language concerns *jing*, *qi*, *shen*, and the heart-mind. Qi is not merely atmospheric air, while jing does not mean only physical reproductive substance. These terms designate different concentrations and functions of living potency. When the heart-mind becomes orderly and tranquil, vital essence can stabilise within it. When agitation, excessive desire, fear, or anger disrupt the inner field, this potency disperses.

Inner radiance can therefore be understood as the experiential quality of gathered vitality and clarified spirit. It is not created through fantasy or forced visualisation. It emerges when the organism becomes sufficiently coherent to retain and conduct its own vital-spiritual power. Clarity, vitality, presence, and ethical balance are not separate achievements here; they are interdependent expressions of right inner ordering - close to what this encyclopaedia calls [[ix-20|health as luminous coherence]].

This offers an important foundation for taking the subtle body seriously. The Neiye does not give the later technical anatomy of an "[[ix-18|etheric body]]", but it does treat life as organised by powers that exceed a purely mechanical description of flesh. Breath, essence, spirit, emotion, posture, attention, and cosmic order participate in one living continuum. The physical organism is the densest visible expression of this continuum, not an isolated machine occasionally visited by an immaterial mind.

## An early discipline of etheric coherence

Within this encyclopaedia, the Neiye can be read as an early discipline of etheric coherence. It does not prove that every later subtle-body map is exact, but it supports the reality of a vital-formative dimension cultivated through breath, stillness, alignment, and the containment of dispersive forces. The heart-mind becomes a vessel; regulated breath becomes rhythmic mediation; gathered essence becomes a reservoir; and inner radiance becomes the sign that life-power and consciousness are entering ordered relation.
