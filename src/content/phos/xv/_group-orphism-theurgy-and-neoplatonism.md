The traditions gathered here do not constitute a single uninterrupted school. Orphic theogonies, the Chaldean Oracles, Plotinian philosophy, Porphyrian asceticism, Iamblichean theurgy, and Proclean metaphysics belong to different periods and sometimes disagree about how the soul reaches the divine. Yet they share a profound conviction: light is not merely something seen by the physical eye. It signifies — and may directly manifest — the procession of reality from hidden unity into intelligible, psychic, and material form.

Within these traditions, illumination becomes an ontological event. A being is illuminated when it participates more fully in its source; a soul is illuminated when it awakens to the divine order already sustaining it; and the world is luminous because higher realities communicate themselves through forms, symbols, lives, substances, and celestial bodies. Light descends without exhausting its origin, while the illuminated being returns by becoming conscious of the source from which its power proceeds.

Historically, these ideas must be distinguished from one another. The following entries therefore preserve the character of each tradition before integrating it into the larger luminous architecture of this encyclopaedia.
