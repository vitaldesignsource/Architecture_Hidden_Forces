The work usually called the Litany of Re was known more precisely as the Book of Adoring Re in the West. Rather than narrating the solar journey hour by hour, it invokes and praises Re through seventy-five forms or manifestations. These include anthropomorphic, mummiform, animal, composite, and more abstract solar appearances.

The forms do not imply that Re has been broken into seventy-five unrelated gods. They disclose the sun god's capacity to become present through many powers, phases, functions, and regions. Re may be hidden, radiant, generative, punitive, deceased, renewed, or united with other divine realities. The litany is therefore a theology of differentiated unity: the single solar power contains a multiplicity of operative forms.

## Re and Osiris

The litany also brings Re into profound relation with Osiris. The nocturnal sun does not remain identical to the visible daytime disc. It becomes a hidden *ba* descending toward the corpse-like depth represented by Osiris. Their union is a mystery through which solar motion and Osirian endurance participate in one another. Re gives movement and illumination to the depths; Osiris gives the nocturnal sun a body in which regeneration can occur.

In the tomb of Thutmose III, the Litany accompanies the Amduat and addresses the sun in its seventy-five forms, especially in relation to the crucial union of Re and Osiris. The Museo Egizio's scholarship describes this combination as part of the royal architecture ensuring the sun's successful return from the Netherworld — see [the Museo Egizio's study of its Amduat papyri](https://rivista.museoegizio.it/article/the-amduat-papyri-in-the-museo-egizio-tradition-and-innovation-between-the-twenty-first-and-twenty-second-dynasties/).

For this encyclopaedia, the seventy-five forms offer an Egyptian model of luminous taxonomy. One source may possess many faces without becoming internally divided. The quality of its manifestation changes according to its region, operation, receiver, and stage of transformation.
