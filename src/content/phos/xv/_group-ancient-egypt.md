Ancient Egyptian religion developed one of history's most elaborate architectures of light. The sun was simultaneously a visible celestial body, a divine being, a source of life, a royal principle, a measure of time, and a traveller passing between the worlds of the living and the dead. Its setting was not understood as simple extinction. The sun entered the Duat, encountered hidden powers, united with the depths of death, regenerated itself, and returned at dawn.

Egyptian luminous theology cannot be reduced to solar worship alone. Solar light existed in relationship with the stars, the nocturnal sky, divine fire, the moon, the luminous eye, the radiance of the gods, and the transformed condition of the dead. Darkness was not merely opposed to light. It was the hidden region through which light underwent renewal. The Egyptian tradition therefore offers an early and remarkably complete vision of procession, manifestation, withdrawal, regeneration, and return.
