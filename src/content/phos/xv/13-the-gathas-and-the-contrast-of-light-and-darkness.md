The Gāthās are seventeen Old Avestan hymns embedded within the Yasna. They are traditionally attributed to Zarathustra and constitute the oldest linguistic and theological stratum of the Zoroastrian tradition. Their language is extraordinarily compressed and difficult, and major passages continue to receive different translations. Any interpretation must therefore avoid treating later Zoroastrian cosmology as though it appeared fully developed in the earliest hymns.

The central Gāthic contrast is not simply physical light against physical darkness. It is the opposition between *aša* and *druj*: truth, right order, and proper existence against deception, distortion, and the Lie. The distinction appears through thought, speech, choice, and action. Human beings do not merely observe this opposition from outside; they participate in it through the manner in which they orient their minds and lives.

## The two spirits

Yasna 30 describes two primordial mental or spiritual orientations whose thoughts, words, and actions are opposed. The wise distinguish between them and choose rightly, whereas the foolish fail to perceive their difference. [The University of Texas Linguistics Research Center's Old Avestan lesson on Yasna 30](https://liberalarts.utexas.edu/lrc/resources/ietexts/iranian/avestan/yasna-30-a-gatha-about-reward-and-punishment.php) emphasises this connection between the two spirits and the ethical opposition of truth and falsehood.

Light and darkness provide powerful images for this difference, but the Gāthās should not automatically be read as teaching two equal, eternal substances. Ahura Mazda — the Wise Lord — is associated with wisdom, truth, good mind, beneficent power, and the order through which life flourishes. The destructive orientation possesses real agency, but it does not possess an equally truthful mode of being. Falsehood depends upon distortion; [[vi-26|evil and darkness must not be equated]].

The Gāthic person must learn to see. Good and evil can be concealed beneath social custom, persuasive speech, institutional authority, and immediate advantage. Wisdom is luminous because it enables the person to distinguish the actual consequences of different paths.

This perception must become choice. The Gāthās do not present cosmic struggle as something resolved entirely by gods beyond humanity. Thought, speech, and action either strengthen the creation of truth or participate in the Lie. Ethical life becomes a form of luminous collaboration with Ahura Mazda.

Within the Architecture of Hidden Forces, the Gāthic contrast expresses light as truth-bearing [[x-9|orientation]]. Darkness is not merely the absence of sensory brightness. It is the condition produced when intelligence becomes separated from truth, speech from reality, and action from right relation.
