The Later Mohist writings, preserved within the Mozi, contain some of the earliest surviving systematic discussions of optics. Their investigators considered the straight-line movement of light, the formation and multiplication of shadows, the effects of differently positioned light sources, reflection from mirrors, and the production of inverted images through a small aperture. The Mohists were not merely collecting marvels. They attempted to establish definitions and give causal explanations for what observers could reproduce.

## The inverted image

Their account of the pinhole image is particularly important. When light from the upper and lower portions of an object passes through a narrow opening, its paths cross: light proceeding from the object's feet reaches the upper region of the receiving surface, while light from its head reaches the lower region. The resulting image is inverted. Modern scholarship recognises this as a genuine theoretical explanation based on the rectilinear propagation and crossing of light paths, not simply an accidental discovery of the camera-obscura effect. The Mohist writings also connect multiple shadows with multiple sources and examine relationships among the sizes and positions of source, obstacle, and shadow; [the Mohist Canon has accordingly been read](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-08797-4_2) as an alternative origin of theoretical science.

This material gives the encyclopaedia an essential corrective: Chinese reflection on light was not exclusively mystical or symbolic. It included disciplined investigation of sensible light. Yet Mohist optics can also serve as an exact physical analogy for higher questions. The aperture determines what passes; the paths of transmission determine where the image appears; and the receiving surface makes visible something that originated elsewhere. An [[iii-15|image]] may therefore be real while differing in scale, position, or orientation from its source.

## The chamber as a model of mediation

Within the Architecture of Hidden Forces, the Mohist chamber offers a model of mediated manifestation. Morphaithēr may be compared - not equated - with the receiving atmosphere in which previously invisible relations acquire appearance. The Fourfold Veil functions as an ordered series of apertures and media. A transmitted power may be inverted or displaced without being wholly false. Distortion can result from the geometry of reception rather than deception by the source.

The Mohists consequently establish a foundational law for this encyclopaedia: to understand any light, examine its source, path, interruption, aperture, and field of reception. This law applies literally to optics and analogically to psychic, etheric, visionary, and spiritual illumination.
