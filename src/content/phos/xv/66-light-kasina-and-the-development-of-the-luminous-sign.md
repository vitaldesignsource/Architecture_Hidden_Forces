A *kasiṇa* is an object used to develop unified concentration. In the Theravāda commentarial system, practitioners may work with earth, water, fire, air, colours, light, or delimited space. The word suggests totality or wholeness because the selected quality gradually occupies the complete field of attention.

In light-kasiṇa practice, the practitioner begins with a visible region of brightness, such as a patch of sunlight or moonlight entering through an opening and falling upon a surface. The object is neither the sun itself nor uncontrolled glare. It is a stable and bounded presentation of luminosity.

[The Visuddhimagga](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/nanamoli/PathofPurification2011.pdf) describes light entering through a wall opening, keyhole, window, or gap among branches and forming a luminous circle. The practitioner attends to the quality of light until it can be apprehended with increasing continuity.

## Learning sign and counterpart sign

Later meditation manuals distinguish a preparatory object, a learning sign or *uggaha-nimitta*, and a counterpart sign or *paṭibhāga-nimitta*. The physical object initially stabilises attention. Its mental sign can then arise without the external support, eventually becoming clearer, more uniform, and more responsive to meditative control.

The counterpart sign is not merely valued because it looks impressive. It indicates that the mind has become capable of sustaining a refined object without constant dependence upon external sensory stimulation. The luminous field becomes internally stable.

The meditator may then expand the sign, allowing the perception of luminosity to fill the field of awareness. The ordinary distinction between a bright object and surrounding darkness gives way to a more comprehensive luminous totality.

The historical distinction between early discourses and later commentarial systematisation should be preserved. The technical stages of learning sign and counterpart sign are developed most clearly in works such as Buddhaghosa's fifth-century Visuddhimagga, rather than being explained in the same form throughout the earliest suttas. The text itself lists light as one of ten kasiṇas and notes this later technical elaboration.

## A formed vessel

Kasiṇa practice demonstrates that interior light can be intentionally cultivated. This separates it from spontaneous visionary light, neurological flashes, dreams, and unexpected spiritual encounters. The resulting sign has been formed through a disciplined interaction among sense, memory, attention, [[x-13|imagination]], and concentration.

Calling the sign "mind-made" does not make it unreal. Thoughts, intentions, dreams, symbols, and psychic forms can produce real transformations. The more exact issue is the order to which the sign belongs and the function it performs.

Within the Architecture of Hidden Forces, the luminous *nimitta* is a deliberately formed psychic vessel. Attention supplies direction, repetition supplies stability, imagination supplies image-form, and the subtle field supplies the living atmosphere within which the image becomes experientially present.

The sign should not automatically be interpreted as a deity, external being, or supreme revelation. It is first an instrument of concentration. Its spiritual value lies in the coherence it establishes and the further insight it makes possible.
