Solstices mark the extremes of the Sun's apparent annual northward and southward movement, corresponding to the longest and shortest daylight periods of the year for a given hemisphere. Equinoxes occur when the Sun crosses the celestial equator, producing approximately equal lengths of day and night.

Symbolically, solstices represent maximal extension and the turning of the cycle. The light reaches an extreme and begins returning toward its opposite. Equinoxes represent dynamic equilibrium: light and darkness entering temporary proportion.

These events are not static points but thresholds within a continuous solar rhythm. Their ritual importance arises from their capacity to make invisible temporal order visibly measurable.
