The decans originated as groups of stars used in ancient Egyptian celestial observation and timekeeping. Their heliacal risings contributed to the division of the night and the organisation of calendrical cycles. Later astrological traditions integrated thirty-six decans into the twelve-sign zodiac as ten-degree divisions.

The Egyptian decans and later zodiacal decans are historically connected but should not be treated as completely identical systems. Their forms and functions changed through transmission — see [Sarah Symons's study of Egyptian decanal star lists](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/272565911_Contexts_and_elements_of_decanal_star_lists_in_Ancient_Egypt).

Within the luminous architecture, the decans represent intensified differentiation. Zodiacal qualities are not uniform across an entire sign but become articulated into smaller temporal and stellar faces.
