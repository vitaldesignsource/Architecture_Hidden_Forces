Planets appear luminous primarily because they reflect sunlight, although astronomical bodies may also emit radiation outside the visible range and possess their own thermal and electromagnetic activity.

In traditional astrology and natural magic, planetary rays describe the differentiated influences attributed to celestial bodies. Medieval thinkers such as al-Kindī developed theories in which stellar and planetary rays contributed causally to events in the sublunary world — see the [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy on al-Kindī](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2009/entries/al-kindi/). These are historical cosmological theories, not established conclusions of modern astrophysics.

Within the system, planetary rays are best treated as structured symbolic-temporal currents whose claims must be evaluated independently. The planet becomes a celestial lens through which qualities of time, psyche, and natural order are interpreted.
