Planets appear luminous primarily because they reflect sunlight, although astronomical bodies may also emit radiation outside the visible range and possess their own thermal and electromagnetic activity.

In traditional astrology and natural magic, planetary rays describe the differentiated influences attributed to celestial bodies. Medieval thinkers such as al-Kindī developed theories in which stellar and planetary rays contributed causally to events in the sublunary world — see the [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy on al-Kindī](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2009/entries/al-kindi/). These are historical cosmological theories, not conclusions of modern astrophysics; but the system does not treat them as traditional beliefs standing in for an absent force.

Within the system, planetary influence is real. Its physical, etheric, psychic, temporal, symbolic, and spiritual mechanisms may differ, overlap, or remain partly unknown, and a planetary ray need not be electromagnetic to be real. The rays are structured currents of celestial quality received upon several planes at once, and the planet is a celestial lens through which qualities of time, psyche, and natural order are genuinely differentiated, not merely interpreted. What remains contested — and the marker above refers to this alone — is the degree and precision of particular astrological claims, which are legitimate subjects of investigation and discernment. The capillary-dynamolysis experiments of Lili Kolisko, which sought to register planetary conditions in the behaviour of metallic salt solutions, belong to that investigation and are acknowledged here as a serious attempt at it rather than a settled proof.
