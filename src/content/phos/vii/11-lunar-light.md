The Moon is not ordinarily a self-luminous source in the way the Sun and stars are. Its visible brightness is primarily reflected sunlight, modified by the geometry among Sun, Earth, Moon, and observer — see [NASA's astronomy glossary](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/glossary/).

Lunar light is therefore relational light. It changes visibly through phases while the Moon itself remains whole. What changes is the illuminated portion presented to the terrestrial observer.

Symbolically, lunar luminosity belongs to receptivity, reflection, memory, periodicity, imagination, fertility, bodily rhythm, and the changing forms of psychic life.

The Moon demonstrates that reflected light can become an extraordinarily powerful organiser of human time and meaning without pretending to be its originating source.
