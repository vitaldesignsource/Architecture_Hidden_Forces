Circumpolar stars remain above the horizon for observers at particular latitudes, appearing to revolve around the celestial pole without rising or setting.

Because they do not disappear beneath the horizon, these stars became powerful images of endurance and immortality. Ancient Egyptian traditions associated northern circumpolar stars with the imperishable or indestructible stars, making them appropriate destinations or companions for the transfigured dead — see [this Waseda University research overview](https://www.asiaresearchnews.com/html/article.php/aid/3647/cid/2/research/science/waseda_university/ancient_egyptian_astronomy.html).

Their immortality is observational and symbolic rather than literal. Stars themselves form, change, and eventually die. Yet from the human horizon, their unsetting motion offered a celestial image of life beyond ordinary cycles of appearance and disappearance.
