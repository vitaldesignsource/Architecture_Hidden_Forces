An ecosystem is an ordered household of transformation. Solar energy is captured by primary producers, stored in chemical bonds, transferred through feeding relations, used in metabolism, and progressively dissipated as heat. Matter circulates through air, water, soil, organism, waste, death, decomposition, and renewed assimilation.

Producers, consumers, decomposers, atmosphere, climate, water, minerals, and living communities form an interdependent field. NASA's Earth Observatory traces this solar dependence from photosynthetic producers through consumers and decomposers in [its account of the carbon cycle](https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/features/CarbonCycle).

## The etheric dimension of a landscape

Ecology also possesses an etheric dimension. Organisms do not merely exchange substances; they participate in shared formative atmospheres. Forests, wetlands, deserts, reefs, and grasslands develop distinctive etheric textures through the reciprocal activity of their inhabitants, elements, rhythms, histories, and celestial conditions.

No organism possesses light entirely for itself. The leaf receives solar radiance and fixes its consequences into form. That form becomes fruit, wood, shelter, animal tissue, movement, waste, memory, death, soil, and new growth. Each being receives from a field it did not create and modifies the conditions inherited by those that follow.

Morphaithēr is the total living atmosphere of this retained and transmitted relation. It does not replace chemistry, climate, evolution, or food-web dynamics. It reveals their participation in a deeper formative world.

Ecology is therefore both an economy of solar transformation and a communion of etheric exchange. Life is sustained by participation, and participation is governed by right relation.
