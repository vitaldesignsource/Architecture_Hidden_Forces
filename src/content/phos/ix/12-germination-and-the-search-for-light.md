A seed holds organised potential in a state of concentrated latency. Germination begins when appropriate relations among water, temperature, oxygen, chemical conditions, and — depending upon the species — light or darkness release developmental activity from dormancy. Plant photoreceptors such as phytochromes and cryptochromes help interpret spectral conditions. Once a shoot emerges, phototropism can orient growth toward advantageous illumination. [Research on plant phytochromes](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6941077/) shows how light participates in germination and early development.

The search for light is not human-style conscious longing, but neither is it meaningless mechanics. It is a living orientation produced through the plant's real sensitivity to its environment. The organism detects a condition necessary for its unfolding and reorganises growth in relation to it.

## The seed as vessel of memory

Etherically, the seed is a vessel of compressed formative memory. Its future shape is not present as a miniature physical plant, yet the capacity to become that plant is actively retained. Germination occurs when outer conditions resonate with inner formative potential.

The seed begins within darkness, gathers water and warmth, breaks its enclosing form, divides its growth between root and shoot, and moves toward illumination. Darkness and light are not enemies in this process. Darkness protects and concentrates; light calls forth and differentiates. Germination is the passage from hidden formative light into visible living form.
