The eye is aperture, lens, chamber, photosensitive surface, living threshold, and organ of revelation. The cornea and lens focus incoming light; the iris regulates the aperture; the retina converts changing patterns of illumination into neural activity. Rods support vision under low-light conditions, cones enable colour and high-acuity vision, and melanopsin-containing retinal ganglion cells participate in non-image-forming responses such as circadian entrainment and the pupillary reflex — see [this review of intrinsically photosensitive retinal ganglion cells](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5436192/).

Vision is not a passive photograph of an independently completed world. The eye receives changing distributions of radiation, while the nervous system and soul participate in the formation of boundaries, colours, movement, depth, significance, and recognisable objects. The visible world arises through the relation of light, object, organ, etheric vitality, nervous activity, memory, attention, and consciousness.

## The proportionate interval

The eye also reveals a central law of illumination: revelation requires limitation. Without aperture, adaptation, discrimination, and selective reception, light would not become sight. Too little illumination conceals, but too much overwhelms. Vision exists within a proportionate interval between deprivation and excess.

Physical sight is only one kind of seeing. Symbolic, imaginal, etheric, astral, and spiritual perception operate through other faculties and conditions. Ordinary vision should not be treated as the universal measure of everything capable of becoming known.
