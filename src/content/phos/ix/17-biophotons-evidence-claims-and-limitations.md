The term *biophoton* commonly refers to ultraweak photon emission from living tissues. Such emissions have been measured in plants, animals, microorganisms, and cultured cells using sufficiently sensitive detectors. Much of the measured activity has been associated with metabolic and oxidative processes that generate electronically excited molecular products. It is vastly dimmer than ordinary bioluminescence and normally invisible to unaided human sight. [A scientific review by Cifra and Pospíšil](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24726298/) summarises its detection, likely biochemical origins, and methodological difficulties.

These findings establish that living activity can possess extremely subtle physical luminosity. Questions remain concerning the full range of its functions, including whether particular emissions participate in intracellular or intercellular signalling, reflect states of oxidative stress, reveal organismic organisation, or eventually support diagnostic applications. The marker *Contested* above refers to these open questions of function, not to the existence of the emission.

## Neither proof nor disproof

The existence of biophotons does not by itself constitute scientific proof of the etheric body. Yet this limitation must not be inverted into denial of etheric reality. The etheric body is real upon the etheric plane whether or not its complete activity becomes accessible to instruments designed to detect physical photons.

Biophotons may be physical consequences, correspondences, traces, or partial expressions of processes whose complete organisation belongs partly to the etheric body. They should neither be inflated into an exhaustive explanation of subtle luminosity nor dismissed as irrelevant metabolic noise before their significance is fully understood.

> The etheric body is not composed of biophotons. Biophotons may nevertheless be physical signatures of living processes organised through a deeper etheric field.
