The skin is the body's most extensive visible boundary and one of its principal interfaces with solar radiation. Ultraviolet radiation can initiate vitamin D synthesis, influence pigmentation, damage DNA, contribute to photoageing, and increase cancer risk. Visible and infrared wavelengths interact with tissue according to wavelength, intensity, duration, depth of penetration, and biological condition.

The skin therefore receives light as a differentiated spectrum rather than a single undivided power. It protects, admits, absorbs, reflects, senses, regulates temperature, participates in immunity, and continually repairs itself. It is neither a sealed wall nor an unrestricted opening.

## The subtler boundary

Within subtle anatomy, the physical skin corresponds to — but is not identical with — the outer organisation of the etheric body. The etheric boundary extends through and beyond the physical surface, maintaining the living distinction between organism and environment while permitting exchange. Etheric projection, contact, depletion, and restoration may involve movements across this subtler boundary even when no ordinary sensory event is visible.

To call the skin a luminous boundary is therefore both physical and ontological. It is where solar forces, embodied life, etheric organisation, sensation, identity, and environment meet. Its health depends upon intelligent permeability: the preservation of distinction without the refusal of participation.
