Sleep and waking arise through the interaction of circadian timing, homeostatic sleep pressure, nervous activity, metabolism, behaviour, and environmental conditions. The solar cycle strongly influences this pattern without mechanically determining every individual's schedule. Darkness ordinarily allows nocturnal physiology to emerge, while morning illumination helps prepare the organism for outward activity.

Artificial light can alter this ancient relation by extending illumination into biological night. Ordinary room light before bedtime has been shown to delay and shorten nocturnal melatonin production — see [the controlled human study by Gooley and colleagues](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article-abstract/96/3/E463/2597236).

## Two orientations of one being

Within the subtle anatomy, waking is an increased orientation of consciousness and vitality toward the sensible world. The astral body becomes more tightly engaged with sensation, movement, desire, and outward attention. Etheric forces are increasingly directed toward active embodiment. Sleep partially loosens this outward orientation, allowing different kinds of restoration, assimilation, memory processing, and subtle reorganisation to occur.

Sleep is not simply an absence of consciousness or a lapse into meaningless darkness. It is an organised withdrawal from one mode of participation into another. The physical organism rests while etheric, psychic, and spiritual processes continue according to their respective levels.

Dawn calls the organism toward manifestation. Noon sustains expenditure. Evening begins recollection. Night returns life toward hidden maintenance. Sleep and waking are therefore biological states, etheric tides, psychic transitions, and images of procession and return.
