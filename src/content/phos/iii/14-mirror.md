A mirror returns light from a boundary. Its image depends upon the condition, angle, curvature, and orientation of the reflective surface.

The soul becomes mirror-like when it receives and returns an image of what encounters it. A calm psyche may reflect with relative fidelity. An agitated psyche produces distortion. A rigid or damaged surface fragments the image.

## The further turn

Reflection is not identity. The mirror contains an appearance of the source, not the source itself.

Self-knowledge requires a further turn: the mirror must become aware of its own surface. Otherwise, its distortions remain invisible and are attributed to whatever it reflects.
