Within the anatomy of an illumination, radiance is the moment that follows the source and precedes the ray. The source is where light is what it is; the ray is light already on its way to somewhere. Between them stands a region of presence that belongs to neither: light surrounding its source in every direction at once, before any direction has been chosen.

## Its place in the sequence

Every illumination the encyclopaedia dissects passes through this moment. A [[iii-1|source]] that gave off no radiance would be a point without a neighbourhood, and nothing could approach it. A [[iii-4|ray]] that proceeded from no radiance would begin nowhere. Radiance is the anatomical condition of both: the field a source establishes simply by being what it is, from which every particular transmission is afterwards drawn.

This is why the receiver's first contact with a source is almost never with its ray. One enters a radiance long before one is struck by a beam. Warmth is felt before the fire is seen; the atmosphere of a person or a place is registered before any word is addressed.

## What the anatomy asks of it

Anatomically, radiance is judged by its relation to its source rather than by its extent. A region of presence that remains continuous with what sustains it is radiance proper. A region that has detached from its source, or that surrounds something incapable of sustaining it, is a field without an origin, and the ray drawn from it will carry nothing.

The quality itself, its degrees, its counterfeits in borrowed brightness, and the discernment they call for belong to [[iv-31|the entry on radiance among the luminous qualities]]. This entry records only where radiance stands in the body of an illumination.
