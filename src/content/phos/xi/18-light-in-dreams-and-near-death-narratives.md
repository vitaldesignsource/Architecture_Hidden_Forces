Dreams frequently contain extraordinary luminosity: suns appearing indoors, beings composed of light, radiant deceased persons, tunnels, stars, luminous cities, and landscapes without visible sources of illumination. These may arise from personal memory, astral experience, psychic symbolism, spiritual contact, or mixed processes.

Near-death narratives likewise often include movement through darkness, entry into intense but non-painful light, panoramic memory, encounters with deceased persons, presences of intelligence or love, and altered experiences of time and identity. Prospective cardiac-arrest studies confirm that some survivors report structured experiences associated with periods of resuscitation, although the data do not by themselves settle their ultimate metaphysical cause — see [the AWARE II study of awareness during cardiac arrest](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37423492/). The marker *Contested* above refers to that question of cause, not to the reality of the reports.

## A serious reading

The Architecture of Hidden Forces permits a serious interpretation of these narratives. During extreme bodily crisis, the ordinary relation among physical, etheric, astral, and psychic bodies may loosen. Consciousness may consequently perceive realities ordinarily veiled by embodied sensory organisation.

A near-death experience is not necessarily complete biological death, and no single narrative should define the post-mortem world for everyone. Nevertheless, the recurrence, coherence, and transformative consequences of many accounts make them important evidence within phenomenological and spiritual inquiry.
