Luminous experiences should be recorded before interpretation hardens around them. The first account should preserve what occurred as precisely as possible, separating direct observation from later explanation.

## Record the following

1. **Date, time, and location.** Note the physical environment, weather, lighting, and whether the experience occurred indoors, outdoors, asleep, awake, meditating, praying, or in transition.
2. **Condition of the body.** Record sleep, food, hydration, illness, pain, migraine history, medication, substances, breathing practices, exertion, and unusual sensory exposure.
3. **State of consciousness.** Note whether awareness was ordinary, meditative, hypnagogic, dreaming, ecstatic, dissociated, grieving, fearful, calm, or near unconsciousness.
4. **Eyes and orientation.** Record whether the eyes were open or closed, whether the light moved with the eyes or head, whether it persisted after blinking, and whether changing the background affected it.
5. **Location of the appearance.** Describe whether the light seemed external, internal, around the body, within a subtle landscape, nonlocal, or impossible to place.
6. **Visual qualities.** Record colour, intensity, shape, texture, transparency, movement, rhythm, duration, boundary, direction, distance, and whether forms appeared within the light.
7. **Other sensory qualities.** Note sound, pressure, temperature, vibration, scent, emotion, bodily sensation, perceived intelligence, and alterations of time or space.
8. **Apparent agency.** Record whether the light behaved mechanically, responsively, symbolically, intelligently, or independently of expectation.
9. **Content and communication.** Write down any words, images, knowledge, commands, memories, presences, or symbolic relations exactly as experienced, before interpreting them.
10. **Witnesses and physical correlates.** Note whether anyone else perceived the event, whether environmental changes occurred, and whether any instrument or recording captured something relevant.
11. **Immediate and lasting effects.** Record changes in mood, health, perception, behaviour, belief, relationships, dreams, spiritual practice, and ethical orientation over subsequent days and weeks.
12. **Alternative interpretations.** Consider physical, retinal, neurological, psychological, imaginative, etheric, astral, symbolic, spiritual, and mixed explanations without forcing an immediate conclusion.
13. **Provisional judgement.** Classify the experience as unresolved, probably physiological, primarily imaginal, possibly psychic, plausibly etheric or astral, spiritually significant, or potentially revelatory. Retain the word *provisional*.
14. **Review over time.** Return to the account after emotional intensity has diminished. Compare recurring experiences, fulfilled information, contradictions, consequences, and changes in interpretation.

## Care and counsel

Urgent physical or psychological symptoms should receive appropriate professional assessment without assuming that medical attention invalidates spiritual meaning. Likewise, spiritual counsel should not automatically replace medical care.

The protocol exists to protect both revelation and the receiver. It prevents genuine encounters from being casually dismissed while preventing dazzling appearances from acquiring authority they have not earned.
