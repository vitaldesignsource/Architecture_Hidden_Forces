A halo is a concentrated luminous field appearing around the head, body, organ, or centre of a living being. It may represent etheric vitality, astral intensity, spiritual presence, concentrated attention, or the penetration of higher light into the subtle constitution.

Traditional sacred art often portrays halos as stable discs or radiant crowns. These images may be theological symbols, artistic conventions, records of visionary perception, or several of these simultaneously. A symbol can preserve the memory of a real perception even after becoming formalised in art.

## Optical borders and subtle halos

Natural contrast effects can produce apparent borders of brightness around figures, while retinal adaptation can generate luminous outlines after prolonged viewing. Authentic subtle halos must therefore be distinguished from ordinary optical effects. Their reality is more convincingly indicated when perception remains stable across changing backgrounds, contains coherent qualitative information, or appears independently to multiple prepared observers.

The halo is not the whole aura. It is a region of intensified luminosity within the larger field, often indicating concentration around consciousness, identity, spiritual receptivity, or a particular active centre.
