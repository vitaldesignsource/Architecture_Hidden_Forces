Coherence asks whether the parts of an experience belong together intelligibly. Does the message contradict itself? Does the luminous form correspond to the quality of the presence? Does the experience illuminate other realities without requiring their distortion?

Stability asks whether the experience maintains its character across time, changing emotional conditions, repeated contemplation, and critical examination. Authentic encounters may be brief, but their essential structure often remains intelligible after the initial intensity fades.

Consequence asks what the experience produces. Does it deepen truthfulness, compassion, courage, responsibility, integration, and spiritual clarity? Or does it generate grandiosity, compulsion, terror, dependency, confusion, isolation, and exemption from ordinary ethical obligations?

## Convergence over time

These criteria are not infallible. Disturbing revelations can be authentic, and comforting experiences can deceive. Genuine initiation may temporarily destabilise a person before deeper integration becomes possible. Consequence must therefore be evaluated across sufficient time.

The most reliable judgement arises from several forms of evidence: phenomenological detail, symbolic coherence, ethical fruit, independent confirmation, tradition, repeated observation, bodily and psychological condition, and the Law of Right Relation.
