A hallucination is conventionally defined as a perception-like experience occurring without a corresponding shared sensory stimulus. Imagination is the faculty through which images can be intentionally or spontaneously formed within consciousness. Spiritual experience is an encounter, participation, or disclosure involving spiritual reality.

These categories overlap phenomenologically but are not identical. A spiritual being may become perceptible through imagination. A psychic projection may possess hallucination-like vividness. A neurological event may carry profound symbolic meaning. A genuine encounter may be partially clothed in forms supplied by the receiver.

## Levels of causation

Clinical terminology identifies the mode of appearance and possible need for care; it does not possess automatic authority to determine the ultimate ontology of every experience. Conversely, calling an experience spiritual does not exempt it from psychological or medical consideration.

The most faithful approach permits multiple levels of causation. An experience may be neurological in mechanism, psychic in content, symbolic in form, and spiritually consequential. Another may be authentic contact distorted by fear or illness. A third may be entirely imaginative yet become a vessel for genuine self-knowledge.

The goal is neither to pathologise the sacred nor to sanctify every disturbance. It is to discern the complete architecture through which the appearance became possible.
