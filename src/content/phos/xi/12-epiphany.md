Epiphany is sudden disclosure. Something previously concealed becomes immediately intelligible, present, or spiritually significant. Unlike theophany, epiphany does not necessarily involve the appearance of a divine being. It may occur through an ordinary object, phrase, landscape, relationship, memory, or moment of recognition.

Epiphanic light may be visually luminous, but it may also consist of an inward clarification in which the familiar world becomes transparent to a deeper order. The object remains physically unchanged while its meaning becomes illuminated.

Such moments reveal that manifestation is not only the arrival of new information. Sometimes what was always present becomes visible because the receiver has entered a new relation with it.

## True and false epiphany

Epiphany should be judged by what it reveals and what it makes possible. A true epiphany integrates previously separated realities. A false epiphany produces certainty without adequate truth, inflation without responsibility, or excitement without enduring clarification.
