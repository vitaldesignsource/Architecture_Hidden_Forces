*Nimitta* means a sign, mark, or mental appearance. In Theravāda meditation literature, particularly the *Visuddhimagga*, the term may refer to signs arising as concentration develops around a meditation object. The preparatory object may give rise to a learning sign, which may then become purified into a counterpart sign associated with access concentration and absorption.

A nimitta is not always light, and different practices, teachers, and practitioners describe it differently. In breath meditation it may appear as a star, pearl, wheel, cloud, moon, sun, luminous mist, or tactile-seeming presence. In *kasiṇa* practice, it may reproduce and then refine the qualities of the original meditation object. The counterpart sign is frequently described as clearer and more purified than the initial learning sign. [Bhikkhu Ñāṇamoli's translation of the *Visuddhimagga*](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/nanamoli/PathofPurification2011.pdf) preserves the complete traditional treatment.

## Where the sign forms

Within the system, a nimitta is an image formed where disciplined attention, psychic substance, subtle perception, and the selected object enter sustained relation. It may be internally generated, genuinely receptive, or both: the mind provides the field of appearance while a deeper formative reality supplies the determining influence.

The nimitta should be stabilised without being worshipped. Its clarity may indicate concentration, but brilliance alone does not establish spiritual rank or metaphysical source.
