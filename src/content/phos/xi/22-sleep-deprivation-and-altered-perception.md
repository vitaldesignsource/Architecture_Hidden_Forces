Sleep deprivation destabilises attention, memory, emotional regulation, temporal judgement, and perceptual continuity. With increasing deprivation, shadows may seem animate, peripheral forms may appear, patterns may organise themselves into faces, and visual, tactile, or auditory hallucinations can occur. Research reviews find perceptual distortions and hallucinations across prolonged wakefulness, with visual effects reported particularly often — see [the review by Waters and colleagues](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2018.00303/full).

The existence of these effects does not prove that every sleep-deprived vision is meaningless. Exhaustion may weaken ordinary filters and allow subconscious, psychic, or subtle material to enter awareness. But it also damages the receiver's stability and capacity for discernment.

## Not a method

Sleep deprivation should therefore not be treated as a reliable method of spiritual attainment. A threshold opened by injuring the vessel may admit genuine perception, distortion, or both, while simultaneously reducing the ability to distinguish among them.

The Law of Right Relation applies: greater permeability is not always greater vision. A prepared threshold is different from a broken boundary.
