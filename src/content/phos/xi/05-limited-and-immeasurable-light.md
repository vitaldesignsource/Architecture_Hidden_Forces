Limited light appears within a bounded region: a point, disc, halo, chamber, body, aperture, or localised field. Its limits may arise from the source, the capacity of the receiver, the chosen object of concentration, or the particular plane through which the light becomes perceptible.

Immeasurable light appears without discernible boundary. It may extend in all directions, dissolve the distinction between centre and circumference, or encompass the experiencer within a field that cannot be spatially surveyed. Contemplative traditions sometimes describe the intentional expansion of a luminous meditation sign until it fills an immeasurable field.

## Size and order

The distinction does not mean limited light is false and immeasurable light is divine. A bounded radiance may represent a precise and authentic being, symbol, centre, or localised operation. An apparently infinite light may arise through genuine spiritual expansion, psychic amplification, or the temporary loss of ordinary spatial boundaries.

The decisive question is not size but order. A small light may carry greater intelligibility than a vast brilliance. Immeasurability indicates the collapse or transcendence of perceived limits; it does not by itself identify what has exceeded them.
