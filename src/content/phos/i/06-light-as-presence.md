Light gives presence to what would otherwise remain unavailable. An unilluminated form may physically exist, but it does not occupy the visual field of the observer. When light reaches it, the form acquires perceptual presence.

Metaphysically, presence is more than visibility. It is the degree to which something becomes available within a field of relationship. A forgotten memory becomes present when it enters awareness. A principle becomes present when it is understood. A spiritual influence becomes present when it produces a recognisable transformation within life or consciousness.

Light is therefore the vehicle of presence-making. It establishes “here-ness,” not necessarily in physical space, but within attention, experience, and participation.

## Availability is not comprehension

Presence does not guarantee comprehension. Something may stand fully illuminated before a receiver and remain unrecognised. Light establishes availability; the receiver must still possess the capacity to distinguish what has become present.
