Beauty is the radiance of relation becoming perceptible. It appears when proportion, vitality, rhythm, meaning, material, and form enter a consonant arrangement.

Beauty is not identical with decoration or prettiness. Severe forms can be beautiful. Ruins can disclose beauty through time, loss, and endurance. Darkness can intensify beauty by giving light depth and boundary.

## Beauty and glamour

Beauty attracts attention toward form. At its highest, that attraction draws the receiver beyond surface appearance toward the relations that make the form coherent. Beauty becomes revelatory when visible harmony points toward an invisible order.

Yet beauty can also become [[vi-18|glamour]]. A brilliant surface may enchant attention while concealing disorder beneath it. Authentic beauty becomes deeper under sustained perception; counterfeit beauty depends upon immediate capture.

The beautiful is luminous when its radiance remains faithful to its inner architecture.
