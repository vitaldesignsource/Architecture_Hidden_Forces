Light does not always create the qualities it reveals. Frequently, it brings latent qualities into activity.

Colour provides the clearest physical analogy. A surface possesses material properties that condition how it interacts with light, but its colour becomes visually actual only within the relationship among illumination, surface, environment, eye, and perception. Light activates a capacity already held within the form.

## Throughout the architecture

This principle extends throughout the architecture. A symbol may contain layers of potential meaning that remain dormant until approached by a sufficiently prepared consciousness. An organism contains developmental capacities awakened under appropriate environmental conditions. An idea may remain implicit until an act of insight brings its structure into understanding.

Actualisation requires more than force. It requires correspondence between what the light carries and what the form can receive.

Light is therefore an agent of becoming. It calls latent relations into expression without necessarily manufacturing them from nothing.
