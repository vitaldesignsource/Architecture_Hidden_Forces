Truth is related to light because truth involves disclosure. Something concealed becomes knowable, and what seemed fragmented is brought into clearer relation.

Yet illumination alone does not guarantee truth. A light can reveal one surface while concealing another. A coloured medium can alter appearance. Glare can destroy distinctions. A receiver may misinterpret what has been accurately shown.

Truth requires a faithful relationship among source, medium, object, and receiver. It requires not only disclosure but correct recognition.

## Shadow is not falsehood

Shadow is not necessarily falsehood. Shadows disclose shape, depth, position, and obstruction. Concealment may protect what is not yet ready for exposure. The enemy of truth is not simply darkness; it is distortion — the presentation of a partial appearance as though it were complete.

Truthful light reveals while acknowledging the limits of its field.
