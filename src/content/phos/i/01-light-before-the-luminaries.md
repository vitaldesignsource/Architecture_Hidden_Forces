The image of light existing before the Sun, Moon, and stars expresses one of the deepest distinctions in luminous metaphysics: the distinction between light itself and the formed bodies through which light becomes localised.

A luminary is a vessel, concentration, or organised bearer of light. The Sun is a physical luminary. A lamp is an artificial luminary. A sacred image, teacher, intellect, or awakened consciousness may become a luminary in an analogical sense. Yet the principle of illumination cannot be reduced entirely to any single vessel through which it appears.

## Priority of principle, not of time

Light before the luminaries does not mean physical radiation existing without physical causes. It describes an ontological priority. Light precedes the lamp in the order of principle even when, within physical time, a lamp must exist before its beam can appear.

This primordial light is the possibility of manifestation before manifestation has been gathered into particular centres. Luminaries arise when that possibility is concentrated, bounded, given rhythm, and made capable of entering determinate relations. The Sun does not create the metaphysical meaning of illumination. It embodies it cosmically.

Light before the luminaries is therefore luminosity prior to localisation, disclosure before the formation of a particular revealer, and intelligibility before the emergence of a particular intelligence capable of articulating it.
