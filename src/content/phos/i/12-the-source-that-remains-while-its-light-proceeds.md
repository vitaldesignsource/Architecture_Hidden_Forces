A source does not need to abandon itself in order to become present through its effects. This principle can be expressed through three movements:

> Remaining — Procession — Return

The source remains grounded in itself. Its light proceeds into increasing differentiation. The forms illuminated by that procession turn back toward their source through recognition, participation, and integration.

## Monē, proodos, epistrophē

In Greek metaphysical language these movements can be associated with *monē*, remaining; *proodos*, procession; and *epistrophē*, return. They should not be imagined as simple physical travel. Procession describes increasing communicability and differentiation, while return describes the recovery of relation to origin.

The source is therefore both transcendent and immanent: transcendent because it exceeds every particular manifestation, and immanent because every authentic radiance remains dependent upon it.

## Two errors

This protects the system from two errors. The first is treating the source as absent once manifestation begins. The second is treating every manifestation as the totality of the source.

The source remains more than its light, even while its light carries a genuine participation in the source.
