To reveal is to remove or penetrate a veil. Revelation is the event in which something hidden becomes disclosed to a receiver.

Yet revelation should not be reduced to the dramatic vision. It occurs whenever obscurity gives way to meaningful recognition. A scientific discovery, an honest realisation, the recovery of a memory, the exposure of an injustice, and a contemplative insight can all participate in the architecture of revelation.

## Not obliged to flatter

Revelation may disclose a form, a relationship, a cause, or a previously unknown capacity. It may illuminate what is beautiful, but it may also reveal corruption, suffering, contradiction, or self-deception. Genuine light is not obligated to flatter the receiver.

A revelation must be distinguished from the intensity accompanying it. Vividness alone is not proof. What appears should be examined through coherence, fidelity, consequence, and its ability to enter responsible relationship with the rest of reality.

Revelation begins with disclosure but reaches completion only through integration.
