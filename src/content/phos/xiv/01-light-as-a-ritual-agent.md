Light becomes a ritual agent when it does more than permit visibility and begins to perform a designated sacred function. It may reveal an image, mark the presence of a deity, purify a boundary, awaken a symbol, guide attention, establish sacred time, expose what is hidden, or carry an offering toward an invisible order.

Its action arises through the union of natural property and ritual intention. A flame physically emits heat and visible light, consumes fuel, changes surrounding air, and produces movement. Ritually, those same properties can become vehicles of purification, transformation, ascent, presence, and sacrifice. The symbolism is powerful because it develops from what fire and light actually do.

## Never decoration

Ritual light should therefore not be treated as mere decoration. Its position, source, colour, duration, intensity, movement, and relation to other objects all participate in the operation. A single lamp placed before an image establishes a different luminous architecture from a ring of candles, an open fire, a solar beam, or a chamber illuminated only by reflected moonlight.
