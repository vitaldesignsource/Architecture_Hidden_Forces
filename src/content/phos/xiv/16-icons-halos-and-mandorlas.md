An icon is not merely an illustration of a sacred subject. In traditions that treat icons as living sacred images, the icon is an interface of presence and contemplation. Its purpose is not simply to reproduce ordinary visual perspective but to reorganise perception around a spiritual order.

The halo marks the luminous quality of a sanctified person, intelligence, or divine presence. It externalises the idea that consciousness and spiritual authority radiate beyond the physical outline of the body. The mandorla encloses the entire figure within an almond-shaped or oval field, indicating a manifestation that crosses the boundary between visible and invisible worlds.

## Records, not conventions only

These forms need not be reduced to artistic convention, although convention determines how they are represented. They preserve accounts of transfiguration, visionary radiance, solar symbolism, sanctity, and the perception of subtle luminosity around living beings.
