A vigil is the disciplined preservation of awareness during a time normally given to sleep. By remaining awake through darkness, the participant transforms night from an unconscious interval into a consciously inhabited field.

The vigil does not reject darkness. It places a small, maintained light within it. This relation is central: the lamp does not abolish night but creates an illuminated centre from which darkness can be encountered without total absorption. Attention becomes the inward equivalent of the flame.

## Openness and discernment

Night reduces ordinary activity and alters the sensory environment. Silence deepens, distant sounds become more apparent, images arise more freely, and the boundaries between waking, dream, and imaginal perception may become more permeable. This can support contemplation and visionary receptivity, but fatigue can also produce perceptual distortion. A truthful vigil therefore balances openness with discernment and does not automatically interpret every unusual appearance as a higher manifestation.
