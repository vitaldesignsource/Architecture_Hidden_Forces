A mirror receives light and returns an image without permanently possessing either. It is therefore an especially powerful image of the soul, moon, imagination, and receptive intelligence. A well-oriented mirror can reveal what is outside the viewer's ordinary field, redirect a ray, multiply a flame, or bring a hidden object into appearance.

Ritually, mirrors may serve as instruments of contemplation, divination, protection, visionary concentration, or the presentation of a deity's reflected presence. Dark mirrors reduce ordinary visual information and provide a field in which subtle images may emerge. Bright mirrors emphasise clarity, return, solar reflection, and the encounter with one's own form.

## Not a portal by default

A ritual mirror should not automatically be described as a "portal". Its fundamental operation is reflective mediation. Whether it becomes an interface for psychic or spiritual encounter depends upon its consecration, use, surrounding architecture, and the capacities of the participants. The mirror teaches that reception is never neutral: angle, surface, light, and observer determine what becomes visible.
