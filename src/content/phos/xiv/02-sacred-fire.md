Sacred fire is physical flame established within a consecrated relationship. It may serve as witness, purifier, messenger, transformer, offering, divine presence, or visible body of an otherwise invisible power. What makes the fire sacred is not a special chemical composition but its origin, maintenance, consecration, purpose, and participation within a larger ritual order.

Fire transforms whatever it receives. Solid fuel becomes heat, light, smoke, ash, and released compounds. For this reason, it provides one of the most complete ritual images of sacrifice: a bounded form is dissolved, its hidden energy is liberated, and its substance passes into other states. The ascending flame and smoke suggest transmission, while the remaining ash preserves the material memory of the operation.

## Purify, then seal

Sacred fire can purify and consecrate, but these are not identical acts. Purification removes incompatible influence; consecration establishes a new sacred relation. Fire may first separate and then seal, first consume and then illuminate. Because physical fire can injure, spread, or consume toxic substances, reverence for sacred fire necessarily includes disciplined material safety.
