Every component of a ritual light contributes to its operation. The candle or lamp is not only a convenient means of producing flame; it is a structured body whose materials determine how the light emerges, how long it remains, how steadily it burns, and what residues it leaves.

The wick is a mediator. It draws fuel into the region of transformation, standing between hidden reserve and manifest fire. A well-prepared wick produces a stable flame; an unsuitable one smokes, flickers, drowns, or burns too rapidly. It therefore offers an image of the trained soul, which must draw power from deeper reserves without either obstructing or being consumed by it.

## Choosing the fuel

Oils, waxes, resins, and other fuels possess distinct histories, scents, textures, and ritual associations. Olive oil may carry solar, peaceful, royal, medicinal, or consecratory meanings depending upon the tradition. Beeswax can signify ordered natural labour, purity, sweetness, and the transformation of floral life. The material should be chosen according to the rite's actual symbolic and practical requirements — not merely because a correspondence chart assigns it an attractive meaning.
