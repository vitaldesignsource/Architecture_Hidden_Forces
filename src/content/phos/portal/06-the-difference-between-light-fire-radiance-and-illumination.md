Light, fire, radiance, and illumination are closely related, but they should not be treated as synonyms.

**Light** is the widest term. It can name a physical phenomenon, a quality of appearance, a power of revelation, a mode of consciousness, or a metaphysical principle.

**Fire** is transformative intensity. Physical fire consumes fuel, releases heat, alters matter, and frequently produces visible light. Symbolically and ritually, fire signifies transformation, sacrifice, purification, consecration, digestion, passion, destruction, and regeneration. Light reveals; fire changes. Light may proceed without consuming, while fire ordinarily acts through transformation. In [[ii-22|Ignisophia]] these operations meet: the inner fire both illuminates and transfigures.

**Radiance** is the outgoing expression of a source. It emphasises procession: the movement or extension by which what is concentrated within a source becomes present beyond it. Radiance may illuminate, warm, attract, awaken, or communicate the character of that from which it proceeds.

**Illumination** is relational. It is the event in which light reaches a receiver and something becomes visible, intelligible, awakened, or transformed. Illumination therefore requires more than a source. It requires a medium, a recipient, and sufficient receptivity. A light may be present while illumination fails because the receiver is closed, the medium is opaque, the field is turbulent, or the intensity exceeds what can be integrated.

**Brilliance** refers to intensity or vividness. It does not, by itself, establish purity, spiritual altitude, intelligence, or truth.

**Colour** is differentiated light as encountered through particular conditions. Physically, it depends upon wavelength distributions, surfaces, environments, sensory systems, and neural interpretation. Metaphysically, the system uses colour to describe the way a more general force receives tattvic, elemental, psychic, or symbolic determination.

These distinctions allow the portal to speak precisely. A divine source may radiate; an intermediary may transmit; a psychic medium may colour; a symbol may embody; an observer may receive; and illumination may or may not occur.
