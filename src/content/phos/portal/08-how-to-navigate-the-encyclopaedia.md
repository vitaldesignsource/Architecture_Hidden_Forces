An encyclopaedia of this size is not read from the beginning. It is entered, and there are three ways in, each answering a different question.

## By division, when you know the subject

The twenty-one divisions arrange the material by what it is about. The first principles of light and its place in the system come first; then the anatomy of an illumination, from source to witness; the qualities a light can have and the orders it can belong to; darkness; the sky, the elements, the organism, the soul; visionary phenomena and the beings that mediate light; the operations light performs and the rites that use it; a history of the doctrine across civilisations; the science; the symbols; the laws the system proposes; the practices; and a reference library.

Each division is a page listing every entry it will hold, whether or not that entry has been written. What is not yet written is listed as forthcoming rather than left out, so the shape of the whole is visible from the first day and a reference always has somewhere to land.

## By movement, when you want the argument

The Portal names a central movement in eight steps: Source, Procession, Mediation, Coloration, Embodiment, Perception, Transformation, Return. It is the path a light takes from where it originates to where it is seen and back, and the divisions can be read along it. A reader who wants the doctrine rather than the catalogue should read the volume at */phos* first, which states it in twelve sections, and then take Divisions I, II, V, VI, and XIX, where the system's own positions are.

## Across, by facet, when you have a question

Every entry names the traditions it draws on, the qualities and planes of light it concerns, the operations it describes, the symbols and primary texts it reads, and the period it belongs to. Browsing by any of these cuts across the divisions: every entry on the Neoplatonic doctrine of light, wherever it is filed; every entry that concerns coherence, or the halo, or the operation of consecration. The vocabulary of each facet is fixed in advance and shown in full, with a count against each value, so a value with no entries yet is a door not yet opened rather than a door that does not exist.

## What every entry tells you first

Before its first sentence, an entry shows the labels that say what kind of claim it makes: a primary text, a historical doctrine, a scientific finding, a phenomenological report, a position of the system, a comparative interpretation, or a speculative synthesis. The key to the labels is [[portal-10]]. Read the labels before the entry; they are the difference between knowing what a tradition taught and being told what to believe.

:: Every entry has an address of the form /phos/division/slug and an id of the form division-number, so that Grosseteste's *On Light* is xv-83 for as long as it keeps that number. Cross-references between entries use the id, and the audit that runs before every change checks that each one resolves.
