The Luminous Architecture begins with five primary registers. These are not presented as five scientifically proven substances stacked mechanically above one another. They are domains of inquiry through which different meanings and operations of light can be distinguished.

### Physical or sensible light

Physical light belongs to measurable interaction. It includes emission, wavelength, colour, reflection, refraction, diffraction, polarisation, interference, absorption, scattering, and the conversion of radiant energy into other forms. It makes visual perception possible, but what is perceived also depends upon the object, the surrounding medium, the eye, the nervous system, and the interpreting mind.

Physical light establishes the indispensable material foundation of the portal. Any metaphysics of light that contradicts well-established optical knowledge must either revise its claim or acknowledge that it is speaking symbolically rather than physically.

### Vital-formative light

Vital-formative light names the relationship between illumination and living organisation. Its scientifically established dimensions include photosynthesis, circadian entrainment, photoreception, seasonal regulation, biological coloration, and numerous light-dependent processes.

Within The Architecture of Hidden Forces the term also opens a metaphysical question: can light be understood as participating in the communication of form, rhythm, orientation, and developmental timing within living systems? The system's etheric interpretation belongs here, but it must be marked clearly as a metaphysical model rather than treated as an established conclusion of biology.

Vital-formative light is therefore the portal between sensible illumination and organised life — the point at which radiance becomes rhythm, growth, timing, orientation, and living response.

### Psychic or imaginal light

Psychic light is luminosity experienced within the field of soul. It appears in dreams, memory, imagination, emotion, visionary states, meditation, symbolic experience, and altered conditions of consciousness. It may be faint or overwhelming, coherent or turbulent, stable or rapidly changing, meaningful or fragmentary.

The psychic register is especially vulnerable to confusion because intensity can be mistaken for truth. An image may be brilliant without being divine. A dark vision may be psychologically truthful without being evil. A luminous figure may be an archetypal image, a memory construction, a neurological event, a symbolic communication, or — in the interpretation of some traditions — an encounter with an intermediary intelligence.

Psychic light must therefore be evaluated through context, coherence, stability, consequence, and relationship to the whole life of the person who experiences it.

### Noetic or intelligible light

Noetic light is the illumination of understanding. It is not necessarily visual. It occurs when a pattern becomes intelligible, when a principle gathers many fragments into unity, or when intelligence recognises an order previously hidden from it.

Noetic light reveals relations rather than merely objects. It is associated with Nous, contemplation, direct apprehension, philosophical insight, and the perception of archetypal form. Its characteristic is not brightness but intelligibility. It clarifies, integrates, orders, and discloses.

Within the system, noetic light stands above psychic imagery because an image may appear without being understood. Noetic illumination recognises the law, meaning, or formative principle expressed through the image.

### Divine or fontal light

Divine light is light considered in relation to the ultimate source. It is described across traditions as uncreated, limitless, superessential, primordial, self-radiant, or beyond the distinction between visible light and darkness.

The term *fontal* indicates light as fountain or origin: not one illumination among others but the source from which the possibility of illumination proceeds. Such light may be called invisible because it is not an object among objects. It is not perceived in the ordinary manner because, within these traditions, it is that by which perception, consciousness, and intelligibility become possible.

Divine light is therefore not simply the brightest member of a scale. It belongs to a different logical order. The source cannot be measured by the intensity of one of its appearances. It must be approached through the structures of participation, mediation, manifestation, and return.
