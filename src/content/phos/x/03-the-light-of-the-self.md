The light of the Self is the inward luminosity by which a being is present to itself. External objects require illumination to become visible, but consciousness does not require a second consciousness standing behind it before awareness can occur. It reveals objects and, in the act of revealing them, discloses its own presence.

The Self must not be confused with the social personality, accumulated biography, bodily image, or stream of inner commentary. These belong to the field of experience and may all become objects of observation. The deeper Self is the centre of continuity and participation through which these changing forms are gathered into a life.

## Intrinsic presence

To call the Self luminous is to affirm that its reality is not borrowed entirely from what it perceives. Consciousness does not become real only when it encounters an external object. It carries an intrinsic capacity for presence, recognition, and self-relation.

This light may become obscured by identification, compulsion, agitation, trauma, fantasy, or excessive outward dispersion. Yet obscuration is not annihilation. The light of the Self may remain present beneath conditions in which the personality can no longer clearly recognise it.
