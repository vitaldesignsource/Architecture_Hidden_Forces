Identity is not an unchanging object hidden somewhere inside the person. The body changes, memories are revised, emotions fluctuate, beliefs develop, relationships alter, and social roles appear and disappear. Yet a recognisable continuity may persist across these transformations.

Within the system, identity is a persistent pattern of illumination: a characteristic way in which essence, soul, memory, desire, attention, embodiment, and relation are gathered into a living centre.

## Pattern and source

The personality is one historical expression of this pattern, but it is not its deepest source. Essence supplies intrinsic determination. Soul mediates experience and meaning. The subtle bodies carry recurring structures of vitality and psychic motion. The physical body gives identity tangible location and action.

Identity can become rigid when a temporary form is mistaken for the whole being. It can become fragmented when no integrating centre can hold its changing contents in relation. Healthy identity preserves continuity without refusing transformation.

The Self is deeper than the identity it illuminates. Self-knowledge therefore requires both recognition of the persistent pattern and freedom from reducing the entire being to that pattern's current form.
