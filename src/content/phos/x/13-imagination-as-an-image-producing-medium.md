Imagination is the soul's image-producing and image-receiving medium. It can recombine sensory memories, embody abstract relations, anticipate possible futures, clothe spiritual intuitions in symbolic form, and give psychic realities an appearance through which consciousness can encounter them.

Imagination is not synonymous with unreality. Not everything imagined exists externally, but imagination itself is a real formative power, and the images arising within it can carry genuine psychic, symbolic, etheric, or spiritual content.

## Productive and receptive

It possesses both productive and receptive dimensions. Productive imagination forms images through deliberate activity. Receptive imagination allows images to arise from depths, beings, fields, and levels not consciously constructed by the personality. In practice these dimensions mingle, making discernment necessary.

The imagination is an intermediary rather than the final judge of truth. A spiritual influence may need imagination to become inwardly visible, just as physical light needs the eye to become sight. Yet the form supplied by the imagination may reflect the capacities, memories, expectations, and distortions of the receiver.

A disciplined imagination does not become empty. It becomes transparent enough to shape images without falsely claiming ownership of everything passing through it.
