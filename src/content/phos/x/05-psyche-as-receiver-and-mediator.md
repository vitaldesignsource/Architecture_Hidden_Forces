Psychē, the soul, is the living mediator between spirit and embodiment. It receives illumination from higher orders, impressions from the sensible world, movements from the astral body, rhythms from the etheric body, and conditions from physical existence. It does not merely transmit these influences unchanged. It interprets, remembers, values, imagines, chooses, and gives them interior significance.

The soul is receptive but not passive. Reception is an activity requiring selection, proportion, and assimilation. Just as the eye must be capable of receiving light without being destroyed by it, the soul must possess sufficient structure to receive spiritual, psychic, and sensory forces without losing its centre.

## Downward and upward

The soul also mediates downward. Intention becomes image, image becomes psychic motion, psychic motion engages etheric vitality, and vitality supports bodily action. It mediates upward when bodily experience becomes sensation, sensation becomes image, image becomes reflection, and reflection becomes knowledge or spiritual understanding.

Psychē therefore occupies neither a purely spiritual nor a merely material position. It is the middle through which higher and lower orders encounter one another. Its dignity lies in mediation.
