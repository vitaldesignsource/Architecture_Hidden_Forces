Metals possess distinctive relationships with light, heat, electricity, magnetism, oxidation, and conductivity because of their physical structures and electronic properties. Their brilliance arises largely from how their electrons interact with incident light.

Alchemy and astrology associated metals with planetary powers: gold with the Sun, silver with the Moon, iron with Mars, copper with Venus, tin with Jupiter, lead with Saturn, and mercury with Mercury. These are not modern chemical classifications, and the system does not present them as such. Nor are they merely historical symbolism: the sympathetic relation between a planet and its metal is affirmed as a real correspondence upon the formative and celestial planes — one that Lili Kolisko's capillary-dynamolysis experiments attempted to register in metallic salt solutions during planetary events.

Within the system, metal is captured solar quality because it gives radiance durable material expression. It can reflect, conduct, resist, contain, and be transformed through heat.

Gold becomes solar not because it contains literal sunlight, but because its colour, durability, rarity, and resistance to corrosion make it an exceptional vessel of solar quality — a real participation in the solar, of which its symbolism is the recognition rather than the invention.
