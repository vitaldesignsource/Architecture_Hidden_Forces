Smoke, mist, and cloud make the atmosphere visible by scattering and absorbing light. They reveal beams that would otherwise pass through clear air without becoming perceptible from the side.

Each creates a different veil. Smoke carries the residue of combustion and therefore joins illumination to sacrifice, destruction, offering, and transformation. Mist softens distance and creates ambiguity. Cloud conceals the source while distributing its radiance across a wider field.

Veiled radiance demonstrates that obscuration can become revelatory. The medium hides the source but makes the path of light visible.

Within the psyche, comparable veils arise through memory, emotion, symbolism, and imagination. They may distort the current, but they may also give invisible influence an image through which consciousness can encounter it.
