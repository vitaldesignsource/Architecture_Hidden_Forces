The triangle gives the first stable plane. Three points establish a bounded surface, allowing unity to differentiate into relation without collapsing into disorder. It therefore represents the emergence of structured multiplicity.

In Western alchemy, the upright triangle traditionally signifies fire, while the downward-pointing triangle signifies water. This distinction should be preserved. "Descending fire" is not automatically represented by changing fire into the water triangle. It is better shown as an upright fiery triangle receiving a ray from above, or as divine flame descending through successive triangular levels.

The upward triangle expresses aspiration, combustion, ascent, and concentration toward a summit. Descending fire expresses the opposite movement of the same spiritual power: illumination entering lower worlds, the divine spark entering a vessel, or spirit transmitting its formative impulse into matter.

## Where the movements meet

When upward aspiration and downward illumination meet, they form a six-pointed figure. This can represent the conjunction of fire and water, above and below, spirit and matter, procession and return. Yet the hexagram carries historically particular Jewish, Islamic, alchemical, and magical meanings that should not be indiscriminately collapsed.

Within [[ii-22|Ignisophia]], the triangle is the geometry of directed transformation. Its three vertices can represent source, medium, and receiver; spirit, soul, and body; or ignition, purification, and consecration. Descending fire is truthful only when the receiving architecture can hold its intensity without inflation or fracture.
