The mandorla is an almond-shaped aureole surrounding the entire body rather than only the head. It often appears around Christ in Majesty, the Transfiguration, the Ascension, or the Virgin in glory. Its shape is closely related to [[xvii-6|the vesica]] formed by intersecting circles.

The halo marks sanctified consciousness; the mandorla marks a sanctified mode of total embodiment. The entire person has entered a luminous field. Body, soul, and spirit appear within a single transfigured atmosphere.

A mandorla can contain concentric bands of colour, stars, clouds, angels, or increasingly dark zones. In some Eastern Christian representations, darkness near the centre indicates divine transcendence beyond ordinary sight - the luminous darkness into which vision cannot penetrate.

## Radiance and threshold

The mandorla is consequently both radiance and threshold. It reveals the holy figure while separating its mode of existence from ordinary space. The viewer sees a body that remains recognisable but is no longer governed solely by common material conditions.

Within this encyclopaedia, the mandorla is among the strongest symbols of the fully activated subtle body. It represents the etheric, astral, and spiritual orders coherently gathered around an embodied centre. It is not escape from form but the disclosure of form's higher capacity.
