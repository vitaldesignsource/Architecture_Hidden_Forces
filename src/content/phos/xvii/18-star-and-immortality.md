The star is a distant but enduring point of light. Because stars appear to preserve their courses beyond the span of human life, they became natural symbols of permanence, divine order, heavenly beings, ancestors, and the transfigured dead.

Egyptian mortuary traditions associated the deceased king with [[vii-14|imperishable circumpolar stars]]. Mesopotamian iconography used stars as divine signs. Jewish, Christian, Islamic, Iranian, Indian, and other traditions developed distinct stellar symbolisms involving guidance, destiny, angels, celestial souls, or divine manifestation.

The number of points changes the star's meaning. Five-pointed, six-pointed, seven-pointed, eight-pointed, and twelve-pointed stars belong to different geometrical and historical systems. They should not be treated as interchangeable versions of a generic emblem.

## Orientation when certainty withdraws

Within the system, the star represents concentrated identity surviving within a greater celestial order. Unlike the sun, which dominates the day, the star requires darkness to become visible. It is therefore a sign of enduring orientation when larger certainty has withdrawn.

The star is also [[xvii-1|the point of light]] expanded into directional rays. It joins centre and emanation within one figure: a stable core expressing itself through ordered extension.
