The lamp is light housed within an intentional vessel. Unlike the sun, it is local, tended, and dependent upon fuel. Its smallness is part of its meaning: limited light can still provide orientation within immense darkness.

The lamp contains a complete architecture. The vessel stores fuel. The wick draws that fuel upward. The flame transforms it. The surrounding glass or niche protects and directs the radiance. The keeper maintains the conditions under which illumination continues - the configuration set out in [[xv-77|the Qur'anic Light Verse]].

The lamp therefore symbolises cultivated consciousness. Inner guidance does not necessarily arrive as overwhelming revelation. It may be a quiet but persistent light sufficient to reveal the next step.

## Tradition as a tended flame

The lamp also represents tradition. A teaching can preserve fire through long periods of darkness, carrying a luminous current across generations. Yet transmission requires replenishment. A lamp without fuel becomes an empty relic; fuel without ignition remains unrealised potential.

Within this system, the lamp is the soul prepared to bear spiritual fire. The oil is accumulated vitality and devotion. The wick is directed attention. The flame is awakened intelligence. The light surrounding it is meaning communicated through the psychic and etheric field.
