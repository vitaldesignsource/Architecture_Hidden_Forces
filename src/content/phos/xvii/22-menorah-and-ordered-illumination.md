The Temple menorah is a seven-branched lampstand described in Exodus as being made from pure gold according to a pattern revealed to Moses. Its lamps used consecrated olive oil and were arranged to illuminate the sacred space before it.

Its form is simultaneously arboreal and luminous. Branches, cups, almond blossoms, central shaft, oil, wick, and flame unite the Tree of Life with ordered illumination. Light is distributed into plurality while remaining supported by one golden body.

Later Jewish interpretations associated the menorah with wisdom, creation, heavenly order, and the celestial lights. Some [Second Temple-period interpretations](https://www.thetorah.com/article/menorah-its-branches-and-their-cosmic-significance) connected its seven lamps with [[vii-17|the seven classical planetary powers]]. These interpretations belong to particular historical settings and should not replace the menorah's primary identity as a sacred Jewish ritual object.

## One body, seven flames

The central shaft provides the axis from which the branches proceed. Multiplicity remains balanced around a sustaining centre. The flames are separate but participate in one consecrated architecture.

Within the system, the menorah represents ordered differentiation. The seven lamps may correspond analogically to planetary currents, rays, faculties, or luminous qualities, while the central support represents the law holding them in right relation.
