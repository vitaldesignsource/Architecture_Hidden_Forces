The winged solar disc combines central radiance with lateral movement. The disc signifies solar unity; the wings signify extension, protection, elevation, speed, and the ability of celestial power to traverse space.

In ancient Egypt the winged disc became associated particularly with Horus of Behdet and could appear above temple entrances as a protective and royal emblem; [the Edfu texts](https://www.attalus.org/egypt/horus_of_behutet.html) narrate his flight as the winged disk against the enemies of Re. Related but distinct winged solar forms also appeared across the ancient Near East, including Anatolia, Mesopotamia, and Iran. The symbol broadly carried associations of divinity, kingship, and heavenly protection.

The wings prevent the solar centre from becoming static. Light moves, overshadows, guards, and arrives. The symbol is therefore less a picture of the astronomical sun than an emblem of mobile solar authority.

## The centre in motion

Within [[ii-22|Ignisophia]], the winged disc represents the Inner Sun entering directed motion. The centre preserves identity; the two wings balance extension. One wing may signify procession and the other return, or mercy and severity, while the disc maintains their unity.

It is an excellent emblem for a power that remains centrally coherent while projecting influence across multiple domains.
