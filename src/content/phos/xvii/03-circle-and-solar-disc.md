The circle expresses unity, continuity, recurrence, and completeness. Every point on its circumference stands in equal relation to the centre. It is therefore a natural geometry for solar power: one centre communicating radiance equally in every direction.

The solar disc represents both the visible sun and the principle of centralised illumination. Unlike the point, the disc has acquired extension. Unlike the sphere, it remains a frontal image - a face of radiance presented to the observer.

Its boundary does not necessarily signify imprisonment. The circumference can mark the coherent limit through which the centre becomes perceptible. A source without any boundary is unrepresentable; the disc gives it a form capable of entering iconography.

## One disc, particular theologies

In Egypt the solar disc could represent Re, Aten, or another particular solar manifestation. Under Akhenaten, [[xv-4|Aten's disc]] was depicted with rays ending in hands, emphasising the sun's direct bestowal of life. This historical form should not be reduced to a generic "sun symbol", because its theology concerns a specific relation among solar deity, royal mediation, and living creation.

Within this system, the circle is the first stable luminous field around a centre. It represents unity preserved within extension. The centre is the fontal source; the circumference is the lawful limit of its present manifestation; the radius is the relation joining every portion of the field to its origin.
