The halo is a localised field of radiance surrounding the head. It indicates that consciousness has become permeated by a sacred or elevated power. The individual is not merely looking toward light; light has become visibly associated with the individual's presence.

Halos developed across several artistic traditions, including Greco-Roman, Iranian, Buddhist, Hindu, and Christian art. Their meanings vary. They may indicate divinity, royalty, sainthood, awakened consciousness, cosmic glory, or transfigured identity.

In Christian iconography the halo commonly marks sanctity, while a cruciform halo is particularly associated with Christ. Gold frequently represents a heavenly mode of presence rather than ordinary environmental illumination; [a reference overview](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/aureole-nimbus) distinguishes the nimbus around the head from the aureole surrounding the whole figure.

## Convention and perception

Within this system, the halo is not necessarily an artistic invention without experiential foundation. Historical accounts of [[xi-10|radiance surrounding saints, sages, rulers, and living beings]] may reflect visionary perception, etheric or astral luminosity, symbolic convention, artistic theology, or combinations of these.

The halo's position is significant. It surrounds the centre of perception and meaning. It is the outward appearance of an inward order that has become sufficiently coherent to radiate beyond its ordinary boundary.
