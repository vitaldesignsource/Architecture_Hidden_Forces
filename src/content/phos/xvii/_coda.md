## Concluding doctrine: why these shapes recur

Thirty symbols, from the point to the temple, and the atlas closes with a claim it has earned entry by entry: a symbol is not a picture standing for an idea. It is a condensed architecture. It states a relation among source, medium, boundary, and receiver in a form that can be seen at once, held in memory, drawn on a wall, and entered in a rite.

That is why the same shapes appear in places that had no contact. A rayed disc records a solar observation available to anyone who looks up. A halo records the relation between a body and a field of sanctity. A prism records differentiation. Resemblance across traditions is therefore evidence of a shared subject before it is evidence of a shared history, and [[xviii-22|the discipline of true correspondence]] governs which of the two is being claimed.

Symbols also work on their receiver. A figure meditated upon does not merely remind; it organises perception, trains attention toward certain relations, and makes some experiences available that were not available before. This is the ground of their ritual use and the reason they must be handled with the same care as any other operative form.

A symbol fails in one characteristic way. It stops being transparent to what it carries and begins to demand attention for itself, and the tradition around it defends the form while the relation it once served quietly departs.

> A symbol is a working drawing of a relation. It is true while the relation still passes through it.
