Human colour vision begins when light reaching the retina is absorbed by photopigments. Rods are highly sensitive and support vision under dim conditions but provide limited colour information. Cones support daylight vision, visual acuity, and colour discrimination.

Most humans possess three classes of cones conventionally labelled S, M, and L according to their relative sensitivity to shorter, medium, and longer visible wavelengths. No single cone directly reports a perceived colour. Colour emerges through comparisons among cone responses and further processing in retinal and cortical pathways, as [the NIH's Bookshelf edition of Purves's *Neuroscience*](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK11059/) sets out.

## Metamers and context

The same spectral distribution can appear different under different backgrounds, adaptation states, and illumination. Conversely, physically different spectra can sometimes produce the same perceived colour; these matches are called metamers. Colour is therefore relational - not a simple label carried unchanged by an isolated photon.

This does not mean colour is unreal. [[iv-15|Colour]] is a real perceptual achievement arising through the relation among spectrum, object, illumination, eye, nervous system, and context. Its reality is participatory rather than reducible to a single external property.

Symbolic colour similarly arises through relations among perceptual disposition, bodily response, natural environment, culture, memory, ritual context, and subtle experience. Physical colour science cannot fully explain why a colour acquires a sacred meaning, but it shows that relational emergence does not make a quality arbitrary.

In Morphaithēr, [[ii-9|tattvic coloration]] may be conceived as a deeper formative counterpart to this principle. The received quality emerges through the interaction of force and receiver. The correspondence becomes irresponsible only if tattvas are falsely identified with the three cone classes or assigned wavelengths without evidence.
