Light is a transverse electromagnetic wave. Its electric field oscillates perpendicular to the direction of propagation. Polarisation describes the orientation and temporal pattern of that oscillation.

In linearly polarised light, the electric field oscillates along a fixed axis. In circular polarisation, its direction rotates while maintaining constant magnitude. Elliptical polarisation is the more general rotating form. Unpolarised light contains changing or mixed orientations without a stable relation over time.

Polarising materials transmit certain field orientations more readily than others. Reflection, scattering, birefringent crystals, and magnetic or electrical effects can also alter polarisation. The blue sky is partly polarised because sunlight is scattered by atmospheric molecules, as [OpenStax describes](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/1-7-polarization).

## Orientation as a quality

Polarisation demonstrates that two beams can share wavelength and intensity while possessing different internal orientations. Light therefore cannot be described adequately by brightness and colour alone. Directional structure matters.

Within the encyclopaedia, polarisation provides a refined analogy for [[x-9|orientation of consciousness]]. Two individuals may receive the same general influence but align with different aspects of it. A ritual boundary, symbol, vow, or established intention may act like a polarising filter, admitting compatible currents while suppressing others.

Again, the correspondence is structural. There is no scientific basis for assigning ordinary spiritual attitudes literal electromagnetic polarisation unless it is independently measured. But polarisation shows clearly that luminous quality includes organised orientation, not merely magnitude.
