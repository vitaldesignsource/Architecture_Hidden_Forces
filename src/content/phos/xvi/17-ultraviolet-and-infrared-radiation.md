Visible light is a small portion of the electromagnetic spectrum. Infrared radiation has longer wavelengths than visible red, while ultraviolet has shorter wavelengths than visible violet. Human eyes ordinarily detect neither, yet both have powerful physical and biological effects.

Infrared is strongly associated with thermal radiation, although infrared and heat are not identical concepts. Molecules emit and absorb infrared according to vibrational and rotational states. Infrared instruments reveal warm bodies, molecular clouds, plant stress, atmospheric composition, and celestial objects hidden by visible dust.

Ultraviolet photons possess more energy than visible photons. UV participates in vitamin D production but can also damage DNA, age skin, injure eyes, and increase cancer risk. Different UV bands penetrate matter and biological tissues differently.

## The window and the spectrum

Their invisibility offers a basic lesson: absence from unaided perception is not absence from reality. Human senses reveal a biologically selected window rather than the total field of physical radiation. Instruments extend perception by converting invisible frequencies into visible images, audible signals, or numerical measurements. [NASA's tour of the electromagnetic spectrum](https://science.nasa.gov/learn/heat/resource/nasa-space-place-explore-the-electromagnetic-spectrum/) presents phenomena no human eye could directly see.

This provides a legitimate precedent for epistemic humility regarding subtle reality. However, "science once discovered invisible light" does not prove every proposed invisible field. It shows only that unaided sensory invisibility is not a sufficient argument against existence. Further evidence must come from the particular phenomenon under consideration.
