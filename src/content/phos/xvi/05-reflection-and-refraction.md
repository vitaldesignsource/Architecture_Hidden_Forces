Reflection occurs when light encounters a boundary and returns into the original medium. For an ideal smooth surface, the angle of reflection equals the angle of incidence, with both measured from a line perpendicular to the surface. Rough surfaces also reflect light, but their microscopic variations scatter it in many directions.

A [[iii-14|mirror]] does not contain the object it reveals. It establishes a geometrically ordered relation through which light originating from or reflected by the object reaches the observer along redirected paths. The apparent image occupies a virtual location because the visual system extends the arriving rays backward.

## Snell's law

[[iii-21|Refraction]] occurs when light changes direction while entering a medium in which its phase velocity differs. Snell's law describes this relation.

> n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂

Here n₁ and n₂ are the refractive indices of the media, while θ₁ and θ₂ are the incident and refracted angles. Light bends toward the normal when it enters a medium with a higher refractive index and generally bends away when entering one with a lower index, as [OpenStax sets out](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/1-3-refraction).

Refraction demonstrates that a change in appearance does not necessarily mean the source has changed. A submerged object appears displaced or bent because its light has crossed a boundary. Distortion is produced by transmission through unlike media.

## An analogy, stated as one

This gives [[viii-16|tattvic refraction]] a scientifically grounded analogy. If consciousness, astral substance, etheric organisation, and physical sensation are genuinely different media, then a force passing between them may change its mode of expression. A spiritual reality might become an image in the astral field, an affective condition in the psyche, and a bodily sensation in the nervous system. These expressions would not be identical, but neither would they necessarily be unrelated.

The responsible claim is structural: physical refraction shows how transmission across media can alter appearance without abolishing continuity. It does not independently prove the existence of metaphysical media.
