Living cells emit extremely faint optical radiation known as ultraweak photon emission, or UPE. "Biophoton" is also used, although that word sometimes carries theoretical claims beyond the established observation.

UPE is much weaker than ordinary bioluminescence and normally far below unaided visual detection. A substantial portion appears to arise through oxidative metabolic reactions. Reactive oxygen species and excited molecular products can release photons as they return to lower-energy states. Emission can change with stress, injury, temperature, inflammation, metabolic activity, and antioxidant conditions; [a scientific review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24674863/) sets out the role of reactive oxygen species in the phenomenon.

The phenomenon is real and instrumentally measurable. What remains uncertain is its biological function. Some researchers have proposed that UPE may participate in cellular signalling, coordination, health assessment, or long-range regulation. These possibilities remain under investigation. Detectable emission alone does not establish that cells use the photons as a primary communication system.

## Two things kept distinct

Biophotons are not automatically [[ix-18|the etheric body]]. The etheric body, as understood in this encyclopaedia, is a vital-formative field organising growth, rhythm, repair, development, and bodily integrity on a subtle plane. UPE is a physical photon emission associated strongly with cellular chemistry. The two should not be conflated.

Neither should they be declared unrelated by definition. If an etheric organism interacts with physical metabolism, photon emission could conceivably be one secondary physical expression of that interaction. At present, science has not demonstrated this relation. The responsible position is therefore open but exact: UPE exists; its dominant known mechanisms are biochemical; wider signalling functions remain unsettled; and its connection to the etheric body has not been scientifically established.

This distinction protects both fields. Biophoton research is not burdened with proving an entire subtle-body ontology, while the etheric body is not reduced to one extremely faint metabolic by-product.
