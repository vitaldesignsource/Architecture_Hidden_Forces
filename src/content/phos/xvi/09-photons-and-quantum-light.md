Quantum theory revealed that light exchanges energy in discrete quantities. Max Planck introduced energy quanta in his treatment of thermal radiation, and Albert Einstein used the quantum concept to explain the photoelectric effect. The energy of a photon is

> E = hf

where E is energy, h is Planck's constant, and f is frequency. Its momentum can be expressed as

> p = h / λ

where λ is wavelength.

## What a photon is

A photon is not best imagined as a microscopic glowing marble. It is a quantum excitation of the electromagnetic field. Under some experimental arrangements, light produces discrete localised detections. Under others, its probability amplitudes evolve and interfere in ways described by wave mathematics.

Photons can be emitted and absorbed by atoms, molecules, nuclei, and other quantum systems. Their energies correspond to changes between permitted states. Light is therefore exchanged according to the structure of the emitter and receiver. Matter does not absorb arbitrary quantities indiscriminately; interaction depends upon resonance with allowed transitions.

This provides a powerful - but limited - analogy for spiritual reception. A [[v-28|receiver]] may respond only to influences compatible with its structure. Transformation can occur when the incoming power matches a possible transition. Yet a "spiritual photon" is not a recognised scientific entity, and quantum terminology should not be used to create false precision around metaphysical claims.

Quantum optics gives a magnificent account of physical light. It does not define every possible meaning of luminosity, consciousness, or spiritual presence.
