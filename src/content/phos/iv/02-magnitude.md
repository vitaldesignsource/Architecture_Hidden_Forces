Magnitude is the total scale, extent, or consequential reach of a luminous phenomenon. Whereas intensity concerns strength at a particular point, magnitude concerns the greatness of the event considered as a whole.

A narrow beam may possess high intensity but limited magnitude. A faint dawn may possess relatively low local intensity while extending across an entire horizon. A quiet idea may enter consciousness subtly yet possess enormous historical magnitude because of the transformations it eventually generates.

Magnitude may therefore be spatial, temporal, psychic, cultural, or ontological. It measures how much of the field becomes involved.

The magnitude of a light should not be judged only by its immediate appearance. Some lights reveal their greatness through duration, propagation, or the forms they make possible.
