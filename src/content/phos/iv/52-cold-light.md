Cold light physically refers to luminescence that produces visible illumination with relatively little accompanying heat compared with incandescence. Fluorescence, phosphorescence, chemiluminescence, and bioluminescence can produce forms of cold light.

Symbolically, cold light represents clarity without warmth, perception without emotional participation, or intelligence separated from desire.

This can be beneficial where precision and detachment are needed. It becomes sterile when illumination fails to animate, nourish, or enter compassionate relation with life.

The *cold flame* is a useful paradox within the system: fire freed from ordinary combustion, retaining radiance and transformative symbolism without destructive heat.

Cold light distinguishes illumination from burning.
