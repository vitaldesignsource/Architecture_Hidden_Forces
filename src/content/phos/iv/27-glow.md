A glow is a soft, continuous luminosity whose precise source or boundary may be difficult to distinguish. It seems to inhabit a surface, atmosphere, or body rather than travel from it in sharply defined rays.

Physically, a glow may arise from heated matter, luminescent material, scattered light, reflected illumination, or atmospheric diffusion. Phenomenologically, it creates an impression of interiority: the object appears lit from within even when the actual mechanism is external.

Within the system, glow represents light that has entered and partially saturated a vessel. It is less directional than a beam and less expansive than radiance. A glow suggests retained luminosity — light held closely enough to form that the form itself appears to participate in illumination.
