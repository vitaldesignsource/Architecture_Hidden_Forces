Luminous density is the concentration of luminous activity within a region, vessel, image, or experience. It should not be confused indiscriminately with the technical optical density of a material, which concerns attenuation and transmission.

A light may appear dense when its effects are closely concentrated, layered, or substantial. Dense symbolic light carries many meanings within a compact form. Dense psychic luminosity may make an image feel unusually present or charged.

## Toward opacity

Density can increase formative power because more relation is gathered into a smaller field. It can also make the light difficult to receive or interpret.

When density becomes excessive, luminosity approaches opacity. The current contains so much compressed significance or force that the receiver cannot distinguish its parts.

Dense light requires time, proportion, and gradual unfolding.
