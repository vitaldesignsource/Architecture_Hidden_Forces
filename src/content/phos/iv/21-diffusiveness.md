Diffusiveness is the tendency of light to spread across a field rather than remain concentrated along a narrow path.

Diffuse light softens boundaries, reduces hard contrast, and creates a common atmosphere. It is well suited to general orientation, nourishment, and the illumination of many forms simultaneously.

Within psychic and cultural fields, diffusiveness allows a mood, image, value, or idea to circulate beyond its original point of emergence. The influence becomes environmental.

Diffusiveness increases breadth while decreasing local concentration. It may establish a supportive atmosphere without producing deep transformation in any one place.

A mature luminous architecture balances diffusion and concentration. Fields nourish broadly; beams act precisely.
