Truthfulness is the degree to which light faithfully discloses its source, object, context, and limitations.

Truthful light does not necessarily reveal everything. It reveals without pretending that its partial field is complete. It preserves relevant distinctions, exposes the role of mediation, and does not disguise reflection as source.

## What cannot guarantee it

Intensity cannot guarantee truthfulness. Purity cannot guarantee it. Transparency, coherence, unity, and intelligence cannot guarantee it independently. A false system may possess all the appearance of luminous order.

Truthfulness requires fidelity among source, transmission, appearance, interpretation, and consequence. It must remain answerable to what is actually present.

The most truthful light reveals not only the object but also the conditions under which the object has appeared.
