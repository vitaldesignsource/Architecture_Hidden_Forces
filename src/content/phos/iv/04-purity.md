Purity is the degree to which a luminous current remains free from admixture with other currents. Physically, purity may refer to a narrow spectral composition or freedom from unwanted contamination. Metaphysically, it concerns simplicity of intention, continuity of source, and freedom from conflicting motives.

## Not superiority

Purity must not be confused with superiority. Mixed light can be beautiful, fertile, and necessary. White daylight itself contains a multiplicity of wavelengths. Biological life depends upon complex mixtures rather than absolute isolation.

Purity becomes important when the identity of a current must be recognised. It allows the receiver to determine what belongs to the originating light and what has been introduced by the medium.

A pure light may still be too intense, poorly directed, or unfit for a particular vessel. Purity is one quality among many, not a final judgment.
