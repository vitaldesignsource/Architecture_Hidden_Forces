White light is perceived when a suitable combination of visible wavelengths stimulates the visual system without producing one dominant hue. It is not the absence of colour but a condition in which chromatic multiplicity is integrated.

A prism can reveal the spectral differentiation concealed within white light. White therefore symbolises unity capable of division without losing its originating coherence.

Metaphysically, white light may represent synthesis, purification, totality, clarity, or the gathering of many qualities into one luminous field.

## Not superior to colour

White light should not be treated as morally superior to coloured light. Differentiation is necessary for manifestation. White contains; colour articulates.

The return to white is meaningful only when it preserves rather than erases the knowledge acquired through the spectrum.
