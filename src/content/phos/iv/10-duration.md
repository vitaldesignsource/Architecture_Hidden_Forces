Duration is the length of time during which a luminous phenomenon remains active or perceptible. It differs from stability. A light may remain stable but brief, or unstable but persistent.

A flash can reveal an entire landscape for an instant. A steady lamp permits examination. A recurring light may disappear and return through rhythm rather than remaining continuously present.

Duration affects what the receiver can accomplish. Brief illumination may initiate recognition, but sustained illumination allows study, orientation, integration, and transformation.

The value of a light is not determined solely by how long it lasts. Some revelations must be brief because the receiver cannot yet sustain them. Others require duration to penetrate habit and become embodied.

Time is one of the principal media of illumination.
