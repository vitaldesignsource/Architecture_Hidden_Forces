Iridescence is a changing display of colour produced when light interacts with fine structures, thin films, layered surfaces, or diffraction patterns. The visible colours shift according to the angle of illumination and observation.

The interference that produces it, between reflections from the two faces of a film a wavelength thick, is explained in [Britannica's treatment of thin-film interference](https://www.britannica.com/science/light/Thin-film-interference).

Soap films, certain shells, feathers, insect wings, minerals, and manufactured surfaces can appear iridescent.

Iridescence demonstrates that colour may arise through structure and relation rather than pigment alone. The same surface produces different appearances as the geometry among source, object, and observer changes.

Within the metaphysical architecture, iridescence symbolises plural disclosure. One form contains several potential appearances, each becoming visible from a particular relation. No single angle exhausts the form.
