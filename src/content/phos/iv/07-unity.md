Unity is the degree to which a luminous phenomenon possesses one governing identity while remaining capable of internal differentiation.

Unity is not uniformity. A unified light may contain many colours, rhythms, functions, and expressions. Its multiplicity remains related to an intelligible centre.

Within [[i-13|the One Light and its many participations]], unity means that differentiated appearances retain a real relation to their source. Diversity does not necessarily indicate fragmentation.

In the soul, unity occurs when memory, desire, thought, body, and purpose enter a shared orientation without losing their proper distinctions. In a system, unity is achieved when many doctrines participate in one architecture rather than being forced into artificial sameness.

True unity coordinates difference. False unity suppresses it.
