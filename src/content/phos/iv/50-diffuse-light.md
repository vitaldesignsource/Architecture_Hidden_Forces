Diffuse light arrives from many directions rather than one sharply defined source. It softens shadows, reduces contrast, and establishes broad, even illumination.

Cloud cover, reflective surfaces, atmospheric scattering, and large luminous areas can create diffuse conditions. Such light reveals overall form gently but may conceal fine surface relief that directional shadows would expose.

Psychically, diffuse light resembles mood or general awareness. It illuminates the field without concentrating upon one object.

Diffuse light is excellent for inclusion and atmosphere. It becomes insufficient when precise discrimination, deep penetration, or directed transformation is required.
