A shimmer is a soft, shifting play of light produced through small and rapid changes in reflection, refraction, surface position, or atmospheric condition.

Water shimmers because its moving surface continually redirects light. Fabric, scales, leaves, heat haze, and finely textured material can produce similar effects.

Shimmer is relational and unstable. It belongs neither entirely to the source nor entirely to the object. It emerges through movement among light, surface, medium, and observer.

Within psychic experience, shimmer suggests an image that remains alive, mutable, and not yet fixed. It reveals through variation. Unlike the flash, which interrupts, shimmer sustains a delicate uncertainty.
