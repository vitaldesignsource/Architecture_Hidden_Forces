A gleam is a concentrated brightness appearing upon a surface. It is usually sharper and more localised than a glow.

Metal, polished stone, water, eyes, glass, and wet leaves gleam because they return light selectively toward the observer. A gleam therefore reveals both the presence of illumination and the reflective character of the surface.

Symbolically, a gleam is the sudden visibility of concealed quality. It may be the gleam of intelligence in an expression, the gleam of possibility within darkness, or the first visible sign that a form contains more than its exterior initially disclosed.

A gleam belongs to the boundary where light and surface meet.
