Divisibility is the capacity of light to be separated, distributed, differentiated, or apportioned.

A beam may be divided. White light may be separated into a spectrum. Radiance may reach countless surfaces, each receiving only a portion of what has been emitted.

Metaphysically, divisibility explains how one source can support many participations. The light becomes distributed without requiring the source to be divided into unrelated pieces.

## Division and fragmentation

Division can reveal concealed structure. The prism separates colours already implicated within the incoming light. Analysis similarly divides a complex idea so that its internal relations can be examined.

Yet division can also become fragmentation. When parts lose relation to their origin and to one another, differentiation ceases to serve understanding.

Divisibility is healthy when plurality remains capable of recollecting unity.
