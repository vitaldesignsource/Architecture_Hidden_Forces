The qualities of light are not separate substances. They are dimensions through which a luminous event can be examined. Every light possesses a particular profile: it may be intense but unclear, subtle but penetrating, coherent but short-lived, colourful but untruthful, or diffuse yet profoundly generative.

A luminous quality may originate in the source, arise through the medium, be imposed by the vessel, or appear only in the experience of the receiver. This distinction is essential. A golden atmosphere may belong to the emitted spectrum, the material through which the light passes, the surface reflecting it, the condition of the eye, or the symbolic interpretation of consciousness.

The qualities that follow therefore describe the complete relationship among source, transmission, medium, form, and reception.
