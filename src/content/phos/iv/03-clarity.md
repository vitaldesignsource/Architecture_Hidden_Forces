Clarity is the capacity of light to reveal distinctions without unnecessary confusion. Clear light allows edges, proportions, relationships, and differences to become perceptible.

Clarity depends upon more than brightness. It requires an adequately ordered source, a medium free from excessive interference, a form capable of reflecting meaningful differences, and a receiver able to discriminate among them.

Psychic clarity appears when emotion, memory, and desire no longer distort the entire field of perception. Noetic clarity appears when concepts acquire definite relations and cease to blur into one another.

Clarity is not the absence of mystery. It is the ability to recognise what has genuinely become available while acknowledging what remains concealed.
