Judgement is the discriminating operation of light. When light enters an architecture, it reveals whether the parts are properly related, whether the form corresponds to its claimed purpose, and whether the structure can transmit the force it professes to embody.

Judgement need not be imagined primarily as a sentence pronounced from outside. Much of it is intrinsic. A coherent structure becomes more articulate in greater light; a false structure reveals contradiction. An action encounters its consequences, a claim meets reality, and a vessel is tested by the force it attempts to contain.

## Judgement as disclosure

Divine judgement is therefore also disclosure. It exposes the actual quality of participation. What belongs to the light becomes more capable of receiving it; what depends upon concealment experiences illumination as crisis. The same radiance can appear consoling to one structure and consuming to another.
