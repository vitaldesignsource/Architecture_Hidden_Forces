Dissolution is the loosening of a form whose organising relations have reached their limit. The elements, forces, memories, and energies held within the structure begin to separate. This may occur through death, decay, crisis, initiation, psychological breakdown, institutional collapse, ritual unbinding, or alchemical decomposition.

Dissolution is frightening because identity becomes attached to continuity of form. Yet no transformation is possible if every existing arrangement must remain intact. Some architectures can be repaired; others must release what they contain. Wisdom lies in discerning the difference.

## Not automatically liberation

Dissolution is not automatically liberation. When a form collapses without adequate containment, its forces may disperse chaotically or become captured by another distorted structure. For this reason, solve must remain related to coagula. Release becomes transformative when it prepares the conditions for more truthful embodiment.
