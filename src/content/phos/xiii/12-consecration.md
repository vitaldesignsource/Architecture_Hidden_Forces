Consecration is the operation through which a person, place, object, time, or action is established within a particular sacred relation. Purification makes the vessel available; consecration gives it orientation, purpose, and belonging. What is consecrated becomes a designated point of participation between an earthly form and a spiritual source.

Consecration may involve names, prayers, invocations, gestures, substances, symbols, planetary timings, mediating beings, or repeated ritual acts. These do not operate merely as psychological reminders. Within the system, they can establish actual relations among orders of force when performed with sufficient knowledge, authority, receptivity, and coherence.

## The vessel must correspond

A declaration alone does not guarantee consecration. The vessel must become capable of carrying what has been invoked. A consecrated architecture is truthful when its material construction, symbolic form, ethical use, and spiritual allegiance all correspond.
