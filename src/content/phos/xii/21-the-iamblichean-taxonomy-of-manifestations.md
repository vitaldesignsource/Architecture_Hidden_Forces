In *De Mysteriis*, Iamblichus distinguishes manifestations of gods, archangels, angels, daimons, heroes, archons, and souls. These are not arbitrary visual costumes. Their appearances reflect differences in essence, power, activity, universality, and relation to the material world.

Divine manifestations possess unity, stability, completeness, and an illuminating power that orders the entire field. The gods do not merely stand as luminous objects within a surrounding environment; their presence can encompass, transform, and govern the whole space of manifestation.

Archangels and angels disclose the divine order in progressively more divided and particular ways. Their light remains elevated and ordered, but it is increasingly adapted to mediation and specific operation.

## The lower and mixed orders

Daimonic manifestations are more varied, active, and connected to generation and nature. Heroes incline toward the providential ordering of embodied works and the elevation of human participation. Archons reveal powers of governance, with Iamblichus distinguishing more elevated cosmic rulers from those immersed in material administration. Souls display the greatest mixture, mutability, localisation, and variability.

The effects upon the observer also differ. Higher manifestations purify, elevate, unify, and draw the soul upward. Lower or mixed manifestations may agitate, bind, fascinate, terrify, stimulate, or intensify material and psychic processes. Iamblichus develops this comparative taxonomy in Book II of [*De Mysteriis*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/72815/72815-h/72815-h.htm).

The importance of this taxonomy is methodological. A being is known not merely by what it claims to be, but through the complete signature of its manifestation.
