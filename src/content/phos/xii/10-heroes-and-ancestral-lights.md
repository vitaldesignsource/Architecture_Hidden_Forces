Heroes occupy a threshold between human mortality and greater-than-human presence. Through extraordinary action, sacrifice, suffering, initiation, lineage, or post-mortem transformation, a human life may acquire enduring daimonic or ancestral power.

In ancient cult, heroes were not merely admired fictional characters. They were real deceased presences connected to tombs, cities, battlefields, families, sanctuaries, and local identity. Their continued agency could protect, warn, inspire, heal, demand recognition, or transmit a particular current of force.

## The ancestral field

Ancestral lights are the continuing luminous presences of the dead within lineages and communities. They may act through memory, dream, inherited capacity, ritual, bloodline, place, and subtle relationship. Not every ancestor possesses the same degree of clarity or freedom. Death does not instantly perfect the soul.

The ancestral field may contain wisdom, protection, unfinished obligation, trauma, repetition, and forgotten gifts. Honouring ancestors does not mean submitting indiscriminately to every inherited influence. It means entering conscious relation with the sources through which one's embodied life became possible.

A hero becomes luminous when individual force is no longer confined to individual biography but becomes an enduring power of orientation for those who follow.
