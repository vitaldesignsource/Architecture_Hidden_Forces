*Archōn* means ruler. Different traditions assign archons different places and moral qualities. In some Platonic and theurgic contexts, archons administer cosmic or material regions. In several Gnostic texts, archons become ignorant, imitative, controlling, or obstructive rulers associated with the deficient organisation of the lower cosmos.

These accounts should not be artificially homogenised. Some archontic beings may exercise legitimate cosmic administration. Others may mistake limited jurisdiction for ultimate sovereignty. Mixed illumination arises when a being possesses real power and intelligence but lacks full knowledge of the source.

## Partial light absolutised

An archon may therefore radiate authority, complexity, and splendour without possessing divine completeness. Its light can organise a domain while simultaneously confining consciousness to that domain's limitations.

The archontic error is not always total darkness. It is partial light absolutised. A fragment of order presents itself as the whole; a local ruler claims universal authority; a necessary boundary becomes imprisonment.

Archons demonstrate why real power, real knowledge, and real luminosity are insufficient by themselves to establish divinity.
