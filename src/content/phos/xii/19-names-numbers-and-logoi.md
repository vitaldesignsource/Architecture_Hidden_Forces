A sacred name is not merely a label attached to a being. It is a structured means of address, relation, invocation, and participation. Names may carry history, office, power, rhythm, divine authorisation, and the specific form through which a being becomes accessible.

Numbers express intelligible proportion. One reveals unity, two relation and polarity, three procession and mediation, four stability and manifested order, and so forth. These meanings are not arbitrary additions to quantity. Number is one of the ways intelligible structure enters form.

Logoi are formative reasons, principles, or articulated expressions through which beings receive intelligibility and characteristic order. A logos is not merely a spoken sentence. It is the inner law or formative articulation according to which a thing becomes what it is.

## Operative, not descriptive

Names identify and open relation. Numbers establish proportion. Logoi articulate formative essence. When coordinated through [[ii-29|Heka]], ritual, symbol, breath, image, timing, and material vessel, they can become operative rather than merely descriptive.

Iamblichus insists that sacred names are not freely interchangeable inventions, because their efficacy belongs to inherited divine and ritual relations rather than ordinary semantic convenience. His defence of sacred names appears in Book VII of [*De Mysteriis*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/72815/72815-h/72815-h.htm).
