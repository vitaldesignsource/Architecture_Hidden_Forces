Cultures have encountered divine powers through the most commanding forms of celestial and elemental luminosity. Solar gods disclose sovereignty, visibility, generation, justice, measure, and the victorious return of light. Dawn deities reveal awakening, passage, renewal, beauty, and the opening of worlds. Fire gods preside over sacrifice, transformation, communication, purification, domestic continuity, and divine presence. Lunar deities govern reflection, reception, fertility, memory, dream, change, concealment, and return. Stellar gods reveal orientation, destiny, immortality, ancestral presence, and the ordered multiplicity of heaven.

These beings should not be dismissed as primitive attempts to explain astronomical objects. The physical Sun, Moon, fire, and stars are visible bodies through which divine powers may become present, worshipped, interpreted, and ritually approached. The deity exceeds the visible vessel while remaining capable of acting through it.

## Names and faces

Different divine names do not necessarily refer to identical beings disguised by different cultures. Nor must every cultural distinction indicate an entirely unrelated power. Some names may address distinct gods; others may reveal local faces, offices, or epiphanies of a more comprehensive divine current.

The Law of Right Relation requires attention to name, place, tradition, ritual lineage, celestial timing, symbol, offering, and the capacity of the receiver. A divine power is not an abstract energy available without relation. It is approached through the forms by which it has chosen, inherited, or been enabled to become present.
