Deity is called Light because the divine is the source of manifestation, intelligibility, life, order, and spiritual vision. Divine light does not merely reveal realities already existing independently of it. It grants their capacity to exist, become intelligible, and participate in goodness.

Yet the ultimate divine source cannot be reduced even to light. If light means something that appears, proceeds, illuminates, or becomes received, then the source must exceed its own manifestation. Deity is simultaneously the font of light and the superessential mystery beyond the distinction between light and darkness.

## Real illumination

Divine light is not electromagnetic radiation, but neither is it merely figurative language. It is real ontological illumination: the procession of divine presence into intelligible, psychic, etheric, and material orders. Physical light participates in this deeper principle without exhausting it.

Different gods and divine names may disclose particular determinations of the inexhaustible source. Their multiplicity need not divide the unity of the divine. Each may be a distinct living power through which the One becomes participable without ceasing to be One.

The divine source remains undiminished while its light proceeds. Its radiance is given without the exhaustion of its origin.
