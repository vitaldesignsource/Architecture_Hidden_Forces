A true correspondence preserves both likeness and difference. It identifies an actual structural relationship without pretending that two terms are historically, physically, or metaphysically identical.

Physical light, noetic illumination, divine glory, astral light, etheric vitality, consciousness, truth, and moral goodness can correspond because each involves some form of manifestation, disclosure, communication, or ordering. But they do not automatically share one substance or mechanism.

Historical similarity is not proof of borrowing. Two cultures may develop solar resurrection imagery because both observe dawn and seasonal regeneration. Conversely, similar words may conceal very different doctrines. *Qi*, *pneuma*, *prāṇa*, *ruaḥ*, aether, and spirit overlap in certain functions but belong to different cosmologies.

## Strong and weak correspondence

The strongest correspondences operate across several dimensions at once. They possess formal likeness, functional similarity, experiential resonance, historical support, and compatibility with the systems being compared. Weak correspondences depend only upon superficial resemblance or an attractive translation.

False equivalence appears when photons are called divine consciousness, quantum entanglement is treated as telepathy, colour wavelengths are assigned directly to moral rank, or every sevenfold system is declared to describe the same seven realities. It also appears when every luminous vision is called revelation or every illness is attributed to spiritual darkness.

The opposite error is reductive isolation: insisting that symbolic, spiritual, and qualitative knowledge is meaningless because it cannot be replaced by numerical measurement. Symbolic perception reveals relations and meanings that measurement was not designed to address.

## Five relationships

The Architecture of Hidden Forces should therefore recognise at least five relationships. Two things may be identical, causally connected, participatory, structurally analogous, or symbolically resonant. These relationships differ in strength and implication. Calling them all "the same" destroys the very architecture comparative inquiry is meant to disclose.

The final rule is exact generosity: take spiritual realities seriously, allow correspondences to reveal unexpected unity, but never purchase unity by erasing distinction. True light clarifies relation. False light makes everything appear interchangeable.
