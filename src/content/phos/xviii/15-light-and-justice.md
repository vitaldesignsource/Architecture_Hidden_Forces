Light is associated with justice because it exposes what concealment protects. The judge brings hidden actions into view, distinguishes true testimony from falsehood, and restores public order.

The Mesopotamian sun god [[xv-11|Utu-Shamash]] was closely connected with justice, truth, divination, and all-seeing perception. The sun crossed the heavens and was therefore imagined as witnessing what human authorities might overlook. His imagery includes radiant shoulders, the solar disc, and implements associated with judgment; [the Ancient Mesopotamian Gods and Goddesses project](https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/amgg/listofdeities/utu/) sets out this connection between illumination and verdict.

## Exposure is not yet justice

But exposure alone is not justice. Light without mercy can humiliate rather than restore. Surveillance can become domination. Dazzling certainty can prevent careful judgment. Justice requires proportion, context, truthful distinction, and the restoration of relation where restoration remains possible.

Within the system, luminous judgment reveals [[xiii-30|whether a form continues to correspond to the force it claims to embody]]. When the architecture becomes false, the light exposes the contradiction and may withdraw from it.

Justice is therefore not spiritual brightness punishing darkness. It is the reestablishment of right relation among beings, actions, consequences, and the greater order.
