Desire directs consciousness toward what is absent, distant, imagined, or potentially attainable. It is therefore ray-like: a movement from the present centre toward an object, condition, or future form.

Light makes the desired object visible, while desire determines what receives attention. Two people within the same illuminated field may perceive entirely different worlds because desire selects, magnifies, and [[x-12|emotionally colours]] what appears important.

In Platonic ascent, desire can rise from beautiful forms toward beauty itself. In Buddhist analysis, craving binds consciousness to cyclic dissatisfaction. In Sufi love, longing draws the seeker toward the divine. In astral theory, desire supplies motion and coloration to psychic images. These are related inquiries, not identical doctrines.

## Orientation, not extinction

Desire becomes luminous when it reveals the deeper good toward which it is genuinely directed. It becomes obscuring when it mistakes a partial object for the complete source of fulfilment - the error that undoes [[xv-36|Pistis Sophia]].

Within the Psychic Flywheel, repeated desire accumulates momentum. It can stabilise aspiration or deepen compulsion. The essential question is not whether desire contains force - it does - but whether its orientation remains truthful.

Purification of desire does not necessarily mean extinction of all movement. It means releasing luminous power from false objects and reorienting it toward right relation.
