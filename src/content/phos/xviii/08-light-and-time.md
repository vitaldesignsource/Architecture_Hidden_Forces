Light measures time and carries it. Day and night, lunar phases, seasons, solstices, equinoxes, and stellar risings supplied humanity's earliest calendars. Living organisms internalised these cycles through [[xvi-22|circadian and seasonal rhythms]].

Because physical light travels at a finite speed, every visible object is perceived as it was a moment ago. Astronomical observation looks deeply into the past. Light is thus a messenger that preserves an interval between event and reception.

## Qualitative hours

Ritual traditions recognise qualitative time. [[xiv-6|Dawn]] is not merely an arbitrary clock position; it is a transition in spectrum, temperature, animal behaviour, hormone regulation, and human consciousness. Noon, sunset, midnight, lunar phases, and planetary configurations likewise establish different physical, psychological, symbolic, and potentially subtle conditions.

This does not mean that every moment is mechanically determined by celestial bodies. Influence may be physical, biological, collective, symbolic, spiritual, or mixed. The sky affects humanity partly through what it does and partly through the meanings by which humans enter relation with it.

Within the system, light makes time perceptible while time differentiates light. The same sun becomes dawn-light, noonday light, declining light, and [[vii-9|the hidden midnight sun]]. Luminous quality is therefore inseparable from phase.
