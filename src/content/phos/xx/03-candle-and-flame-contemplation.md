A flame provides a concentrated image of living luminosity. It has a material base, a transforming zone, a radiant body, and an ascending movement. It consumes fuel yet creates a field beyond the visible boundary of combustion. It is therefore a powerful symbol of mediation between matter, energy, atmosphere, attention, and spirit.

## Setting and safety

Place the candle on a stable, nonflammable surface in a ventilated room, away from fabric, draughts, children, and animals. The flame must never be left unattended. Sit at a comfortable distance and allow the gaze to rest upon it without strain. There is no need to stare without blinking. Observe the darker base, the luminous body, the outer aureole, the movements produced by air, and the surrounding field of reflected light.

## The afterimage

After several minutes, the eyes may be closed and the resulting [[xi-20|afterimage]] observed. The afterimage is an ordinary physiological event, but its lawful transformations can still serve contemplation. Its changing colour, movement, and dissolution demonstrate that sensory reception is active rather than passive. One may then attend to any inner images that arise, carefully distinguishing the retinal trace from imaginal elaboration.

A simple question can guide the practice: what does this flame reveal about the relation between source, fuel, form, atmosphere, and radiance? The goal is not to force a vision but to allow a physical phenomenon to become an exact teacher of luminous architecture.
