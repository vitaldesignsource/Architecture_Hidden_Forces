Stillness is not the absence of activity. It is the reduction of unnecessary motion so that subtler movements become perceptible. When gross bodily movement, scattered thought, and reactive desire settle, the practitioner may begin to notice the quiet luminosity already present within consciousness and the surrounding field.

The practice begins with a stable posture that can be maintained without strain. External light may be dim but should not be so dark that sleep becomes inevitable. Rather than searching the field for extraordinary appearances, notice the basic fact that experience is disclosed at all. Thoughts, sensations, sounds, and images arise within an already illuminated field of awareness - [[x-1|consciousness as illumination]].

## What may arise

As stillness deepens, points, clouds, colours, flashes, geometric traces, bodily radiance, or a diffuse interior brightness may appear. Some will be entoptic or neurological; some imaginal; some may arise through etheric or spiritual perception. Immediate classification is less important than exact observation. Where did the phenomenon appear? Did it possess apparent intelligence? Did it respond to attention? Did it leave clarity or agitation?

Stillness becomes spiritually fruitful when it makes the practitioner more receptive without making the personality passive or suggestible. True receptivity preserves judgment, ethical agency, and the ability to return fully to ordinary life.
