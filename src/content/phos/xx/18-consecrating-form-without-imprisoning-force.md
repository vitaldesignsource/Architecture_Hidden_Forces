Consecration gives light a vessel. Imprisonment begins when the vessel claims ownership of the light and refuses its further development. Every sacred form must therefore remain both stable enough to carry force and permeable enough to release it when its work is complete.

## Before consecrating

Before consecrating an object, symbol, room, text, practice, or institution, determine its purpose and limits. What order of light is it intended to mediate? For whom? Under what conditions? How will it be maintained? How can it be deconsecrated, transformed, or respectfully dissolved? A form established without a lawful means of release easily becomes a reservoir of confused or captive force.

Consecrated form should be revisited periodically. Does it remain alive? Does it still serve the purpose for which it was created? Has its meaning changed? Is it transmitting, distorting, or merely retaining? These questions do not profane sacred things. They protect the distinction between reverence for the light and attachment to its temporary vessel.

## The final practice

The final practice of luminous discernment is therefore neither invocation nor vision. It is the capacity to recognise when light should enter form, how it should dwell there, what relations preserve its truth, and when it must be released.

A mature practitioner does not seek to possess illumination. The practitioner becomes a truthful participant in its passage:

> receiving without grasping, interpreting without reducing, embodying without imprisoning, testing without denying, and returning what was received to the greater life from which it came.
