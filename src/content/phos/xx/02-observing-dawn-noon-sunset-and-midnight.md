The four solar stations are not merely clock times. They express different relations between manifestation and concealment.

At dawn, light emerges gradually from hiddenness. Forms return before the sun itself becomes fully visible. Dawn is therefore suited to observing regeneration, first appearance, hope, and the transition from imaginal or dream consciousness into waking embodiment. The practitioner may watch how darkness becomes colour, how the horizon receives the approaching sun, and how the living environment reorganises itself in response.

Noon represents maximum outward manifestation. Shadows shorten, boundaries become clear, and solar force reaches its most direct expression. Noon practice concerns clarity, exposure, strength, judgment, and the danger of excess. It asks whether the practitioner can remain lucid within intensity without confusing forcefulness with truth.

[[vii-8|Sunset]] reveals withdrawal rather than simple extinction. Colours often become more differentiated as direct solar presence recedes. It is an appropriate time for recollection, gratitude, release, and the gathering of scattered forces. The practitioner observes what remains luminous when the source of visible illumination passes below the horizon.

## The hidden station

Midnight is the hidden solar station. The sun has not ceased to exist; it acts beyond the visible horizon. Midnight contemplation concerns [[vii-9|latent activity]], the underworld passage, primordial memory, and the inner sun that does not depend upon outward brightness. Practice at midnight should ordinarily remain quiet and measured. Exhaustion should not be mistaken for spiritual receptivity, and sleep deprivation should never become a badge of initiation.
