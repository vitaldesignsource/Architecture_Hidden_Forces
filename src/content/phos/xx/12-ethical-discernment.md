Ethics is not an external restriction imposed upon spiritual perception. It is one of the organs by which the quality of light is recognised. A presence that appears brilliant but cultivates cruelty, domination, deception, reckless obedience, or contempt for the vulnerable reveals disorder in its operation or reception.

Ethical discernment asks who benefits, who bears the cost, whether consent is respected, whether boundaries remain possible, and whether authority can be questioned. It examines whether a practice enlarges freedom and responsibility or makes the practitioner dependent upon a teacher, group, being, or system.

## The invasive benevolence

The desire to help can also become invasive. One should not direct subtle influence, ritual force, psychic projection, or spiritual intervention toward another person merely because one believes the intention is benevolent. [[ii-25|Right relation]] requires respect for the other's sovereignty, circumstances, and lawful development.

Ethical failure does not necessarily prove that every perception involved was unreal. A person can receive something genuine and interpret or apply it wrongly. This is precisely why spiritual capacity must include character. Power without ethical formation magnifies the unresolved structure of its bearer.
