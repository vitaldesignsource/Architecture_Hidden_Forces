A personal lexicon gathers the recurring luminous forms of one's own experience. Entries might include golden radiance, blue points, veiled white light, dark luminosity, inner dawn, solar pressure, crystalline atmosphere, peripheral shimmer, etheric warmth, dream stars, or the light surrounding a particular symbol.

Each entry should distinguish description, context, personal association, traditional correspondences, possible mechanism, and observed consequence. If golden light repeatedly accompanies intellectual clarity, record that pattern. If it sometimes accompanies emotional excitement instead, record the exception. A living lexicon grows more precise by preserving contradictions rather than hiding them.

## Personal and inherited meaning

Personal meanings should be compared with historical traditions, but neither automatically submitted to them nor declared superior. Traditional symbols are collective instruments formed through long participation. Personal symbols arise from the particular architecture of an individual life. The strongest interpretations often emerge where personal experience and inherited symbolism illuminate one another without being forced into identity.

The lexicon eventually becomes a map of the practitioner's modes of reception. It shows which lights belong to which thresholds, which images recur during transition, and which apparent illuminations reliably lead toward coherence.
