Psychic excitement can imitate illumination. It accelerates thought, intensifies association, increases confidence, and makes coincidences feel charged with universal importance. Because genuine illumination may also produce energy and joy, the distinction cannot be made by intensity alone.

Excitement tends to demand immediate interpretation and action. It dislikes delay, correction, ordinary duties, and competing explanations. It may encourage the practitioner to announce a revelation before understanding it. Radiance, by contrast, can survive stillness. It does not lose its truth when examined calmly. Even when powerful, it contains an inner order.

## Bodily evidence

The body offers additional evidence. Excitement may involve strained wakefulness, racing thought, impulsivity, restless movement, decreased need for sleep, or inability to disengage. Authentic radiance may energise, but it generally becomes compatible with embodied coherence. It permits eating, sleeping, listening, working, and caring for others.

The most reliable practice is delayed judgment. Record the event, rest, return to ordinary responsibilities, and reconsider it after several days. Light that is real does not need frantic defence. If an insight contains truth, time usually reveals its deeper proportions.
