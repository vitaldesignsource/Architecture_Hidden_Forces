Breath mediates between voluntary and involuntary life. It can be consciously altered, yet it continues without deliberate command. For this reason, it forms a natural bridge among body, etheric rhythm, psychic state, and attention.

In light-and-breath practice, one may sit in gentle natural illumination and observe how inhalation appears to receive, how the pause gathers, how exhalation distributes or releases, and how the empty interval prepares renewed reception. These are contemplative relations rather than claims that physical photons are literally inhaled into the lungs.

Within [[ix-18|the etheric body]], breathing participates in larger rhythms of expansion, contraction, replenishment, circulation, and exchange. Attention may disclose warmth, tingling, currents, pressure, spaciousness, or changes in the perceived boundary of the body. Such sensations should be recorded without forcing them into a predetermined map.

## Comfort as a limit

The breath should remain comfortable. Prolonged retention, hyperventilation, dizziness, tingling caused by overbreathing, or intense respiratory manipulation can create unusual sensations that may be mistaken for spiritual signs. The objective is not to manufacture luminosity through physiological stress. It is to bring breath, attention, and subtle perception into coherent relation.
