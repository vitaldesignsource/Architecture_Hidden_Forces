Colour is light differentiated through relation. It arises through source, spectrum, medium, surface, surrounding colours, and the perceiving organism. In subtle experience, colour can also express tattvic quality, emotional atmosphere, psychic condition, symbolic meaning, or the signature of a particular presence.

Begin with one colour presented in a simple form - a cloth, mineral, flower, painted surface, stained glass, or field of natural light. Observe its hue, saturation, temperature, density, and interaction with its surroundings. Notice how long attention can remain with the colour before associations begin to dominate perception.

## What follows the looking

Then record the feelings, memories, bodily responses, images, and movements of thought that appear. These responses may disclose personal associations, cultural symbolism, or genuine resonance with subtler qualities. They should not immediately be treated as universal correspondences. A colour's meaning depends upon context. Red may signify life, blood, danger, sovereignty, heat, desire, sacrifice, or protection. White may indicate purity, death, totality, emptiness, brilliance, or concealment by excess.

[[ii-9|Tattvic colour contemplation]] proceeds similarly but adds attention to the atmosphere created by the colour. Does it seem to open, move, heat, join, cool, stabilise, or condense? Repeated observation reveals whether a correspondence remains stable or changes according to mood and expectation. Stable patterns deserve further study; unstable ones should remain provisional.
