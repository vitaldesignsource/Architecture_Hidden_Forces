An experience is not adequately judged by how sacred it felt while occurring. Its fruits must be observed across time. Does it produce greater truthfulness, compassion, courage, discrimination, creative capacity, and freedom from compulsion? Or does it produce superiority, obsession, fear, dependency, contempt, and withdrawal from shared reality?

## Five levels of consequence

Consequences should be examined at several levels. Spiritually, did the experience deepen relation to the source rather than fascination with the experience itself? Psychically, did it integrate the personality or intensify fragmentation? Etherically, did it support rhythm and vitality or lead to continuing depletion? Bodily, did it permit rest and grounded activity? Relationally, did it increase the ability to encounter others as living centres rather than characters in a private revelation?

Difficult consequences do not automatically invalidate an encounter. Genuine illumination can expose wounds, dissolve false identities, and initiate a period of disorientation. The question is whether this disruption participates in an intelligible process of integration or becomes an indefinite justification for deterioration.

The most luminous revelation is not necessarily the one that shows the practitioner the highest heavens. It may be the one that makes a previously impossible act of honesty, forgiveness, protection, or transformation possible.
