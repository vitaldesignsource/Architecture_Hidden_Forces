The Luminous Reference Library is the scholarly memory of the entire encyclopaedia. It gathers the languages, texts, historical figures, symbols, diagrams, correspondences, and technical terms through which humanity has approached light. It is not an appendix of miscellaneous information. It is the chamber in which the encyclopaedia preserves its sources, makes its comparisons examinable, and distinguishes inherited teachings from the original doctrines of the Architecture of Hidden Forces.

This division must preserve both correspondence and difference. Greek phōs, Latin lux, Sanskrit jyotis, Arabic nūr, Tibetan ’od gsal, and Chinese guāngmíng may all enter discussions of luminosity, but they are not automatically interchangeable. Each belongs to a particular language, period, textual environment, and metaphysical history. The Library should reveal relations without erasing those distinctions.

Every lexical entry therefore contains the original script, standardised transliteration, approximate pronunciation, grammatical form, basic semantic range, historical setting, important textual occurrences, later interpretations, and its possible — but never automatically presumed — relation to the Architecture of Hidden Forces.
