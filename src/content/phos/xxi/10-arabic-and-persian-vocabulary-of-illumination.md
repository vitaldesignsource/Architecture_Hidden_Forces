*Nūr* is the central Arabic word for light. It can refer to physical illumination, divine guidance, intelligibility, spiritual presence, and the symbolism of the Qur'anic Light Verse. *Ḍiyāʾ* indicates brightness or radiant light and is used in Qur'anic language in relation to the sun. *Sirāj* is a lamp, including the sun as a celestial lamp; its Qur'anic occurrences can be traced word by word through the [Quranic Arabic Corpus](https://corpus.quran.com/).

*Miṣbāḥ* is a lamp, while *mishkāt* is the niche in which the lamp is placed. These terms become architecturally important through the Light Verse, and through the reading given them in [[xv-79|al-Ghazālī's Niche of Lights]].

## The philosophical and Sufi lexicons

*Ishrāq* is illumination, radiance, or sunrise, and gives its name to Suhrawardī's Illuminationist philosophy. *Tajallī* means disclosure, manifestation, or self-revelation, particularly the manifestation of divine attributes or presence. *Kashf* means unveiling, while *basīra* is inner perception or insight.

Persian entries include *rawshan* or *rowshan*, bright or clear; *rawshanāʾī*, light or illumination; *farr*, the Persian continuation of the Iranian concept of glorious potency treated in [[xxi-9|the Avestan and Middle Persian terms]]; *āftāb*, sunlight or sun; and *khurshīd*, the sun.

## Five architectures, one word

The Library distinguishes Qur'anic vocabulary, philosophical terminology, Sufi usage, Persian poetic symbolism, and the technical language of Suhrawardī. Nūr in the Qur'an, al-Ghazālī, Ibn ʿArabī, Rūmī, and Suhrawardī participates in related but not identical architectures. A reader who carries Suhrawardī's hierarchy of victorial lights back into a Qur'anic verse has not deepened the verse; they have replaced it.
