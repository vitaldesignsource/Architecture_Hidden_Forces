光 — *guāng* is light, radiance, brightness, or a ray. 明 — *míng* means bright, clear, illuminated, discerning, or intelligent. Joined as 光明 — *guāngmíng*, the words signify brightness, illumination, or luminous clarity, and become especially important in Chinese Buddhist translation.

照 — *zhào* means to illuminate, shine upon, reflect, or examine. It can join perception and illumination: light reveals an object, while awareness illuminates experience. 暉 — *huī* means radiance or sunlight; 耀 — *yào* means to shine brilliantly or display splendour; and 景 — *jǐng* may refer to brightness, sunlight, appearance, or scene according to context.

## Polarity, not moral opposition

日 — *rì* is the sun or day; 月 — *yuè* is the moon; 火 — *huǒ* is fire; 陽 — *yáng* is the bright, active, sunward aspect of polarity; and 陰 — *yīn* is shaded, concealed, receptive, or moonward. Yin must not be translated simply as evil darkness, nor yang as spiritual goodness. They are relational principles whose meanings arise through polarity and transformation, as [[xv-74|the entry on yin, yang, sun, and moon]] sets out.

## The Daoist vocabulary and its periods

Daoist entries include 天光 — *tiānguāng*, the light of Heaven; 玄 — *xuán*, dark, mysterious, or profound; and the terminology associated with inner illumination, the golden flower, spirit-light, essence, qi, and return.

The section distinguishes early texts such as the Dao De Jing, the Zhuangzi, and the Neiye from the later alchemical and modern interpretive traditions, whose readings are often projected backwards onto them.
