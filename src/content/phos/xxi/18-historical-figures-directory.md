The directory connects thinkers, ritualists, scientists, translators, artists, prophets, mystics, and philosophers to their particular contributions. Entries include dates, locations, traditions, primary works, major luminous concepts, historical influence, and modern controversies.

## Who is gathered

Figures include Akhenaten, Zoroaster, Plato, Aristotle, Euclid, Plotinus, Iamblichus, Proclus, the authors associated with the Hermetic and Gnostic corpora, Pseudo-Dionysius, Ephrem, Ibn al-Haytham, al-Ghazālī, Suhrawardī, Robert Grosseteste, Ficino, Paracelsus, Böhme, Fludd, Newton, Goethe, Éliphas Lévi, Helena Blavatsky, Rudolf Steiner, and Lilly Kolisko.

The list crosses every boundary the modern disciplines maintain. [[xv-89|Newton]] appears for his optics and his alchemy together, because he did not separate them. [[xv-78|Ibn al-Haytham]] appears as an experimentalist working within a metaphysical world, not despite it.

## Attested, attributed, legendary

The directory distinguishes documented biography from traditional attribution. A corpus ascribed to a founder may have been assembled long after them; a name may belong to a school rather than a person; a date may rest on a single late notice.

Legendary figures may be included, but their historical status is clearly stated without assuming that mythic significance depends upon modern biographical verification. Whether a historical Zoroaster can be dated does not determine what the Gāthās teach, and a directory that admitted only the securely datable would exclude much of what the traditions themselves regard as foundational.
