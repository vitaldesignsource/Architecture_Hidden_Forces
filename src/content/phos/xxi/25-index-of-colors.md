The colour index extends beyond basic hue names. Each entry includes shade, saturation, luminosity, opacity, temperature, material, planetary association, elemental relation, ritual use, historical tradition, and visionary quality.

## One name, several meanings

White may signify undifferentiated totality, purity, death, sanctity, dazzling excess, or the conjunction of the spectral colours. Black may indicate absence, concealment, fertile darkness, absorption, *nigredo*, or dark luminosity. Gold may indicate solar incorruptibility, sovereignty, divine intelligence, or consecrated matter.

None of these readings is available without its setting. White as mourning and white as sanctity are the same hue inside two different systems, and a correspondence table that lists only the hue has recorded nothing.

## Against universal formulas

The index resists universal colour formulas. Correspondences may be stable within a ritual system without becoming universally binding across cultures or individuals. A colour assigned to a planet in one grimoire tradition may carry a different assignment in another, and both may be internally coherent.

The practitioner's own repeated observation is admissible evidence here, and is often more reliable for their own work than an inherited table — provided it is recorded as personal and tested by repetition rather than promoted to a law.
