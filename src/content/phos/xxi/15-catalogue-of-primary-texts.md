The Catalogue of Primary Texts is the documentary foundation of the encyclopaedia. Each entry provides title, original language, approximate date, authorship status, manuscript witnesses, available editions, reliable translations, relevant passages, and the divisions in which the text is discussed.

## What a book is

The catalogue distinguishes original texts from later paraphrases, modern occult reconstructions, and popular summaries. The Pyramid Texts, Chaldean Oracles, Zohar, Upanishads, and Corpus Hermeticum should not be represented as single modern books produced under circumstances resembling contemporary authorship. Their textual histories are different and often complex.

A corpus inscribed across several pyramids over generations is not a book. A collection of oracular fragments surviving through quotation in later commentators is not a book. A work of composite authorship assembled over centuries and printed as one volume is a book only in the printer's sense. Each of these requires a different kind of reading, and the catalogue records which kind is called for.

## Indexed by theme

Texts are also indexed by luminous theme: primordial light, solar ascent, clear light, intelligible fire, divine glory, ritual flame, astral light, inner radiance, optics, colour, transformation, and return. A reader following one theme can therefore assemble its witnesses across languages, and see at once how thin or how dense the documentary base for a given claim actually is.
