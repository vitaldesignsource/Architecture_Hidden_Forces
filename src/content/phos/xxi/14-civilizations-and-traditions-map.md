The map displays luminous traditions geographically without implying that ideas remained contained inside fixed modern borders. Trade, migration, conquest, pilgrimage, translation, and initiation carried words and symbols across regions, and a border drawn today explains almost nothing about where a doctrine travelled.

## The layers

Major layers include Egypt and the Nile Valley; Mesopotamia; Iran and Central Asia; the Levant; Greece and Rome; India; Tibet and the Himalayas; China; the Islamic world; medieval Europe; Mesoamerica; the Andes; sub-Saharan African traditions; and the modern global esoteric networks through which older teachings were reinterpreted.

## The routes

Routes are as important as territories: Alexandria, Harran, Gundeshapur, the Silk Roads, the Buddhist translation routes, Syriac-Arabic transmission, the Arabic-Latin translation centres, Renaissance manuscript circulation, colonial movements, and modern print culture.

The map therefore shows that doctrines of light often developed through contact. A Greek text reaches Latin Europe through Syriac and Arabic hands, gaining commentary at each passage. A Sanskrit term enters Tibetan through a translation committee working to a fixed lexicon. Neither arrives unchanged, and the changes are frequently where the interesting doctrine is.
