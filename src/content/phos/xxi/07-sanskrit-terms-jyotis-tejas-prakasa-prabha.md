*Jyotis* is light, brightness, flame, celestial light, or a heavenly luminary. It can refer to the sun, fire, stars, and the inward light of consciousness. In Vedic and Upanishadic settings its meaning ranges from physical brilliance to that light by which experience itself becomes possible — the concern of [[xv-58|the Upanishadic light of the Self]].

*Tejas* signifies brilliance, fiery energy, sharpness, splendour, heat, vigour, and luminous potency. In elemental systems it may be connected with fire, transformation, vision, colour, and digestion. Tejas should not be flattened into either light or fire; it expresses an active radiance capable of forming, heating, distinguishing, and transforming, as [[viii-12|the entry on tejas and fiery radiance]] describes.

## Manifestation and lustre

*Prakāśa* means light, manifestation, illumination, clarity, or the activity of making evident. In several Indian philosophical systems, especially later nondual traditions, it becomes an important term for the self-revealing luminosity of consciousness.

*Prabhā* is radiance, lustre, brightness, or emitted splendour. Related terms include *dīpti*, brilliance or flaming splendour; *bhās*, shining or appearing; *raśmi*, ray; *arcis*, flame or beam; *agni*, fire and the god Fire; *sūrya*, the sun; and *uṣas*, dawn.

## Reading a term inside its own tradition

The Sanskrit directory distinguishes Vedic, Epic, Upanishadic, Buddhist, Sāṃkhya, Yoga, Vedānta, Śaiva, Śākta, and tantric usages. The [Monier-Williams Sanskrit-English Dictionary in the Cologne Digital Sanskrit Lexicon](https://www.sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/MWScan/2014/web/index.php) provides a lexical starting point, but meanings should ultimately be verified within the particular text and tradition being discussed. A dictionary gathers what a word has meant somewhere; it does not settle what it means here.
