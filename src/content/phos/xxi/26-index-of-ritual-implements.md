This index gathers lamps, candles, wicks, oils, incense, mirrors, crystals, bowls of water, metals, pigments, statues, icons, veils, crowns, vestments, ritual circles, temple windows, solar apertures, and instruments of sacred sound.

## The material relation is the teaching

Every implement is studied materially and metaphysically. A lamp requires fuel, vessel, wick, flame, oxygen, attention, and placement. These physical relations make it a model of luminous mediation: nothing in the chain can be removed and nothing in it is symbolic only.

A mirror reflects but reverses. A crystal receives, refracts, concentrates, and preserves form. Incense makes otherwise invisible currents of air perceptible. In each case the metaphysical reading is drawn out of the physics rather than laid over it, which is why the physics has to be got right first.

## Cautions carried with the meanings

Entries contain practical cautions as well as symbolic meanings. Open flame, hot oil, enclosed smoke, toxic pigments, heavy metals, and unattended lamps carry real hazards that no consecration suspends.

The consecration of form must remain accountable to the actual properties and risks of the material being used — the principle [[xx-18|consecrating form without imprisoning force]] states for practice. A rite that ignores the material has not transcended it.
