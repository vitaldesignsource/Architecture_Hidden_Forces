Syriac preserves one of the richest Christian languages of luminous symbolism. *Nūhrā* means light and becomes central to Syriac accounts of creation, baptism, resurrection, divine revelation, and the robe of glory. *Zīwā* suggests splendour, radiance, brightness, or luminous glory, and is also important in Mandaean vocabulary. *Šubḥā*, glory or praise, can acquire luminous associations without being identical to light. *Nūrā* names fire, preserving the distinction between illuminating radiance and burning power.

## The robe and the garment

The Syriac library gives special attention to the imagery of the robe of light, the garment of glory, baptismal illumination, paradisal luminosity, and the luminous body. Ephrem the Syrian, the Odes of Solomon, the Acts of Thomas, and the Syriac liturgical traditions are linked directly to the relevant lexical entries, and to [[xv-56|the entry on Syriac Christianity and the robe of light]]. The [Simtho Syriac thesaurus and the SEDRA lexicon](https://bethmardutho.org/simtho/) allow a word to be followed through a corpus of more than seven million tokens rather than through a single quotation.

## Coptic, by dialect

In Coptic, ⲟⲩⲟⲉⲓⲛ — *ouoein* is a principal word for light, familiar from Coptic biblical, monastic, and Gnostic texts. ⲉⲟⲟⲩ — *eoou* belongs to the field of glory, while ⲕⲱϩⲧ — *kōht* denotes fire. Because Coptic contains several dialects — Sahidic, Bohairic, Akhmimic, Lycopolitan, and others — spellings and forms are indexed by dialect rather than silently standardised. The [Coptic Dictionary Online](https://coptic-dictionary.org/) records both the native Egyptian vocabulary and the large body of Greek loanwords.

That last distinction carries weight. This section shows how Greek philosophical and Christian vocabulary entered Coptic literature, especially in works translated from Greek. A Greek loanword in a Coptic text may carry a different history from an inherited Egyptian term even when both are translated into English as light.
