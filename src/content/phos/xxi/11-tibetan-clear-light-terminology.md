འོད་གསལ་ — *’od gsal* is conventionally translated as clear light, luminosity, or luminous clarity. Literally, its two elements concern light or radiance and clarity or lucidity. It should not be interpreted as ordinary visible light, although visionary luminosity may accompany experiences described through the term. The [84000 glossary and translation corpus](https://84000.co/translation/toh147/UT22084-057-006-glossary) shows *’od gsal* used to translate Sanskrit terms such as *ābhāsvara* in canonical contexts.

*’Od* means light or radiance. *Gsal ba* means clear, lucid, evident, or luminous. *’Od zer* is a ray of light. *Gsal snang* can refer to clear appearance, and *rang bzhin ’od gsal* to naturally luminous or clear-light nature, depending on context.

## Where the term is being used

The Library distinguishes uses in sūtra, tantra, Dzogchen, Mahāmudrā, dream yoga, completion-stage practice, death teachings, and philosophical commentary. It also distinguishes *’od gsal* from *rig pa*, pristine awareness or knowing, despite their close relationship in some systems. A translation that renders both as luminosity can conceal important differences, and a reader who meets only the English will not know that two words stood behind it.

## What clear light names

Clear light is not merely brightness within meditation. It concerns the intrinsic clarity through which appearances and knowing become possible, sometimes disclosed when grosser mental constructions subside. The system may place this in correspondence with aphanic or noetic light, but should not claim identity without careful comparison — the caution [[xv-69|the entry on tantric and Tibetan clear light]] carries in full.
