This section gathers short, carefully selected passages that serve as primary witnesses to humanity's luminous thought. Every passage includes the original language where legally and practically possible, a reliable translation, textual location, historical note, and commentary on essential terms.

## Naming the hand

When existing translations are used, translators and editions are identified. When the encyclopaedia supplies its own translation, it is labelled as such. Difficult terms may be left partially untranslated when an English equivalent would erase their structure — *kavod*, *xᵛarənah*, *tejas*, *’od gsal*, and *akh* are all better carried into English unresolved than flattened into glory, radiance, energy, luminosity, and spirit.

## Long enough to hold its context

Passages are short enough to encourage comparison but substantial enough to preserve context. A phrase such as *light of lights* can be misleading when detached from the argument, hymn, ritual, or cosmology in which it appears. In [[xv-60|the Bhagavad Gītā]] it stands within a theophany; in [[xv-80|Suhrawardī]] it names the apex of a graded ontology. The words coincide; the architectures do not.

The section therefore serves the comparison it makes possible and also disciplines it. A reader who can see the surrounding sentences is a reader able to notice when a resemblance was produced by the excerpt rather than found in the text.
