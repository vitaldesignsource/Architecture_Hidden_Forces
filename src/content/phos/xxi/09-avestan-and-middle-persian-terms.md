The Iranian section centres upon the Avestan semantic fields of light, fire, glory, truth, and the luminous realms. *Raocah-* belongs to the vocabulary of light and brightness; its forms appear in expressions involving the celestial lights and the realm of endless light. *Ātar* is fire, both as a physical reality and as a sacred presence within Zoroastrian life — the subject of [[xv-14|the entry on Avestan fire]].

## Glory as a power, not a brightness

*Xᵛarənah*, later Middle Persian *xwarrah* or *khwarrah*, is glory, fortune, royal or divine splendour, and the luminous potency associated with rightful sovereignty and spiritual distinction. It cannot simply be translated as optical radiance. It is a power of legitimacy, excellence, and participation in a higher order. [Encyclopaedia Iranica's article on farr(ah)](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/farrah/) traces both the traditional reading, which took glory, splendour, and luminosity as primary, and the etymological connection to the Iranian word for sun.

## Middle Persian and the cosmological texts

Middle Persian entries include *rōšn* or *rōšnīh*, bright, light, or brightness; *ādur*, fire; and *asar rōšnīh*, Endless Light, in cosmological contexts. The vocabulary is linked to the Gāthās, the Younger Avesta, the Yasna, the Mihr Yašt, the Bundahišn, the Dēnkard, and the later Zoroastrian cosmological writings.

These entries show how light and darkness operate cosmologically and ethically without reducing Zoroastrian teaching to a crude equality between two ultimate gods. The asymmetry matters: the tradition's own texts do not present the two principles as equal powers, and a summary that makes them so has already stopped describing the tradition.
