In Pāli, *āloka* commonly means light, illumination, or brightness. It appears in discussions of perception, meditation, and the cultivation of the perception of light. *Obhāsa* means radiance or illumination and can refer to meditative light. *Pabhā* indicates radiance, while *pabhassara* means luminous, radiant, or brightly shining. The well-known language of the luminous mind must be treated in its textual context rather than transformed immediately into a universal doctrine of consciousness, as [[xv-64|the entry on the early Buddhist luminous mind]] shows.

*Tejo* can indicate the fire element, heat, combustion, warmth, or the fiery property of matter. *Rasmi* signifies a ray. *Ābhā* indicates light or splendour. In meditation manuals, terms for the luminous sign must be connected with *nimitta*, the sign or mental appearance that may arise as concentration develops — the subject of [[xi-4|the entry on the Buddhist nimitta]]. The [Pali Text Society's Pali-English Dictionary](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/dictionaries/pali/) remains the standard first reference, though it is a century old and its glosses often need checking against the commentarial literature.

## Buddhist Hybrid Sanskrit

Buddhist Hybrid Sanskrit introduces or adapts terms such as *prabhāsvara*, luminous or clear-radiant; *ābhāsvara*, radiantly shining; *avabhāsa*, appearance or illumination; and *āloka*, light. Their meanings vary across Nikāya, Abhidharma, Mahāyāna, Yogācāra, tathāgatagarbha, and tantric settings, and a translation adequate in one of these can mislead badly in another.

## What the language does not assert

The Library resists treating every Buddhist use of luminosity as a statement about an eternal metaphysical self. Buddhist traditions frequently use luminous language while maintaining distinctive teachings concerning impermanence, emptiness, dependent arising, and non-self. The encyclopaedia may place such language in correspondence with its own doctrines, but the correspondence is a proposal to be examined, not an identity to be assumed.
