The principal Hebrew word is אוֹר — *’or*, light, illumination, or daylight. Its verbal root can express becoming light or giving light. In Genesis, light appears before the creation of the celestial luminaries, allowing later traditions to distinguish primordial light from the sun, moon, and stars — the reading traced in [[xv-42|Genesis and light before the luminaries]].

נֹגַהּ — *nogah* indicates brightness, radiance, or shining splendour. It appears in descriptions of celestial and visionary luminosity. זֹהַר — *zohar* means radiance or brilliance and later becomes inseparable from the title and symbolism of the major Kabbalistic work.

## Glory, and why it is not simply light

כָּבוֹד — *kavod* fundamentally concerns weight, importance, honour, or glory, but in visions of divine manifestation it can acquire strongly luminous qualities. It should not be reduced to light, although divine radiance may be one mode of its appearance. The distinction matters wherever an English translation renders several unrelated originals by the same word.

Other essential Hebrew terms include אֵשׁ — *’esh*, fire; נֵר — *ner*, lamp; לַפִּיד — *lappid*, torch or flame; בָּרָק — *baraq*, lightning; and חֹשֶׁךְ — *ḥoshekh*, darkness.

## Aramaic, by dialect

Aramaic vocabulary must be divided by dialect. A common word for light is נְהוֹרָא — *nəhōrā*, but Biblical, Jewish Palestinian, Jewish Babylonian, Syriac, Mandaic, and other forms must not be treated as a single timeless language. The [Comprehensive Aramaic Lexicon](https://cal.huc.edu/) is valuable precisely because it organises evidence from multiple Aramaic dialects across a corpus of millions of lexically analysed words, and records the date at which a reading was found.
