The Atlas gathers the point, ray, circle, sphere, triangle, flame, lamp, crown, halo, mandorla, star, solar disc, winged disc, solar barque, eye, mirror, crystal, rainbow, lotus, menorah, cross of light, radiant heart, black sun, cave, and temple.

Every entry includes visual examples, geographical range, historical development, ritual use, traditional interpretation, and meaning within the Architecture of Hidden Forces. Similar-looking symbols are not automatically treated as historically related. A rayed disc in two unconnected cultures is evidence of two solar observations before it is evidence of contact, and the Atlas records which of the two a given case actually supports.

## Symbols as condensed architectures

The atlas shows how symbols act as condensed architectures. A halo establishes a relation between body and sanctified field. A mirror joins reception and reflection. A prism expresses differentiation. A cave places concealed light inside generative darkness.

A symbol is not merely something that represents an idea; it can organise perception and mediate force. This is why the Atlas belongs in a reference library rather than in an appendix of illustrations: the drawing is the argument's evidence, not its decoration.

The thirty-two symbols carried by the entries of the encyclopaedia are also gathered in the [Symbol Atlas](/phos/tools/symbols), where each glyph lists the entries that bear it.
