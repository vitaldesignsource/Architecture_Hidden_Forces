The Egyptian section is organised chronologically, because Egyptian vocabulary developed across Old, Middle, Late, Demotic, and Coptic stages. A spelling or meaning attested in one period should not be projected uncritically across three millennia. The [Thesaurus Linguae Aegyptiae](https://thesaurus-linguae-aegyptiae.de/info/text-corpus) is particularly important here, because its lexicon is grounded in a large corpus of hieroglyphic, hieratic, and Demotic texts extending from approximately 3000 BCE into the early centuries CE.

## The solar field

Central entries include words associated with the sun, shining, rays, dawn, brilliance, fire, and transfigured effectiveness. Rꜥ, Re or Ra, names both the sun and the solar deity, but divine, celestial, visible, and ritual meanings must be separated by context. Itn, Aten, refers to the solar disc and, especially in the Amarna period, to the divine power whose rays communicate life — the theology of [[xv-4|the Great Hymn to Aten]]. Wbn carries meanings associated with rising, shining, or appearing luminously; psḏ belongs to the semantic field of shining or gleaming; and stwt may designate rays.

## Akh, and why it is not simply light

Ꜣḫ, conventionally written *akh*, requires special precision. It should not be translated simply as light. It belongs to a field of effectiveness, radiance, blessedness, and the transfigured condition of the dead. An akh is not merely a glowing apparition but an effective transfigured being capable of participating in divine and stellar orders, as [[xv-10|the entry on akh, radiance, and the transfigured dead]] sets out at length.

## The wider vocabulary

Other entries include šw, Shu, whose associations with air, space, and light make him relevant to cosmic separation; ꜣḫt, *akhet*, the horizon where the sun appears and disappears; the solar eyes; the benben; the scarab Khepri; the djed; the solar barque; the circumpolar stars; and the luminous transformations described in the Pyramid Texts, Coffin Texts, Amduat, Litany of Re, and Book of Gates.
