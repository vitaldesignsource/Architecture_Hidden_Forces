*Lux* generally indicates light considered as a source, presence, or condition of illumination. It may name daylight, heavenly light, or the light by which things become visible. In Christian and medieval metaphysics, *lux* could be used for originating light or light in its more fontal condition.

*Lumen* commonly refers to emitted or received light, a lamp, an illuminating power, or that through which vision becomes possible. Later writers sometimes distinguish *lux* as light at its source from *lumen* as light proceeding, diffused, or received. That distinction is philosophically valuable, but the Library does not present it as a rigid rule governing all Latin texts.

*Radius* is a ray, beam, spoke, or straight line extending from a centre. Its geometric and optical meanings make it a crucial term for procession, directionality, transmission, and the relation between centre and circumference — the concern of [[xvii-2|the symbol of ray and line]].

## Clarity, splendour, and fire

*Claritas* means clarity, brightness, brilliance, or illustriousness. In medieval aesthetics and theology it acquires connections with intelligible radiance, beauty, and the manifestation of form. *Claritas* is not merely strong brightness; it is the disclosure of an inner order through appearance.

*Splendor* signifies brilliance, lustre, magnificence, or reflected radiance. It may describe something that shines through its participation in another source. The word is therefore important to the distinction between fontal and participatory light held by [[i-12|the entry on the source that remains while its light proceeds]].

*Ignis* is fire: material flame, celestial fire, sacrificial fire, passionate force, or spiritual ardour. Related terms include *fulgor*, a sudden gleam or flash; *fulmen*, lightning or thunderbolt; *candor*, shining whiteness; *gloria*, radiance and exalted presence; and *illuminatio*, the act of making luminous.
