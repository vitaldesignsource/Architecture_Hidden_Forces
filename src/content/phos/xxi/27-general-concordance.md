The General Concordance is the final connective instrument of the encyclopaedia. It records every significant term, variant spelling, transliteration, divine name, text, person, symbol, place, operation, and doctrine, directing the reader to every relevant occurrence.

## Spellings and the words behind a word

It recognises alternative spellings such as *phōs* and *phos*, Re and Ra, Suhrawardī and Sohrevardi, *xwarrah* and *khwarrah*, Dao and Tao. It cross-references translations without pretending that one English word completely contains its originals. A search for *glory* leads to *kavod*, *doxa*, *gloria*, *šubḥā*, and *xᵛarənah*, and then explains their differences rather than presenting them as a set of synonyms.

## The status of a teaching

The concordance also identifies the status of a teaching.

- **Historical** — explicitly attested in a source.
- **Traditional** — received through a defined lineage or interpretive community.
- **Comparative** — proposed through structural or symbolic resemblance.
- **Experiential** — grounded in recorded phenomenological observation.
- **Scientific** — supported within an empirical field of inquiry.
- **Architectural** — native to the Architecture of Hidden Forces.
- **Speculative** — plausible but not yet securely determined.

Through this classification the Library becomes more than a storage chamber. It becomes the encyclopaedia's faculty of recollection and self-correction, allowing ancient witnesses, living traditions, scientific observations, visionary experiences, and the original architecture to enter relation without being confused.
