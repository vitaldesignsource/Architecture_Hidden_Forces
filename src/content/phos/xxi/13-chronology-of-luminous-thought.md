The chronology displays the history of light across several simultaneous tracks: scientific optics, cosmology, theology, philosophy, ritual, mysticism, art, and subtle-body teaching. This prevents the misleading impression that humanity moved through a single sequence from myth to reason.

## The tracks

The timeline begins with prehistoric solar alignments and early fire ritual, then moves through Egyptian solar theology, Mesopotamian astral religion, Vedic fire and solar hymns, Iranian light cosmology, Greek theories of vision, Israelite creation and glory traditions, Buddhist luminosity teachings, Hellenistic Hermetism, Gnosticism, Neoplatonism, Christian illumination, Islamic optics and Illuminationism, medieval lux metaphysics, Renaissance solar philosophy, alchemy, modern physics, Goethean colour theory, occult astral light, the four ethers, biophoton research, and the Architecture of Hidden Forces.

Read across rather than down, the tracks show what a single-line history hides. Ibn al-Haytham's optics and al-Ghazālī's niche belong to the same century. Newton's spectrum and his alchemy belong to the same man.

## Two dates, not one

Each entry identifies both the date of a text or figure and the date of the surviving manuscript when those are different. A cosmology composed in the sixth century BCE and preserved in a ninth-century CE copy has two histories, and an argument that depends on the earlier one must say so.

*Ancient* must never be allowed to become a vague category in which writings separated by a thousand years are placed side by side without explanation. Most false continuities in the comparative study of light are built out of that single imprecision.
