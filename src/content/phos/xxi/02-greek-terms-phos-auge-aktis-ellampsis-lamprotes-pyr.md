Φῶς — *phōs* is the broad Greek word for light. It may indicate daylight, visible illumination, the source of visibility, or metaphorical and spiritual illumination. In philosophical and religious texts it can become a name for intelligibility, divine presence, truth, life, and the illuminated condition of the soul.

Αὐγή — *augē* suggests radiance, brightness, gleaming light, or the light of dawn. It often carries a sense of light becoming perceptible: radiance at the threshold between hiddenness and appearance. It is therefore especially useful for the encyclopaedia's treatment of first disclosure and the gradual emergence of manifestation.

Ἀκτίς — *aktis* means a ray or beam, especially a solar ray. It expresses directed procession, light extending from a source toward a receiver. The ray preserves an intelligible relation between unity and multiplicity, because numerous rays can proceed from one sun without requiring the sun to divide into separate sources.

## The act, the quality, and the fire

Ἔλλαμψις — *ellampsis* means illumination, shining upon, or an irradiating presence. In later philosophical and theological usage it can designate the reception of higher illumination by the intellect or soul. It should be distinguished from the source itself: *ellampsis* concerns the act or event of illumination.

Λαμπρότης — *lamprotēs* signifies brightness, brilliance, splendour, distinction, and sometimes magnificence. It refers not only to the existence of light but to the quality through which something appears luminously eminent.

Πῦρ — *pyr* is fire. The Greek library must preserve the difference between fire and light while showing their ancient intimacy. Fire heats, consumes, transforms, and generates light; light illuminates and reveals but need not burn. Their convergence produces the encyclopaedia's doctrine of fiery intelligence; their distinction prevents all luminosity from being interpreted as combustion, as [[viii-2|the entry on why fire and light are related but not identical]] sets out.

## The wider Greek field

Further entries include *phaos*, an older poetic form of light; *phainein*, to bring to appearance; *phainomenon*, that which appears; *doxa*, which can mean opinion but also glory or splendour in biblical Greek; *apaugasma*, radiance proceeding from a source; and *photismos*, illumination or enlightenment. The [Liddell–Scott–Jones lexicon in the Perseus Digital Library](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.04.0057) gives the attested range for each, which should be consulted before a philosophical weight is placed on any of them.
