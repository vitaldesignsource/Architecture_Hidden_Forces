Apophatic Darkness is encountered where concepts, images, names, and distinctions can no longer contain the reality toward which they point.

It is not ignorance in the ordinary sense. Ignorance lacks knowledge that might otherwise be acquired. Apophatic unknowing recognises that the object exceeds the structure of finite knowing itself.

At this boundary, even *light* becomes an inadequate name. The source cannot be made into one more luminous object standing before the mind. Its excess may therefore be experienced as silence, darkness, absence, or incomprehensibility.

Apophatic Darkness disciplines the desire to possess mystery through language. It does not forbid knowledge; it prevents partial knowledge from pretending to be total.
