Darkness is not one condition. It may indicate the absence of sensible light, the concealment of an existing form, the latency of something not yet manifested, the resistance of a medium, the shadow produced by interruption, or the failure of a receiver to perceive what is present. It may also name the apophatic obscurity encountered when reality exceeds the limits of knowledge.

These conditions must not be collapsed into a single symbol. Primordial darkness is not the same as evil. Generative darkness is not the same as ignorance. Shadow is not always deception, and illumination is not always goodness. A complete philosophy of light must understand the darkness through which light becomes visible, protected, differentiated, and capable of transformation.
