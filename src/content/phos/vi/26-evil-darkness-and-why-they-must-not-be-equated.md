Evil cannot be defined adequately as darkness. Optical darkness harms no one by itself. Generative darkness shelters life. Apophatic darkness protects mystery. Night permits rest, repair, contemplation, and the perception of stars.

Within this system, evil is better approached as a radical deformation of right relation: the deliberate reduction of beings into instruments, the wilful destruction of their proper development, or the organised use of force without regard for truth, proportion, or participation.

## Where evil actually hides

Evil may hide in darkness, but it can also surround itself with brilliance, purity, beauty, certainty, and claims of divine authority.

Equating darkness with evil prevents genuine discernment. It condemns the hidden while leaving luminous deception unrecognised.
