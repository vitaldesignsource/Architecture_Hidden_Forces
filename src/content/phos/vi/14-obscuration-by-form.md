Form reveals by giving force boundary, but the same boundary can also conceal. Every form makes certain aspects present while hiding others.

A symbol reveals an intelligible pattern but cannot contain the whole principle. A body reveals a person while concealing much of the person's interior life. A doctrine makes relations articulate while potentially obscuring whatever falls outside its categories.

Obscuration by form is therefore unavoidable. Manifestation always involves selection.

The danger arises when a form mistakes itself for the totality of what it mediates. Then the vessel claims the authority of the source, the image replaces the reality, and the doctrine becomes incapable of further revelation.

A truthful form makes its limits visible.
