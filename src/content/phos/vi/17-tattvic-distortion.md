The tattvas are morphogenic biases, not moral forces. Distortion arises when a tattvic tendency becomes excessive, isolated, or disconnected from the balancing architecture.

Ākāśa may distort into dissociation, vacancy, or indefinite abstraction. Vāyu may become fragmentation, restlessness, and uncontrolled dispersal. Tejas may become inflammation, aggression, exposure, or destructive intensity. Apas may become enmeshment, stagnation, and loss of boundary. Pṛthivī may become rigidity, inertia, and impermeability.

The problem is not the tattva itself but disproportion.

Tattvic distortion colours light so strongly that one mode of formation appears to be the whole field. Restoration requires renewed relation among openness, movement, transformation, cohesion, and stability.
