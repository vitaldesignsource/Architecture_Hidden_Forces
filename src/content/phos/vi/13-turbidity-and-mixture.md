Turbidity occurs when suspended particles, impurities, or internal irregularities scatter light and reduce transparency.

Turbidity does not eliminate light. It redistributes it. A beam passing through mist becomes visible precisely because the suspended medium scatters portions of the light toward the observer.

Psychically, turbidity arises when emotion, memory, expectation, fear, and desire intermingle so densely that the originating current cannot be clearly distinguished. The field may appear highly luminous while remaining difficult to interpret.

Mixture is not inherently corrupt. Living systems depend upon mixtures. Turbidity becomes a problem when the components cannot be differentiated or when the receiver mistakes a composite appearance for a pure transmission.
