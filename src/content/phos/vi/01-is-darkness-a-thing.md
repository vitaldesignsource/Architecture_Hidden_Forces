The first question is whether darkness possesses an existence of its own or is merely the absence of light.

At the physical level, darkness generally describes a condition in which insufficient visible light reaches a receiver. It is not ordinarily a substance radiating darkness particles into space. Yet darkness remains experientially real. It alters perception, behaviour, orientation, emotion, and the kinds of relationships possible within an environment.

Metaphysically, several different realities can appear as darkness: unmanifest potential, material opacity, unconscious content, deliberate concealment, deprivation, mystery, or an excess exceeding perception.

Darkness may therefore lack independent physical substance while remaining a genuine relational condition. It is what appears when light is absent, interrupted, absorbed, concealed, or beyond the capacity of the receiver.
