Sensible darkness occurs when the human visual system receives too little visible light to distinguish forms adequately.

This darkness is relative to the receiver. Many organisms can detect wavelengths, intensities, or contrasts unavailable to human sight. A space appearing completely dark to one creature may remain perceptually structured to another.

Darkness is therefore not always an absolute property of the environment. It can be a relationship among available radiation, atmospheric conditions, sensory organs, nervous systems, and thresholds of perception.

## The caution it teaches

This teaches an important metaphysical caution: what is invisible to a receiver cannot automatically be declared nonexistent. Yet invisibility also does not prove that a hidden entity or force is present. Darkness establishes uncertainty, not confirmation.
