A spiritual technology may be effective, ancient, visionary, or powerful and still be unsuitable for a particular person, time, or purpose. Some paths demand commitments incompatible with other obligations. Some awaken forces faster than they can be integrated. Some lead toward genuine attainments that nevertheless do not serve the deepest vocation of the individual.

## Examining the path

Before walking a path, one should examine its sources, methods, mediators, ethical expectations, effects upon practitioners, treatment of dissent, relationship to power, and capacity to acknowledge harm. One should also examine one's own motive. Is the path sought for truth, healing, service, fascination, escape, status, revenge, certainty, or the desire to become exceptional?

## Judged by its people

A path should also be judged by the people it produces. Are experienced practitioners becoming freer, wiser, more truthful, and more capable of relationship? Or are they becoming dependent, secretive, grandiose, fearful, and unable to tolerate questioning? This is the test [[xx-12|ethical discernment]] applies to a single experience, extended to a whole tradition.

## Refusal, delay, and leaving

A valid path need not be walked merely because it is valid. Refusal can be an act of wisdom. Delay can be a form of preparation. Leaving a path can be obedience to a deeper light rather than betrayal.

The correct path is not simply the one that opens hidden doors. It is the one whose demands correspond to the person's lawful development and whose destination remains worthy when clearly seen.
