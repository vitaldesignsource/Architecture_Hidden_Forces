Contact may occur before comprehension, but stable participation requires an answering organ. Intellectual light requires disciplined understanding. Moral light requires the ability to act beyond immediate impulse. Imaginal light requires a trained faculty of symbolic perception. Etheric light requires sensitivity to vitality, rhythm, and formative movement. Theurgic light requires ritual exactitude, reverence, protection, ethical preparation, and knowledge of mediation.

## Brilliance and capacity are not the same

Capacity is not merely technical expertise. A person may possess remarkable visionary ability while lacking the moral structure needed to interpret or steward what is perceived. Another may experience little dramatic imagery while possessing a profound capacity for spiritual truth through constancy, compassion, judgment, and service. Brilliance of experience and depth of capacity are not identical — the distinction [[v-28|the capacity of the receiver]] draws, and [[xx-14|avoiding inflation and claims of spiritual rank]] protects.

## How capacity grows

Capacity grows through practice, study, self-observation, ethical refinement, suffering consciously integrated, service, right relationship, and repeated acts of truthful embodiment. It also develops through periods of rest and apparent withdrawal, during which previously received force is assimilated.

## A responsibility, not a caste

The law does not establish a caste of permanently superior beings. It describes a developmental responsibility: one must become capable of carrying what one asks to receive.
