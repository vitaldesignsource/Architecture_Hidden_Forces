A prism differentiates white light into spectral colours. In subtle reality, tattvic conditions differentiate force into characteristic tendencies of space, movement, radiance, cohesion, and solidity. Ākāśa opens luminous spaciousness; vāyu sets light into motion; tejas sharpens, heats, and intensifies; apas reflects, joins, and carries; pṛthivī fixes luminous activity within enduring form.

## One impulse, five expressions

Tattvic refraction explains why a single spiritual impulse can assume very different expressions. Passing predominantly through tejas, it may appear as revelation, courage, purification, severity, conflict, or consuming zeal. Through apas it may become devotion, empathy, dream, memory, reflection, or emotional union. Through vāyu it may become inspiration, communication, movement, restlessness, or dispersion. Through pṛthivī it may become institution, craft, body, monument, doctrine, or boundary. Through ākāśa it may remain spacious, silent, archetypal, resonant, or unbounded.

## Proportion, not allegiance

No tattva is inherently pure or corrupt. Distortion arises when one current becomes isolated, exaggerated, stagnant, or improperly related to the whole — the condition [[vi-17|tattvic distortion]] examines. Fire without water becomes merciless. Water without earth loses containment. Earth without air becomes rigid. Air without a centre disperses. Space without formation remains unrealised possibility.

Luminous architecture therefore depends upon elemental proportion rather than allegiance to one supposedly superior current.
