Consecration is not the arbitrary assignment of holiness to an object. It is the establishment of correspondence among intention, material, symbol, place, time, action, speech, and invoked presence. Through such alignment, an ordinary vessel becomes capable of holding and transmitting a particular order of light — [[xiv-24|consecration as the establishment of right relation]] gives the ritual form of this.

## Temple, word, life

A consecrated temple is more than decorated space. Orientation, proportion, threshold, sound, icon, movement, offering, colour, and communal attention cooperate to shape its Morphaithēr. A consecrated word is more than a sequence of sounds. Meaning, breath, intention, lineage, rhythm, and spiritual address are united within it. A consecrated human life is one in which knowledge, desire, action, and service become increasingly aligned with a central truth.

## Matter spiritualised, not discarded

Consecration does not abolish the material nature of a thing. It brings matter into conscious participation with spirit. Matter is not discarded but spiritualised: made capable of expressing more faithfully the force that inhabits it, as [[ii-26|light and the spiritualisation of matter]] sets out.

## Maintained by use

Consecration cannot be secured permanently by a past ceremony. It must be maintained through truthful use. A sacred office can be desecrated by corruption; a ritual object can become empty through neglect; a temple can preserve power while losing wisdom. Form remains consecrated only while its living relations continue to serve the purpose for which it was set apart.
