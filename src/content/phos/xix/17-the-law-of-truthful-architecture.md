Truthful architecture does not require perfection. It requires the capacity to reveal its own condition, acknowledge failure, receive correction, and reorganise itself around what is true. A person, doctrine, ritual system, temple, or institution may contain contradictions without becoming wholly false. Falsity becomes structural when contradiction must be concealed to preserve authority.

## Known by the movement of force

A luminous architecture is known by the movement of force within it. Does light circulate, or is it hoarded? Does authority generate capacity in others, or create dependency? Are shadow and failure brought into consciousness, or projected onto enemies? Can the structure distinguish revelation from fantasy, mystery from vagueness, and hierarchy from domination?

## Inner and outer, aligned without collapse

Truthful architecture aligns inner and outer orders without collapsing them. Spirit remains prior to form, but form is treated as consequential. Symbol is not reduced to measurement, yet factual reality is not discarded whenever it becomes inconvenient. The invisible does not excuse visible harm. The visible does not exhaust the invisible — the double guard [[i-16|light and truth]] establishes among the first principles.

Truth resides in the living correspondence between levels. When the architecture's visible actions contradict its hidden purpose, the contradiction must be repaired rather than concealed beneath greater displays of luminosity.
